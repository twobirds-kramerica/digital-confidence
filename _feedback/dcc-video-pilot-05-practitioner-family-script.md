# DCC Video — PILOT 05 — "For the person you care about" (Practitioner / Parent / Family intro, audience variant A2)

**Introductory / onboarding tier, ~90 seconds.** The Practitioner/Parent/Family cut of the welcome video per `dcc-onboarding-and-measurement-2026-07-17.md` (audience variant A2): different framing from the Adults intro (pilot 02). This one speaks to the busy son, daughter, grandchild, PSW, or librarian, and its core message is: **this is for the person you care about; here's how to use it with them, without taking over.** Characters: **David** (the sandwich-generation son, the anchor for this audience per `dcc-character-cast-architecture.md` §2.3) with **Margaret** present as the guide the learner will meet. Tone: warm, direct, respectful of the caregiver's time. Bilingual EN + fr-CA below (FR is DRAFT, human review gated). Governance: written against `dcc-brand-guidelines.md` + the cast architecture + the art briefs, all read in full this run (Fable creative sprint, 2026-07-18).

**Casting note (why David speaks and Margaret doesn't teach here):** cast rule §2 says Margaret teaches learners; the cast lives the scenarios. This video's learner is the CAREGIVER, and the most credible voice for "how to help without taking over" is someone who had to learn it. David narrates as a peer, and the video's one staged beat shows him practising what he preaches beside Margaret. Margaret is seen, warm and in charge of her own phone, but the guidance to caregivers is David's. This keeps Margaret's teaching bond exclusively with the senior learner, which is the point of the whole cast design.

**Staleness rule (binding):** no live website UI shown. The experience is described in plain words over the etched characters and simple generic motifs.

## Narration — English (en-CA)

> If you're helping someone with technology, a parent, a neighbour, someone you work with, this is for you. And because your time is short, so is this: about ninety seconds.
>
> The Digital Confidence Centre is a set of short, calm lessons built for adults who want to feel more comfortable with everyday technology. Staying safe, staying connected, everyday skills. Everything is self-paced. Nothing is timed, nothing is graded, and nothing they do here can break anything.
>
> Here's what I'd want you to know, from someone who's been the helper. The hardest part isn't the technology. It's keeping your hands off it.
>
> When it's quicker to just do it for them, they lose the chance to learn it. So the one rule is: they drive, you watch. Sit beside them, not over their shoulder. Let them tap. Let them get it wrong and find their own way back. It's slower. It sticks.
>
> The lessons do the teaching, so you don't have to be the expert, or the patient one, at the end of a long day. You just have to be there, at the start.
>
> So here's the whole ask: start with one lesson together. Then step back, and let them surprise you.

## Narration — Canadian French (fr-CA) — DRAFT, requires human FR review before use

> Si vous aidez quelqu'un avec la technologie, un parent, une voisine, une personne que vous accompagnez, ceci est pour vous. Et comme votre temps est précieux, ce sera bref : environ quatre-vingt-dix secondes.
>
> Le Centre de confiance numérique, c'est un ensemble de courtes leçons, calmes, conçues pour les adultes qui veulent se sentir plus à l'aise avec la technologie de tous les jours. Rester en sécurité, rester en contact, les habiletés de tous les jours. Tout se fait à leur rythme. Rien n'est chronométré, rien n'est noté, et rien de ce qu'ils font ici ne peut briser quoi que ce soit.
>
> Voici ce que j'aimerais que vous sachiez, de la part de quelqu'un qui a déjà été l'aidant. Le plus difficile, ce n'est pas la technologie. C'est de garder ses mains loin de l'appareil.
>
> Quand c'est plus rapide de le faire à leur place, ils perdent la chance de l'apprendre. Alors la seule règle, c'est : c'est eux qui conduisent, vous, vous regardez. Assoyez-vous à côté d'eux, pas par-dessus leur épaule. Laissez-les toucher l'écran. Laissez-les se tromper et retrouver leur chemin. C'est plus lent. Mais ça reste.
>
> Les leçons s'occupent d'enseigner. Vous n'avez donc pas à être l'expert, ni la personne patiente, au bout d'une longue journée. Vous avez juste à être là, au début.
>
> Alors voici tout ce qu'on vous demande : commencez une leçon ensemble. Ensuite, prenez un pas de recul, et laissez-les vous surprendre.
