# DCC Video — PILOT 04 — "Is this real?" (spotting AI-generated and fake content, module intro)

**Introductory tier, ~85 seconds.** Module anchor: DCC Adults media-awareness module: AI-generated content, fake images and videos, and voice impersonation. **Distinct from pilot-01** (scam texts and calls): pilot-01 is "someone is asking you for something"; this is "something you're looking at, or hearing, may not be real." Guide: **Margaret** (locked). Story cast: **June** (68, retired office manager, the "this happens to sharp people" carrier, cast architecture §2.2). Tone: calm, factual, dignity-first; the message is "your instincts still work," never "you can't trust anything anymore." Bilingual EN + fr-CA below (FR is DRAFT, human review gated). Governance: written against `dcc-brand-guidelines.md` + `dcc-character-cast-architecture.md`, both read in full this run (Fable creative sprint, 2026-07-18). Hook follows the approved pilot-01 v2 rule: 2-3 generic, universally relatable moments.

## Narration — English (en-CA)

> Have you ever had a moment like one of these?
>
> A video of a famous person, saying something that doesn't sound like them at all.
>
> A photo in your feed that looks real at first, and then something about it is… not quite right.
>
> Or a voice on the phone that sounds exactly like someone in your family. Asking for money. Urgently.
>
> Here's what's going on. Some pictures, videos, and even voices can now be made by computers. They can look and sound very convincing. And here's the important part: you can still tell. Not by being a computer expert. By slowing down.
>
> Three calm checks. One: where is this from? A trusted source, or a stranger's post? Two: pause. Real news, and real family, can wait five minutes. Fakes hate waiting. Three: confirm with the real person. If a voice on the phone says it's your grandson, hang up and call your grandson, at the number you already have.
>
> My friend June got one of those calls. It sounded just like her nephew. It nearly had her, and June knows better. That's the point: this isn't about being smart enough. It's about knowing the checks.
>
> In this short lesson, you'll learn how to look at what you see and hear online with calm eyes, and know what to do when something doesn't feel real.

## Character direction

- **Margaret (guide):** teaches to camera (sc.1, 5, 6, 8). Sc.5 is the tonal keystone: she names the reality of AI fakes plainly, with zero fear-performance; steady eyes, settled voice. Her glasses-up gesture lands at the turn from "here's what's going on" to "you can still tell." Voice per locked baseline; a full beat before "you can still tell."
- **June (story):** appears in sc.4 (the call) and sc.7 (to camera, candid). Sc.4: listening hard, brow tightening; concern, never panic. Sc.7: her locked register, brisk-but-calm, zero shame, confiding: she insists on telling the story so it doesn't happen to you. Her refrain "and I know better, that's the point" is canonical to her persona and is used here. She sets the phone down (decisive, not fearful).
- **The Caller (threat archetype):** heard/implied in sc.4 only. Shown as the approved abstract shaded silhouette; never ethnically or nationally coded, never monstrous. The cloned "family voice" is conveyed by June's reaction and the caption, not by depicting a family member; this avoids ever painting a real cast member (Marcus, Sophie) as the fake.
- **Public figure in sc.2:** a fully generic etched silhouette at a podium. NO real person's likeness, name, or recognizable styling (hard IP rule). The point reads through the caption, not the face.
- Level-profile swap lines (marked per cast architecture §3.4): sc.5's "not by being a computer expert" reassurance and the closing sentence are the tier swap zone. L3 gets "you knew media literacy; here's what changed: voices can be cloned now." The three checks (sc.6) are tier-stable.

## Accuracy note

- AI voice-cloning "family emergency" phone scams (the modernized grandparent scam) are a real, widely documented pattern in Canada; the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre has publicly warned about criminals using AI to imitate loved ones' voices. Safe to depict; depicted generically, no invented phone numbers, scripts kept to the documented pattern (family voice + urgent money request).
- AI-generated images and videos of public figures used in fake endorsements/statements are likewise a documented, current pattern. The video makes no claims about detection being easy or guaranteed: the three checks are process habits (source, pause, verify directly), which remain valid even as generation quality improves. No detection "tells" that could go stale (e.g. "look at the hands") are taught in this intro; the visual "lamppost bending" in sc.3 is a storyboard illustration of the feeling of wrongness, and the narration deliberately does not claim artifacts will always be visible.
- No fabricated statistics used anywhere.
