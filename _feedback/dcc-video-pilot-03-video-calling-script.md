## Narration — English (en-CA)

> Have you ever had a moment like one of these?
>
> Someone sends you a photo of the grandkids. It's lovely. But you find yourself wishing you could hear the laugh that went with it.
>
> Or you make a birthday phone call, and it's nice. But you hang up thinking, I couldn't see their face.
>
> Or the people you love live far away, and the visits are rare, and the time in between feels long.
>
> There's a middle way, between a phone call and a visit. It's called a video call. You talk the way you always have, and you see each other while you do. And yes, you can do this.
>
> My friend Walter thought he couldn't. His grandson Marcus showed him once: when the call comes in, you tap the green button. One tap. That's the whole trick. Everything else is just talking.
>
> Now Walter sees his grandson's face every Sunday. Not just hears him. Sees him.
>
> In this short lesson, you'll learn how to answer a video call, how to make one yourself, and how to end one politely. Step by step, at your own pace. And if someone in your family wants to practise with you, even better. That's what they're there for.

## Narration — Canadian French (fr-CA) — DRAFT, requires human FR review before use

> Avez-vous déjà vécu un moment comme ceux-ci?
>
> Quelqu'un vous envoie une photo des petits-enfants. Elle est belle. Mais vous vous surprenez à souhaiter entendre le rire qui allait avec.
>
> Ou vous faites un appel d'anniversaire, et c'est agréable. Mais vous raccrochez en pensant : je n'ai pas pu voir son visage.
>
> Ou encore, les gens que vous aimez habitent loin, les visites sont rares, et le temps entre les deux semble long.
>

>
> Mon ami Walter pensait que non. Son petit-fils Marcus lui a montré une seule fois : quand l'appel arrive, on touche le bouton vert. Un seul geste. C'est tout le truc. Le reste, c'est simplement une conversation.
>
> Maintenant, Walter voit le visage de son petit-fils tous les dimanches. Il ne fait pas que l'entendre. Il le voit.
>
> Dans cette courte leçon, vous apprendrez à répondre à un appel vidéo, à en faire un vous-même, et à le terminer poliment. Étape par étape, à votre rythme. Et si quelqu'un de votre famille veut pratiquer avec vous, c'est encore mieux. Ils sont là pour ça.

## Character direction

- **Margaret (guide):** teaches to camera only (sc.1, 5, 8); she introduces and debriefs, never acts inside the scenario (cast rule 1). Warm, unhurried; the "And yes, you can do this" line gets her open-palms gesture and a full beat before it. Voice per locked baseline, ~130–135 wpm.
- **Walter (story):** the emotional throughline. Sc.4: quiet, not sad-performed; wistful stillness, phone face-down (his phone-shyness cue). Sc.6: concentrating, a little stiff, dignified; he does the tap himself. Sc.7: the payoff is a slow exhale and a small smile, never celebration (his locked tone: relief as exhale). Nobody is ever the fool; Walter is competent, just new to this.
- **Marcus (story):** shows once, points once, then sits back and lets his grandfather drive. This models the exact family-helper behaviour DCC teaches: helping is not doing-it-for-them. Sunglasses pushed up (approved teen cue). Relaxed, encouraging; no sarcasm.
- **Sophie (story, cameo):** appears only on the call screen in sc.7, holding up a drawing. Bright, sincere, brief. The fridge drawing in sc.4 and the on-screen drawing in sc.7 are the same artwork: a quiet continuity payoff.
- Level-profile swap lines (marked per cast architecture §3.4): sc.5's reassurance sentence and the closing "at your own pace" block are the tier swap zone. L3 trims to "you know calls; video is one tap more." Sc.6–7 (the Walter story) is tier-stable.

## Accessibility

- Burned-in captions EN + FR versions; separate `.txt`/`.vtt` transcript each language.
- Everything shown on screen is also spoken; the "green button" beat names the colour AND the action ("tap to answer") so colour is never the only cue.
- Calm pacing throughout; the payoff scene holds long enough for low-vision viewers to take in the composition. High-contrast end-card per DCC tokens.
- No live app UI is shown (staleness rule): the "green button" is a generic etched motif, true across FaceTime, WhatsApp, Messenger and Zoom alike, not a screenshot of any one app.

## Accuracy note

- "Tap the green answer button" is generically true across the mainstream video-calling apps a Canadian senior will meet (incoming-call screens use a green/answer affordance); the lesson body, not the intro, handles per-app differences. No invented app names, no fabricated statistics, no version-specific claims that could go stale.
- The Walter/Marcus story is fictional and presented as such by cast convention (recurring original characters), consistent with his canonical backstory in the cast architecture. No real-person likeness.
