# DCC Video — PILOT 02 — "Welcome to the Digital Confidence Centre" (intro / welcome)

**Introductory / onboarding tier, ~90 seconds.** The airline-safety-briefing model from `dcc-onboarding-and-measurement-2026-07-17.md`: calm, crisp, says everything, shows nothing that can date. Character: **Margaret** (the single locked guide, per `dcc-character-cast-architecture.md` §1). Tone: serious, warm, dignified, permission-giving. Bilingual EN + fr-CA below (FR is DRAFT, human review gated). Governance: written against `dcc-brand-guidelines.md` + the character cast architecture, both read in full this run (Fable creative sprint, 2026-07-18).

**Staleness rule (binding):** no live website UI is shown at any point. Navigation is described in plain words over simple generic motifs (a phone, a tablet, a computer, three plain section cards). The video stays true even when the site's design changes.

## Narration — English (en-CA)

> Welcome. I'm so glad you're here.
>
> Before anything else: this welcome is about ninety seconds long, and there is nothing you need to do while you watch. You can just listen.
>
> This is the Digital Confidence Centre. It was built for Canadians who want to feel more comfortable with everyday technology. Phones, messages, video calls, staying safe. All of it, one small step at a time.
>
> Here's how it works. The Centre is organized into short lessons, grouped into a few simple sections: staying safe, staying connected, and everyday skills. Each lesson tells you up front how long it takes. Most are just a few minutes.
>
> You can use whatever device you have. A phone, a tablet, a computer. And if you're not sure what you have, that's okay too. The lessons will meet you where you are.
>
> Now, the most important part. You are in charge here. You can skip anything. You can pause anytime. You can watch a lesson twice, or ten times. Nothing is timed, nothing is graded, and nothing you do here can break anything.
>
> And if you've been feeling a little behind, or a little uncomfortable with all of this, I want you to know: you are exactly who this place was made for. You're not behind. You're just getting started.
>
> Take your time. We'll be right here.

## Character direction (Margaret)

- Etched DCC style, seeded from the master reference art once designed (post DESIGN GATE).
- This is Margaret at her warmest and stillest. No scenario, no problem to solve; she is simply hosting. Emotive arc: warm welcome (sc.1–3) → clear, unhurried explaining (sc.4–5) → open-palmed permission-giving, glasses pushed up into hair (sc.6, her signature "here's the fix" gesture repurposed as "here's your freedom") → quiet, direct sincerity for the "feeling a little behind" line (sc.7). That line is the emotional centre of the video; she slows down for it and looks straight at the viewer.
- Voice per the locked baseline (§1.4 of the cast architecture): ~130 wpm here (low end of her range), full beats after "You are in charge here" and before "You're not behind." No hype-lift anywhere; every sentence lands settled.
- Level-profile swap lines (marked now per cast architecture §3.4): the reassurance block in sc.6–7 is the L1/L3 swap zone. L3 "Back in the Game" trims sc.7 to a single respectful acknowledgement; L1 extends the permission statements. Sc.1–5 are tier-stable.

## Accessibility

- Burned-in captions EN + FR versions; separate `.txt`/`.vtt` transcript each language.
- Everything shown on screen (section names, device names, the length statement) is also spoken; nothing relies on the visual alone.
- The stated length ("about ninety seconds") is honoured in the final cut; a video that promises 90 seconds and runs 3 minutes breaks the exact trust it exists to build.
- Calm pacing, high-contrast text on the calm blue/black background per DCC tokens. Never black text on the darker-blue banners (known contrast bug class, brand guidelines §4).

## Accuracy note

- No statistics, no threat content, no claims about the product beyond what it verifiably is (short lessons, self-paced, no grading, works on common devices). The "nothing you do here can break anything" line is accurate for DCC itself (static site, no account, no data collection per ADR-0004) and is scoped by context to the Centre, not to the learner's device in general.
- Section names in sc.4 ("staying safe, staying connected, everyday skills") are descriptive groupings, deliberately generic so the video does not go stale if site section labels change (staleness rule). If final site labels diverge sharply, revisit the narration once, not per redesign.
