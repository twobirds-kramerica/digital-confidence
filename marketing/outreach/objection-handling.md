# Objection Handling Guide — Digital Confidence Centre
## Two Birds Innovation — B2B Sales Reference

---

### 1. "We don't have budget."

Budget constraints are real, and I don't want to dismiss that. What I'd ask is whether the cost of not acting has been quantified — one fraud claim, one staff hour a week fielding "how do I use my app" calls, one member who leaves because they never felt supported. The platform is $12,000 annually for a financial institution, and it's eligible to be offset through the federal New Horizons for Seniors Programme — which funds up to $25,000 for exactly this kind of digital inclusion work. Would it be worth a 15-minute call just to see if the grant angle could change the math?

---

### 2. "We already have financial literacy programs."

Financial literacy and digital confidence are genuinely different problems. Most financial literacy programmes cover budgeting, debt, and investment — they're not designed to help a 74-year-old recognise a phishing text before she clicks it. Digital Confidence Centre is specifically about safe technology use: scam recognition, password safety, online banking navigation, and smartphone basics. Can I ask what your current programme covers for seniors who are new to digital banking? That would help me understand whether there's a real gap or if you're already set.

---

### 3. "Seniors don't use technology."

That was true 10 years ago. Today, the fastest-growing group of smartphone adopters in Canada is adults 65 and over — and banking apps, telehealth, and government services are increasingly digital-only. The challenge isn't that seniors won't use technology; it's that they don't feel confident doing it safely. That's exactly the gap Digital Confidence Centre is built to close. What's your experience been with your own senior members — are they engaging with your digital banking at all?

---

### 4. "I need to talk to my team."

Absolutely — that's the right process for a decision like this. Would it help if I sent a one-page summary you could share with them? It covers the platform, the pricing tiers, the grant funding angle, and a demo link so they can see it without needing a call. And if it's useful, I'm happy to join a brief call with whoever needs to be in the room — just let me know who that is and I'll work around your schedule.

---

### 5. "We tried something like this before and it didn't work."

That's really useful to know — and honestly, it's the most important thing I could hear. Can you tell me more about what you tried? Was it a programme that didn't get uptake from seniors, a vendor that underdelivered, or something else? I want to understand what "didn't work" meant in your context, because the failure modes for digital literacy programmes are pretty specific — and some of them are exactly what we built around. I'd rather know that upfront than find out after.

---

### 6. "Can't we just use YouTube videos for free?"

You can — and some of your members probably are. The issue is that YouTube isn't curated, isn't sequenced, isn't branded as coming from you, and most importantly, it's designed for people who already know how to search for what they need. A 73-year-old who doesn't know what she doesn't know won't find the right video at the right time. Digital Confidence Centre is a structured 16-module curriculum, written specifically for that person, under your organisation's name. The question isn't whether free content exists — it's whether free content actually reaches the members who need it most. Does that distinction land for you?

---

### 7. "What's your track record? You're a new company."

That's a fair question and I'd rather address it directly than sidestep it. Two Birds Innovation is early-stage, yes. What I can offer is: the platform is fully built and live, the content has been reviewed for accuracy and accessibility, and I can put you in touch with early pilot participants who can speak to their experience. I'm also building for a long-term relationship — I'm not here to close a deal and disappear. Part of my pitch is that I'll help you apply for grant funding and support your implementation. Does it help to see the demo and judge the product on its own merits?

---

### 8. "The grant process sounds complicated."

It can be — I won't pretend otherwise. But for New Horizons for Seniors specifically, the application is more straightforward than most federal programmes, and community organisations in Ontario apply successfully every year. More importantly, I help with it. Part of the Tier 2 and Tier 3 onboarding is walking through the application together — I know the framing that works for digital inclusion projects. You wouldn't be navigating it alone. Would it help if I sent you the programme overview so you can see what's involved before we go any further?

---

### 9. "Our members are too old for this."

The platform was designed for adults 70 and over — that's not an accident. We use large text, plain language, no jargon, and an iPad-first interface because we know who the user is. I'd gently push back on the idea that any senior is "too old" — what most older adults need is a learning environment that meets them where they are, not one that assumes they'll adapt to whatever the tech industry builds. That's exactly what Digital Confidence Centre is. What age range are most of your senior members? I'd be curious whether you've seen appetite for digital help from that group when it's offered in the right format.

---

### 10. "What about privacy and data security?"

This is actually one of our clearest differentiators: we don't collect data from end users. The platform runs on your infrastructure (GitHub Pages or your own hosting), and seniors' activity is not tracked, not stored, and not sent anywhere. There's no login system, no analytics pipeline, no third-party data sharing. We built it this way deliberately because data privacy for older adults is a serious issue and many seniors (rightly) worry about it. Your organisation controls the platform — we don't have a back door into your members' behaviour. Does that address your concern, or is there a specific aspect of data handling you'd want documented?

---

### BONUS OBJECTION 11. "We're waiting to see how the market develops."

Waiting makes sense when you're evaluating an unproven technology. But digital literacy for seniors isn't an emerging trend — it's a documented, growing gap that costs organisations money every year it goes unaddressed. The question isn't whether this will matter; it's whether you want to be ahead of it or behind it. Credit unions and libraries that invest in member/patron education now will have a story to tell funders, boards, and communities. What would need to be true for this to feel like the right time to act?

---

### BONUS OBJECTION 12. "Our IT team would need to review it."

That's a great instinct — and the review will be very short. The platform is entirely static: flat HTML, CSS, and JavaScript files hosted on GitHub Pages or any standard web host. There's no database, no login system, no API calls to external servers, no data in transit. There's genuinely nothing for IT to worry about from a security standpoint. I can send technical documentation for their review, or I can join a brief call with your IT contact if that's helpful. Want me to put together a one-pager for them?

---

### BONUS OBJECTION 13. "We'd want to customise the content."

The white-label licence includes branding customisation — your logo, colours, and contact information. Module content is standardised because it's been written and reviewed for accuracy and accessibility. That said, if there's a specific module or topic that's particularly relevant to your members — say, a section on your own digital banking app — we can discuss what's possible as part of onboarding. What kind of customisation were you thinking about?

---

### BONUS OBJECTION 14. "We already have a vendor for something similar."

I'd genuinely like to understand more about what they offer. Is it a digital literacy platform built for seniors specifically, or is it a broader financial education tool? And is it white-labelled under your brand? I ask because most of the "similar" products I'm aware of are either built for younger users and adapted, or they're American content that references banking systems and fraud patterns that don't match Ontario. I'm not here to dismiss what you're using — but I'd be curious whether it's actually solving the same problem.

---

### BONUS OBJECTION 15. "The price seems high."

I understand, especially for smaller organisations. The Tier 1 Community licence is $5,000 annually — that's for community organisations, senior centres, and smaller libraries. For financial institutions, the Tier 2 licence is $12,000, which works out to less than $1,000 a month for a platform that runs year-round without additional staff time. To put it in context: one prevented fraud claim can easily exceed that amount, and the New Horizons grant can offset the entire first-year cost. What's your typical budget range for member education programming? That would help me point you to the right tier.
