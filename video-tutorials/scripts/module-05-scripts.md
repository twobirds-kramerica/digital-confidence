## Video 1 of 3: Setting Up and Using Email on Your iPad
**Target Duration:** 7 minutes
**Difficulty:** Beginner

### HOOK (First 15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Email is how your doctor's office sends reminders, how your grandchildren share photos, how your bank confirms transactions, and how you receive important receipts. Getting your email set up properly on your iPad takes just a few minutes, and it makes a real difference in how connected you feel day to day."

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Welcome to Module 5. In this first video, we'll add your email account to your iPad's Mail app, learn how to navigate your inbox, and write and send a complete email from scratch. Whether you have a Gmail, Outlook, Rogers, or Bell email account, the steps are very similar."
[VISUAL CUE]: Mail app icon. Title card.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 5 · Video 1 of 3: Setting Up and Using Email

---

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: Adding Your Email Account to iPad**
[VOICEOVER]: "If your email isn't already showing up in the Mail app, you need to add your account. Go to Settings, scroll down to Mail, tap Accounts, then Add Account. You'll see a list of popular email providers — tap yours. If you use Gmail, tap Google. If you use a Rogers or Bell address, tap Other and enter your details manually. Enter your email address and password, then tap Next. Your iPad will verify your account and add it. From now on, new emails will arrive in the Mail app automatically."
[VISUAL CUE]: Settings → Mail → Accounts → Add Account → provider list → Gmail selected → login screen.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Settings → Mail → Accounts → Add Account → choose your provider

**SEGMENT 2: Reading and Navigating Your Inbox**
[VOICEOVER]: "Open the Mail app — the blue envelope icon. Your inbox shows messages in a list, newest at the top. Messages with a blue dot on the left are unread. Tap any message to open it. At the top of the open message, you'll see the sender's name and email address — tap the name to see the full address. Use the arrow buttons at the top to move to the next or previous message. Tap the back arrow to return to the inbox list."
[VISUAL CUE]: Inbox shown, blue dot highlighted, message opened, sender info expanded, navigation arrows used.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Blue dot = unread · Tap the sender's name to see their full email address

**SEGMENT 3: Composing and Sending an Email**
[VOICEOVER]: "To write a new email, tap the compose button — it's the pencil-in-a-square icon, usually in the bottom-right corner of the inbox. In the To: field, type the email address of your recipient. As you type, the iPad will suggest contacts you've emailed before — tap one to use it, or finish typing the full address and tap the spacebar or Return key. Type a subject in the Subject: line. Then tap the body of the email and write your message. When you're ready, tap the Send button — the blue paper airplane icon at the top-right."
[VISUAL CUE]: Compose icon tapped → To: field typed → contact suggestion tapped → Subject filled → body typed → Send tapped.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Compose → To → Subject → your message → Send (blue paper airplane)

**SEGMENT 4: Replying, Forwarding, and the Toolbar**
[VOICEOVER]: "When you've opened an email, you'll see a toolbar at the bottom with several options. The curved arrow pointing left is Reply — tap it to respond to the sender. The arrow with two heads is Reply All — sends your response to everyone who received the message. The curved arrow pointing right is Forward — sends the email to someone new. There's also a Trash icon to delete the message, and a folder icon to move it to another folder. Take a moment to find these on your screen."
[VISUAL CUE]: Bottom toolbar of an open email — each icon highlighted and labelled in sequence.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Reply · Reply All · Forward · Trash · Move to Folder

**SEGMENT 5: Managing Your Inbox — Flagging and Folders**
[VOICEOVER]: "To keep your inbox manageable, two features help most. First: flagging important emails. Swipe left on an email in the inbox list and tap the orange Flag option. Flagged emails get an orange dot and appear in a special Flagged mailbox you can filter by. Second: folders. Your email provider usually creates folders automatically — Sent, Trash, Spam. Tap the back arrow from your inbox to see your full mailbox list, including these folders. You can also create your own folders by tapping Edit in the mailbox list."
[VISUAL CUE]: Swipe left on email → Flag option. Mailbox list shown with Sent, Trash, Spam folders visible.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Swipe left → Flag (orange) to mark important emails

---

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Two common email frustrations. First: sending an email to the wrong person because the autocomplete suggested the wrong contact. Before tapping Send, check the To: field one more time to make sure the right address is showing. Second: accidentally sending an email before you've finished writing it by tapping Send too early. There is no 'undo send' in the standard Mail app — once it's sent, it's sent. Get into the habit of reading your message once before tapping Send."
[VISUAL CUE]: Autocomplete showing two similar contacts — wrong one selected. Read-before-send reminder.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Always check the To: field before sending — autocomplete can suggest the wrong person

---

### RECAP (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Today's key points. One: add your email account in Settings > Mail > Accounts. Two: blue dot means unread, tap to read. Three: compose button starts a new email, Send button delivers it. Four: Reply, Reply All, Forward, and Trash are in the bottom toolbar of any open message."
[VISUAL CUE]: Four-point recap list.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Add account · Read (blue dot) · Compose · Reply/Forward/Trash

## Video 2 of 3: iMessage and Text Messaging on Your iPad
**Target Duration:** 7 minutes
**Difficulty:** Beginner

### HOOK (First 15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Texting with family is one of the most popular things people do on their iPads — quick messages, photos, funny things the grandkids said. Today we'll make sure you can do all of that effortlessly, including the difference between a blue bubble and a green bubble."

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "In this video, we'll cover the Messages app on your iPad — how to send and receive text messages and iMessages, how to share photos through messages, and a few features that make messaging more expressive and enjoyable."
[VISUAL CUE]: Messages app icon — green speech bubble. Title card.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 5 · Video 2 of 3: iMessage and Text Messaging

---

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: iMessage vs SMS — Blue Bubbles vs Green Bubbles**
[VOICEOVER]: "When you send a message on your iPad, the colour of the speech bubble tells you which type of message it is. Blue bubbles are iMessages — these use the internet and are free, encrypted, and only work between Apple devices. Green bubbles are standard SMS text messages — these go through your phone carrier and may use your text message plan. If you're messaging another iPhone or iPad user, it will almost always be blue. If you're messaging an Android phone, it will be green."
[VISUAL CUE]: Two message bubbles side by side — blue labelled iMessage (free, encrypted, Apple only) and green labelled SMS (phone carrier, any device).
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Blue = iMessage (free, Apple devices) · Green = SMS (phone carrier)

**SEGMENT 2: Starting a New Conversation**
[VOICEOVER]: "Open the Messages app — the green speech bubble icon. Your recent conversations are listed on the left side on iPad. To start a new message, tap the compose icon in the top-right corner — it's a pencil-in-square. In the To: field, type a name from your contacts or a phone number. Tap the name when it appears in the suggestions. Then tap the text field at the bottom — the one that says 'iMessage' or 'Text Message' — and type your message. Tap the blue or green send arrow to send it."
[VISUAL CUE]: Messages opened → compose tapped → contact typed → name selected → message typed → send tapped.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Compose → To: field → type contact name → message → send arrow

**SEGMENT 3: Replying to a Message**
[VOICEOVER]: "When someone sends you a message, it will appear in your conversation list on the left. A red number badge on the Messages icon tells you how many unread messages you have. Tap the conversation to open it. The messages appear in chronological order — newest at the bottom. Tap the text field at the bottom to bring up the keyboard, type your reply, and tap the send arrow. To quote a specific message in your reply, press and hold that message until options appear, then tap Reply."
[VISUAL CUE]: Messages badge, conversation opened, message thread shown, text field tapped, reply sent.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Red badge = unread messages · Tap conversation → type at the bottom → send

**SEGMENT 4: Sending Photos in a Message**
[VOICEOVER]: "To send a photo in a message, tap the plus (+) icon or the camera/photo icon to the left of the text field. Choose 'Photo Library' to pick a photo from your camera roll, or 'Camera' to take a new one right now. After selecting, the photo will appear above the text field — you can add a message below it if you want. Tap the send arrow to send. Photos sent by iMessage are full quality; photos sent by SMS are often compressed."
[VISUAL CUE]: Plus icon tapped → Photo Library chosen → photo selected → photo in message field → sent.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Plus icon or camera icon → Photo Library → select photo → send

**SEGMENT 5: Reactions, Memoji, and Tapbacks**
[VOICEOVER]: "Here's something delightful: you can react to a message without typing anything. Press and hold a message bubble until a row of small emoji icons appears — these are called Tapbacks. Tap one to react with a heart, thumbs up, exclamation point, and so on. The person who sent the message will see your reaction pop up on their message. If you want to use Memoji — the animated characters that look like you — tap the Memoji sticker icon next to the text field. These can make messaging much more fun and expressive."
[VISUAL CUE]: Message held → Tapback row appears → heart selected → heart appears on message. Memoji icon tapped and sticker panel shown.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Hold a message → Tapback reactions appear — react without typing

---

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Two messaging mix-ups. First: sending a sensitive message or photo to a group conversation instead of one person. Always check the name at the top of the conversation before sending. If you see multiple names, it's a group chat. Second: accidentally tapping Send before you've finished writing. On iPhone and some iPads with iMessage, you can undo a sent message within about 2 minutes by pressing and holding the message and tapping 'Undo Send' — but only for iMessages, not SMS."
[VISUAL CUE]: Group chat header shown vs single contact header. Undo Send option shown (press and hold sent message).
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Check the conversation header — multiple names means it's a group chat

---

[VISUAL CUE]: Four-point recap list.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Blue = iMessage · Green = SMS · Plus = send photos · Hold = Tapback reactions

## Video 3 of 3: Staying Safe in Your Inbox
**Target Duration:** 6 minutes
**Difficulty:** Beginner

### HOOK (First 15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Your email inbox and text messages are where scammers most often try to reach you. But with a few simple habits, you can spot a suspicious message in seconds — and delete it before it causes any trouble. Let's walk through exactly what to look for."

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "In this final video of Module 5, we'll cover how to identify phishing emails and smishing texts, how to report spam on your iPad, and how to set up filters to reduce junk mail. We covered scam tactics in Module 2 — this video shows you the practical steps to protect your inbox."
[VISUAL CUE]: Inbox with one clearly suspicious email visible (illustrated, not real). Title card.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 5 · Video 3 of 3: Staying Safe in Your Inbox

---

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: The Sender Address Test**
[VOICEOVER]: "The single most reliable way to check if an email is legitimate is to look at the sender's full email address — not just the display name. Scammers can make an email appear to be 'TD Canada Trust' in the name field, but the actual email address might be something like support@td-accounts-verify.com — which has nothing to do with TD Bank. To see the full email address in iPad Mail, tap the sender's name in the From: field. The full address will expand. If it doesn't match the company's real domain, delete the message."
[VISUAL CUE]: Email open. Sender name tapped. Full email address revealed. Domain highlighted. Suspicious domain labelled.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Tap the sender's name → check the full email address after the @ symbol

**SEGMENT 2: Red Flags in Email Content**
[VOICEOVER]: "Beyond the address, suspicious emails share several patterns. Watch for: urgency ('Your account will be closed in 24 hours'), threats ('Failure to respond will result in legal action'), requests to click a link to 'verify' your account, generic greetings ('Dear Customer' instead of your name), and spelling or grammar errors. Legitimate organisations — your bank, Canada Post, the CRA — send well-written, non-threatening emails that don't demand immediate action or scare you."
[VISUAL CUE]: Illustrated suspicious email with five red flags highlighted and labelled: urgency, threat, link, generic greeting, spelling error.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Urgency + threats + generic greeting + link = almost always a scam

**SEGMENT 3: What To Do With a Suspicious Email**
[VOICEOVER]: "When you receive a suspicious email, the safest action is: do not tap any link, do not reply, do not call any phone number listed in the email. Simply delete it. Swipe left on the email in your inbox list and tap Trash. If you want to report it, swipe left and tap the flag icon, then choose Move to Junk — this helps train your email provider's filters to catch similar emails in future. You can also forward it to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre at reportphishing@antifraudcentre.ca."
[VISUAL CUE]: Suspicious email → swipe left → Move to Junk. Then email forwarded to reportphishing address.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Suspicious email → swipe left → Move to Junk → delete — don't click anything

**SEGMENT 4: Filtering Out Text Message Spam**
[VOICEOVER]: "On your iPad, you can filter text messages from unknown senders so they go to a separate list rather than appearing in your main inbox. Go to Settings, then Messages, and turn on 'Filter Unknown Senders.' This creates a separate 'Unknown Senders' tab in the Messages app — messages from people not in your contacts land there first, so you can check them at your leisure rather than having them interrupt your regular conversations."
[VISUAL CUE]: Settings → Messages → Filter Unknown Senders toggle turned on. Messages app showing two tabs: All Messages and Unknown Senders.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Settings → Messages → Filter Unknown Senders — separates texts from strangers

**SEGMENT 5: Blocking a Number or Email Address**
[VOICEOVER]: "If the same spam number or email address keeps contacting you, you can block it entirely. In Messages: open the conversation, tap the contact name or number at the top, then tap Info, then Block this Caller. In Mail: open the email, tap the sender's name, then tap Block this Contact. Blocked contacts cannot reach you through Messages or Mail. You can review and unblock contacts anytime in Settings."
[VISUAL CUE]: Messages → conversation → contact info → Block this Caller. Mail → email → sender tapped → Block this Contact.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Messages: tap contact → Info → Block · Mail: tap sender → Block this Contact

---

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Two mistakes that lead to problems. First: unsubscribing from spam by clicking the 'Unsubscribe' link at the bottom. Clicking unsubscribe from a legitimate newsletter you signed up for is fine. But clicking unsubscribe from a spam email you never signed up for confirms to the scammer that your email address is active — which often leads to more spam. For messages you never signed up for, just delete and junk, don't unsubscribe. Second: assuming an email with your name in the greeting is legitimate. Scammers can buy name-and-email lists that include your name."
[VISUAL CUE]: Unsubscribe link at bottom of email — with a warning overlay. "Your name in the greeting ≠ legitimate sender" graphic.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Don't click "Unsubscribe" on spam you never signed up for — just delete

---

### RECAP (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Today's key points. One: tap the sender's name to see the full email address — check the domain. Two: red flags are urgency, threats, generic greeting, and links. Three: don't click — swipe to Junk and delete. Four: turn on Filter Unknown Senders for texts. Five: block persistent nuisances in Messages or Mail settings."
[VISUAL CUE]: Five-point recap list.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Check sender address · Spot red flags · Junk and delete · Filter unknown texts · Block repeat offenders
