## Video 1 of 3: FaceTime — Video Calling With Apple Devices
**Target Duration:** 7 minutes
**Difficulty:** Beginner

### HOOK (First 15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Being able to see someone's face when you talk to them — really see them, in real time, on the other side of the country or the world — is one of the most meaningful things your iPad can do. FaceTime has brought together families and friends separated by distance in a way that no other technology quite has. Let's get you set up today."

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Welcome to Module 8 of the Digital Confidence Centre — Stay Connected. In this first video, we'll cover FaceTime — Apple's free video calling app. You'll learn how to make and receive a FaceTime call, how to use the controls during a call, and a few ways to make the experience better for everyone."
[VISUAL CUE]: FaceTime app icon. Family video call illustration. Title card.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 8 · Video 1 of 3: FaceTime Basics

---

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: What Is FaceTime and What Do You Need?**
[VOICEOVER]: "FaceTime is Apple's built-in video calling app. It's completely free to use — it calls over the internet using your Wi-Fi connection rather than your phone plan, so there are no long-distance charges, even for calls to family in British Columbia or overseas. To use FaceTime, both you and the person you're calling need an Apple device — iPhone, iPad, or Mac. They also need to have FaceTime set up with their Apple ID. If someone has an Android phone, we'll cover video calling options for them in Video 2 with Zoom."
[VISUAL CUE]: FaceTime app icon. Two Apple devices connected over the internet. No long-distance cost graphic.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: FaceTime: free video calls between Apple devices · No long-distance charges

**SEGMENT 2: Making a FaceTime Call**
[VOICEOVER]: "To start a FaceTime call, open the FaceTime app — the green icon with a white video camera. Tap the plus icon in the top-right corner to start a new call. Type the name or Apple ID email address of the person you want to call, or tap the plus icon next to the To: field to choose from your contacts. Once you've selected them, tap the green video camera icon to make a video call, or the phone icon for an audio-only FaceTime call. Your call will ring on their device — wait for them to answer."
[VISUAL CUE]: FaceTime opened → plus icon → contact typed → video button tapped → ringing state shown.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: FaceTime → + → contact name → green video button → wait for them to answer

**SEGMENT 3: The FaceTime Call Screen**
[VOICEOVER]: "Once connected, you'll see the other person large on your screen and yourself in a small window — usually in the top corner. You can drag your small window anywhere on the screen if it's in the way. During the call, you'll see a set of controls — tap the screen if they disappear. The microphone button mutes your audio. The camera button switches between front and rear camera. The red phone button ends the call. There's also a button to add more people for a group call."
[VISUAL CUE]: FaceTime call screen with each control labelled: mute, flip camera, end call, add person, self-view window position.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Tap screen to see controls: mute · flip camera · end (red button) · add people

**SEGMENT 4: Receiving a FaceTime Call**
[VOICEOVER]: "When someone FaceTimes you, a notification will appear on your screen — even if your iPad is locked. You'll see the caller's name and two buttons: a green Accept button and a red Decline button. Tap Accept to answer. If you're not in a position to talk, tap Decline to send them to voicemail, or tap the small 'Remind Me' option to set a reminder to call back. If your iPad was asleep when the call came in and you missed it, you'll see a missed call notification in FaceTime when you next open the app."
[VISUAL CUE]: FaceTime incoming call notification on lock screen. Accept and Decline buttons highlighted. Missed call shown in app.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Incoming call: green = Accept · red = Decline · Missed calls appear in FaceTime app

**SEGMENT 5: Group FaceTime and SharePlay**
[VOICEOVER]: "FaceTime supports group calls — you can have your whole family on screen at once. To add people during a call, tap the screen for controls, then tap the Add People button, and enter more names. Each person appears in a tile. Whoever is speaking gets a highlighted tile. FaceTime also has SharePlay — a feature where everyone on the call can watch the same video, listen to music, or share their screen together. Tap the SharePlay icon during a call and select Share Screen if you want to show someone what's on your iPad."
[VISUAL CUE]: Group FaceTime call with multiple tiles. Add person button tapped. SharePlay icon shown and screen share initiated.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Add people during a call · SharePlay: watch together or share your screen

---

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Two FaceTime frustrations to avoid. First: the call looking dark or you being hard to see. This is usually a lighting issue — make sure you're facing a light source, not sitting with your back to a window. Even just turning on a lamp in front of you makes a huge difference. Second: the other person can't hear you. Check that you haven't accidentally tapped the mute button — it looks like a microphone with a line through it when muted. Also check that your iPad volume is turned up using the side buttons."
[VISUAL CUE]: Dark video call → lamp turned on → bright, clear video. Mute icon shown with 'muted' label.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Can't be seen clearly? Face a light source · Muted? Tap the microphone button

---

### RECAP (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Today's key points. One: FaceTime is free between Apple devices using Wi-Fi. Two: open FaceTime, tap plus, choose a contact, tap the video button. Three: during the call, tap the screen to see controls — mute, flip camera, end. Four: incoming calls appear as a notification even when your screen is locked."
[VISUAL CUE]: Four-point recap list.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: FaceTime free on Wi-Fi · Plus → contact → video button · Tap screen for controls · Answer from lock screen

---

## Video 2 of 3: Zoom — Video Calling With Any Device
**Target Duration:** 7 minutes
**Difficulty:** Beginner

### HOOK (First 15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Not everyone in your family has an iPhone. Some are on Android. Some are on Windows laptops. Zoom is the video calling app that works for everyone — and it became the way millions of families stayed connected during the past few years. Today we'll make sure you can join and host Zoom calls with confidence."

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Zoom is a free video calling and meeting app that works on any device — Apple, Android, Windows, or Mac. In this video, we'll cover how to download and set up Zoom on your iPad, how to join a Zoom call when someone sends you an invitation, and how to start your own call and invite others."
[VISUAL CUE]: Zoom app icon. Multiple device types shown connecting together. Title card.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 8 · Video 2 of 3: Zoom Video Calls

---

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: Downloading and Setting Up Zoom**
[VOICEOVER]: "Search for 'Zoom' in the App Store — the official app is called 'Zoom — One Platform to Connect' and is published by Zoom Video Communications. Download it free. When it opens, you'll be asked to sign up or sign in. Tap 'Sign Up Free' and create an account using your email address. You'll receive a confirmation email — open it and tap the confirmation link. After confirming, you're ready to use Zoom. You don't need a paid account for most personal use."
[VISUAL CUE]: App Store → Zoom search → official app → download → sign up → email confirmation tapped.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: App Store: search "Zoom" → Zoom Video Communications → free download → sign up

**SEGMENT 2: Joining a Zoom Call From an Invitation**
[VOICEOVER]: "The most common way to join a Zoom call is when someone sends you an invitation by email or text message. The invitation will contain a link that says something like 'Join Zoom Meeting' followed by a web address. Tap that link on your iPad. If you have the Zoom app installed, it will open automatically. You may be asked whether to allow your microphone and camera — tap Allow for both. You'll see a waiting room screen until the host lets you in. Some calls have a 'Join with Video' button — tap it to appear on screen."
[VISUAL CUE]: Email with Zoom link → link tapped → Zoom app opens → camera/mic permissions → waiting room → admitted.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Tap the invitation link → allow camera + microphone → wait in waiting room → you're in

**SEGMENT 3: Zoom Controls During a Call**
[VOICEOVER]: "Once you're in a Zoom call, controls appear at the bottom of the screen. The microphone icon mutes and unmutes your audio. The video camera icon turns your camera on or off. If the controls disappear, tap the screen once and they'll reappear. Participants shows you everyone on the call. Chat lets you type a message to the group or an individual. To leave when you're done, tap End or Leave at the bottom-right, then tap 'Leave Meeting.'"
[VISUAL CUE]: Zoom call screen with labelled controls: microphone, video, participants, chat, Leave button.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Mute mic · Turn off camera · Participants list · Chat · Leave Meeting

**SEGMENT 4: Starting Your Own Zoom Call**
[VOICEOVER]: "To start a Zoom call and invite others, open the Zoom app and tap New Meeting. You'll see your camera view — tap Start Meeting to begin. Then tap Participants, then Invite, and choose how to send invitations — by email or by copying the meeting link to paste into a text message. Send the link to everyone you want to invite. They'll tap the link from their device and join your meeting. The person who starts the meeting is the host — you control when the meeting starts and ends."
[VISUAL CUE]: Zoom home screen → New Meeting → Start Meeting → Participants → Invite → email invitation sent.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: New Meeting → Start Meeting → Participants → Invite → share the link

**SEGMENT 5: Zoom Tips for Better Calls**
[VOICEOVER]: "A few practical tips for better Zoom calls. Place your iPad so the camera is at eye level — propped on a book or a stand — rather than looking up at you from the table. This feels more natural for the people you're talking to. If you're in a meeting and there's background noise, mute yourself when you're not speaking — just remember to unmute when you want to talk. If someone can't hear you, check that the correct microphone is selected under the audio settings. And a good lamp in front of you makes the biggest difference to how well people can see you."
[VISUAL CUE]: iPad propped at eye level. Mute button shown with reminder to unmute. Lamp positioned in front for good lighting.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: iPad at eye level · Mute when not speaking · Face a light source

---

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Two Zoom mistakes. First: accidentally leaving the meeting instead of muting yourself. Leave Meeting is different from the mute button — make sure you tap the microphone icon to mute, not Leave. Second: forgetting to check your background. Whatever is behind you is visible to everyone — take a quick look before joining any call. Zoom has a virtual background feature in settings if you'd prefer more privacy, though these can occasionally look a bit blurry on older devices."
[VISUAL CUE]: Mute vs Leave — two buttons shown and differentiated clearly. Camera preview showing tidy vs cluttered background.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Mute = microphone button · Leave = separate button in the corner — don't confuse them

---

### RECAP (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Today's key points. One: Zoom works with any device — download free from the App Store. Two: join by tapping the invitation link in an email or text. Three: controls are at the bottom — microphone, camera, participants, leave. Four: start your own call with New Meeting and invite by sharing the link."
[VISUAL CUE]: Four-point recap list.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Zoom free download · Tap invitation link · Bottom controls: mic/camera/leave · New Meeting to host

## Video 3 of 3: Staying Connected Between Video Calls
**Target Duration:** 6 minutes
**Difficulty:** Beginner

### HOOK (First 15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Video calls are wonderful, but connection doesn't have to mean a scheduled 45-minute call. A quick photo sent over messages, a voice memo, a short voice call — these small touchpoints between the big calls are what really keep relationships warm. Today we'll look at all the options."

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "In this final video of Module 8, we'll look at phone calls on your iPad using FaceTime Audio and regular telephone calls, sending voice messages, and a few ideas for staying creatively connected that your family will love."
[VISUAL CUE]: Collage: phone call icon, voice message wave, photo, text bubble. Title card.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 8 · Video 3 of 3: Staying Connected Between Calls

---

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: Regular Phone Calls From Your iPad**
[VOICEOVER]: "Your iPad can make and receive regular phone calls if your iPhone is nearby and you've set up iPhone Cellular Calls in your iPhone's Settings. When your iPhone rings, your iPad rings too, and you can answer on either device. To call someone from your iPad, open the FaceTime app, find the person, and tap the phone icon for an audio call. Or use the Contacts app and tap the phone number — your iPad will connect through your iPhone."
[VISUAL CUE]: FaceTime app → contact selected → phone (audio) icon tapped. Call connecting visual.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Phone call on iPad: FaceTime → contact → phone icon · Requires iPhone nearby

**SEGMENT 2: FaceTime Audio — Better Than a Phone Call**
[VOICEOVER]: "FaceTime Audio is Apple's audio-only version of FaceTime. It works over Wi-Fi, sounds significantly better than a regular phone call, and is completely free — no minutes used, no long-distance charges. If you want to have a long phone chat with family in another province or country, FaceTime Audio is the best option. Just make sure both of you are on Wi-Fi or have a good data connection. Start it the same way as a video FaceTime call, but choose the phone icon instead of the video icon."
[VISUAL CUE]: FaceTime → contact → phone icon (not video). Quality comparison graphic: FaceTime Audio vs. regular call.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: FaceTime Audio: free, crystal-clear calls over Wi-Fi · Phone icon in FaceTime app

**SEGMENT 3: Sending Voice Messages**
[VOICEOVER]: "Sometimes you want to share something more personal than a text but a full call isn't possible. Voice messages are perfect for this — they're audio recordings sent through the Messages app. To send one, open a conversation in Messages, press and hold the microphone icon next to the text field, record your message, then lift your finger to send. The recipient hears your actual voice, with all the warmth and tone that text can't convey. They can play it back whenever convenient."
[VISUAL CUE]: Messages app → microphone icon held → recording wave shown → released → voice message sent → recipient playing it back.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Messages → hold microphone → record → release to send a voice message

**SEGMENT 4: Creative Ways to Stay Connected**
[VOICEOVER]: "Here are a few ideas that families find especially meaningful. First: send a photo with context — instead of just sending a photo of your garden, add a message describing what you were thinking about or why that moment felt special. Second: use the Live Photo feature — it captures a tiny video around your photo, so when family tap and hold the photo in their Messages, they see the moment come alive briefly. Third: share an article, a recipe, or a YouTube video via Messages — tap the share button in Safari or YouTube and choose Messages to send it directly to a family member."
[VISUAL CUE]: Photo with caption being sent. Live Photo icon in Camera app. Safari share → Messages path.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: More than a photo: add context · Live Photos bring moments to life · Share articles and videos

**SEGMENT 5: Setting Regular Connection Times**
[VOICEOVER]: "One habit many families find valuable is setting a regular, predictable time to connect — even a brief one. A Sunday morning FaceTime, a Tuesday evening voice message exchange, a shared family photo album that everyone adds to during the week. Predictability reduces the pressure of having to find time spontaneously and gives everyone something to look forward to. You can even use the Reminders app on your iPad to set a gentle weekly reminder."
[VISUAL CUE]: Calendar showing a recurring Sunday event "FaceTime with Family." Reminders app shown with weekly reminder.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Regular connection times reduce pressure — a brief weekly call is better than occasional long ones

---

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "One thing that disconnects people: assuming the other person will initiate. Many family members are waiting for you just as you are waiting for them. The small act of sending a photo, a voice message, or a quick 'thinking of you' text is almost always appreciated — and rarely burdensome to receive. Don't wait for the perfect moment or the perfect thing to say. A brief, genuine message is always better than silence."
[VISUAL CUE]: Two phones face down, each waiting for the other. Then one sends a message. Response comes. Connection made.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Don't wait — a short "thinking of you" message is always welcome

---

Three: share photos, articles, and videos through Messages for richer connection. Four: set a predictable regular connection time — even brief ones matter."
[VISUAL CUE]: Four-point recap list.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: FaceTime Audio free calls · Voice messages · Share articles and videos · Regular connection time
