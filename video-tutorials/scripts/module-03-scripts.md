**Target Duration:** 6 minutes
**Difficulty:** Beginner

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Welcome to Module 3 of the Digital Confidence Centre. Today we're covering passwords — one of the foundations of staying safe online. By the end of this module's three videos, you'll understand what makes a password strong, how to create passwords you can actually remember, and how to use your iPad's built-in security features like Face ID and Touch ID so you barely need to type passwords at all."
[VISUAL CUE]: Title card. Lock icon graphic.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 3 · Video 1 of 3: Why Passwords Matter

---

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: What a Password Actually Does**
[VOICEOVER]: "A password is the key to your digital life. Think of your email account like your home mailbox — but not just for letters. Your email is where password reset links go, where bank statements arrive, where your doctor's office communicates. If someone gains access to your email password, they can potentially access everything connected to that email address. That's why your email password is the most important one you have. Every other account you own could be reset through your email."
[VISUAL CUE]: Simple graphic: email inbox at the centre, with branches going out to: banking, shopping, healthcare, social media. All connected through email.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Your email password protects your entire digital life

**SEGMENT 2: How Hackers Crack Weak Passwords**
[VOICEOVER]: "When hackers try to break into accounts, they often use automated programs that try thousands of password combinations per minute. Simple passwords like 'password,' 'abc123,' or your name and birth year can be cracked in seconds. Names of pets or grandchildren are also easy to guess if someone knows anything about you from social media. This isn't meant to frighten you — it's meant to explain why a long, unusual password is so much safer than a short, predictable one."
[VISUAL CUE]: Animated graphic showing a password strength meter — 'password123' filling instantly vs. a long complex password taking much longer.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Simple passwords are cracked in seconds by automated programs

**SEGMENT 3: What Makes a Password Strong**
[VOICEOVER]: "A strong password has three qualities: it's long — at least 12 characters; it's unpredictable — not a real word or obvious sequence; and it's unique — different for every account. Length is actually the most important factor. A 16-character passphrase is genuinely harder to crack than an 8-character combination of letters and numbers. We'll look at passphrases in the next video, because they're both secure and much easier to remember than random characters."
[VISUAL CUE]: Three-column graphic: LENGTH (12+ chars), UNPREDICTABLE (no real words), UNIQUE (one per account).
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Strong password = Long + Unpredictable + Unique for every account

**SEGMENT 4: Why Using the Same Password Everywhere Is Dangerous**
[VOICEOVER]: "Many people use one password for everything because it's easier to remember. The problem is that if any one of those sites is hacked and your email and password are stolen, criminals immediately try that same combination on hundreds of other sites — your bank, your email, everything. This is called credential stuffing, and it's extremely common. The solution isn't to memorise dozens of passwords — it's to use a password manager, which we'll introduce later in this module."
[VISUAL CUE]: One domino falling, triggering a chain of other dominoes — each labelled with a type of account (email, bank, shopping). Visual metaphor for password reuse.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Same password everywhere = one breach = everything at risk

**SEGMENT 5: Password Managers — What They Are**
[VOICEOVER]: "A password manager is an app that stores all your passwords securely in one place, locked behind a single master password or your fingerprint. You only need to remember one password — the rest are handled for you. Apple's iCloud Keychain, which is built right into your iPad, is a free and very capable option. It remembers your passwords, offers to create strong ones, and fills them in automatically when you visit a site. We'll set this up in Video 2."
[VISUAL CUE]: iCloud Keychain icon. Animated graphic of passwords being stored safely in a vault.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Password manager = one master password protects everything else

---

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Two password mistakes that are very common. First: writing your passwords on a sticky note and attaching it to your computer or iPad. This is the digital equivalent of leaving your house key under the doormat. If you do write passwords down, keep that paper in a secure place — a locked drawer, not near your device. Second: using your name, birth date, or address as any part of your password. These are the first things anyone who knows you will try."
[VISUAL CUE]: Sticky note on a laptop — with a red X. Then a secure locked box for password notes — green checkmark.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Written passwords belong in a locked drawer — not near your device

---

### RECAP (30 seconds)

Three: a password manager like iCloud Keychain does the hard work for you. Next video: how to create passwords you can actually remember."
[VISUAL CUE]: Three-point recap list.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Email password = most important · Long = most secure · Password manager = your solution

## Video 2 of 3: Creating Strong Passwords You Can Remember
**Target Duration:** 7 minutes
**Difficulty:** Beginner

### HOOK (First 15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "What if I told you there's a type of password that's both extremely secure and easy to remember — no random symbols, no trying to recall whether you used a capital A or an ampersand? It's called a passphrase, and it's about to change how you think about passwords."

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "In this video we're going to cover the passphrase technique for creating strong passwords, how to use iCloud Keychain to save and generate passwords automatically, and how to update weak passwords on your most important accounts. This is the practical video — by the end you'll have a real plan for securing your accounts."
[VISUAL CUE]: Title card. A key icon.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 3 · Video 2 of 3: Creating Strong Passwords

---

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: The Passphrase Technique**
[VOICEOVER]: "A passphrase is a password made from four or more random, unrelated words strung together. Something like: 'PurpleLakeBiscuitTrain' — or better yet, add a number and punctuation: 'PurpleLake7Biscuit!' That's 21 characters, incredibly hard for a computer program to guess, and much easier for a human to remember than something like 'Xk7!r2Qp.' The key is that the words should be genuinely random — not a phrase people associate with you, like your hometown or grandchild's name."
[VISUAL CUE]: Four random words appearing one by one: Purple · Lake · Biscuit · Train. Combined into passphrase. Number and punctuation added.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: 4 random words + number + symbol = strong, memorable passphrase

**SEGMENT 2: Creating a Passphrase for Your Email**
[VOICEOVER]: "Let's put this into practice with your email account — since we established in Video 1 that it's the most important. Think of four completely unrelated words that aren't connected to your personal life. Write them down on a piece of paper for now — just until you've memorised it. Add a number in the middle and a symbol at the end. This is now one of the strongest passwords you've ever had. Keep that piece of paper somewhere secure — not near your devices — and we'll eventually move it into iCloud Keychain."
[VISUAL CUE]: Handwritten passphrase on paper. Paper being placed in a drawer — not near a computer.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Write it down temporarily → then memorise it → then add to password manager

**SEGMENT 3: Setting Up iCloud Keychain**
[VOICEOVER]: "iCloud Keychain is Apple's built-in password manager and it's completely free. To turn it on, go to your iPad Settings — the grey icon with gears. Scroll down and tap your name at the very top. Tap iCloud, then Keychain, and turn the toggle on. Once it's on, whenever you create or update a password on a website or app, your iPad will offer to save it. When you come back to that site, the iPad will automatically fill in your username and password. It's like having someone remember all your passwords for you."
[VISUAL CUE]: Step-by-step screen recording: Settings → Apple ID name → iCloud → Passwords and Keychain → toggle on.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Settings → your name → iCloud → Keychain → turn it on

**SEGMENT 4: Using the Strong Password Suggestion**
[VOICEOVER]: "When you create a new account on any website or app, and the iPad sees a password field, it will often pop up a yellow bar at the bottom of the keyboard offering to use a 'Strong Password.' This is iCloud Keychain suggesting a long, random password for you. If you tap 'Use Strong Password,' it creates the password and saves it automatically. You never need to see or remember the password — your iPad will enter it for you every time. This is the ideal setup."
[VISUAL CUE]: Password field on a new account page. Yellow Keychain suggestion bar appearing. "Use Strong Password" tapped. Password saved confirmation.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Tap "Use Strong Password" — your iPad saves and fills it automatically

**SEGMENT 5: Updating Your Most Important Passwords**
[VOICEOVER]: "Now that you know how to create and save strong passwords, let's prioritise. Start with these three: your email password, your banking app password, and your Apple ID password. Your Apple ID is the account you use for everything Apple — the App Store, iCloud, Find My iPhone. If you've had any of these passwords for more than a couple of years, or if they're short and simple, update them now using the passphrase technique. Your iPad will offer to save each one as you update it."
[VISUAL CUE]: Three icons: email, bank app, Apple ID. Each gets a green checkmark as it's described.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Priority: email + banking + Apple ID — update these three first

---

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Two things that trip people up. First: forgetting the iCloud Keychain master — which is your Apple ID password and your device passcode. These unlock everything else, so they need to be memorised or safely written down somewhere very secure. If you lose your Apple ID password, Apple's account recovery process can take days. Second: assuming a saved password means it's stored in the cloud and could be hacked. iCloud Keychain passwords are encrypted — even Apple cannot read them."
[VISUAL CUE]: Apple ID icon. Warning message about forgetting the master password. Lock icon with "encrypted" label.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Memorise your Apple ID password — it unlocks your entire Keychain

---

### RECAP (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Today's key points. One: a passphrase — four random words plus a number and symbol — is both strong and memorable. Two: iCloud Keychain saves and fills your passwords automatically. Three: when creating a new account, use the Strong Password suggestion. Four: update your email, banking, and Apple ID passwords as a priority."
[VISUAL CUE]: Four-point recap list.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Passphrase + iCloud Keychain = secure, simple password management

---

## Video 3 of 3: Face ID and Touch ID — Unlocking Securely Without Typing
**Target Duration:** 6 minutes
**Difficulty:** Beginner

### HOOK (First 15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "What if you could unlock your iPad, open your banking app, and confirm purchases — all without typing a single password? With Face ID and Touch ID, you already can. Let's get that set up today."

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "In this final video of Module 3, we're covering biometric security — which is just a fancy way of saying your iPad can recognise your face or your fingerprint to unlock itself. It's more secure than a passcode, much faster, and completely private — your face or fingerprint data never leaves your device and Apple never has access to it."
[VISUAL CUE]: iPad face recognition animation. Face ID scan graphic.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 3 · Video 3 of 3: Face ID and Touch ID

---

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: What Is Face ID?**
[VOICEOVER]: "Face ID uses the front camera of your iPad to scan your face. It maps over 30,000 points on your face in three dimensions — creating a unique mathematical profile that only your face matches. When you go to unlock your iPad or make a purchase, the camera scans your face in less than a second and, if it matches, lets you in. If it doesn't match, it falls back to asking for your passcode. You can't trick it with a photo."
[VISUAL CUE]: Animated illustration of face-scan dots mapping a face. Green checkmark when matched.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Face ID: scans 30,000 points on your face — more secure than a passcode

**SEGMENT 2: What Is Touch ID?**
[VOICEOVER]: "Touch ID uses the fingerprint sensor on the Home button — or on some newer iPads, the power button on the side. It reads the unique pattern of ridges in your fingerprint. To unlock, you just rest your finger on the sensor for a moment. Like Face ID, if it doesn't recognise the fingerprint, it will ask for your passcode as a backup. Both Face ID and Touch ID are designed so that the device can only be unlocked by you."
[VISUAL CUE]: Finger resting on Home button, ripple animation, green unlock confirmation.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Touch ID: rest your registered finger on the Home or power button

**SEGMENT 3: Setting Up Face ID**
[VOICEOVER]: "To set up Face ID, go to Settings — the grey gear icon — and scroll down to Face ID & Passcode. You'll be asked to enter your current passcode first. Then tap Set Up Face ID and follow the on-screen instructions. Hold your iPad in portrait position, look at the front camera, and slowly move your head in a circle as directed. You'll be asked to do this twice. The whole process takes about 30 seconds. After that, Face ID will be active for unlocking and purchases."
[VISUAL CUE]: Settings → Face ID & Passcode → Set Up Face ID → head rotation animation shown.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Settings → Face ID & Passcode → Set Up Face ID → follow the circles

**SEGMENT 4: Setting Up Touch ID**
[VOICEOVER]: "For iPads with Touch ID, go to Settings and then Touch ID & Passcode. Enter your passcode, then tap Add a Fingerprint. Follow the on-screen instructions — you'll lift and rest your finger on the sensor repeatedly as it builds a complete picture of your fingerprint. When it says 'Adjust your grip,' shift your finger slightly so it captures the edges of your print. After setup, you can add multiple fingers — useful if you sometimes hold the iPad with your left hand."
[VISUAL CUE]: Settings → Touch ID & Passcode → Add a Fingerprint → fingerprint capture animation.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Settings → Touch ID & Passcode → Add a Fingerprint → repeat as directed

**SEGMENT 5: Using Biometrics for Apps and Purchases**
[VOICEOVER]: "Once Face ID or Touch ID is set up, your iPad will use it automatically when you open secure apps like your banking app, when you make purchases in the App Store, and when you access saved passwords in iCloud Keychain. You'll see a small Face ID icon or a fingerprint prompt — just glance at the camera or rest your finger on the sensor, and you're in. If you want to use biometrics in a specific app, check that app's settings — most banking and health apps have a 'Use Face ID' toggle you can turn on."
[VISUAL CUE]: Banking app opening with Face ID prompt. Glance at camera. App unlocks.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Look at the camera or touch the sensor — your apps unlock automatically

---

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Two situations to be aware of. First: Face ID can struggle if you're wearing a mask, sunglasses, or if it's very dark. In these cases, it will just ask for your passcode instead — that's normal behaviour, not a malfunction. Second: don't register someone else's fingerprint or face without thinking carefully. You'd be giving them permanent access to everything on your iPad, including banking apps. If you want a trusted family member to be able to unlock your device in emergencies, discuss it with them and consider whether that level of access makes sense."
[VISUAL CUE]: Face ID fail with mask on — passcode prompt appearing instead. Reassuring explanation.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Face ID with a mask? It will ask for your passcode instead — that's normal

---

Two: set up Face ID in Settings > Face ID & Passcode, or Touch ID in Settings > Touch ID & Passcode. Three: biometrics work automatically for app logins and purchases once set up. Four: your biometric data never leaves your device."
[VISUAL CUE]: Four-point recap list.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Face/fingerprint data stays on your device — Apple never sees it
