## Video 1 of 3: Taking and Viewing Photos on Your iPad
**Target Duration:** 7 minutes
**Difficulty:** Beginner

### HOOK (First 15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Your iPad has a camera that most professional photographers 20 years ago would have envied. It takes stunning photos, stores thousands of them without printing a single one, and they'll never fade or get lost in a drawer. Today we'll get you completely comfortable taking photos and finding them afterwards."

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Welcome to Module 7 of the Digital Confidence Centre. This module is one of the most enjoyable — photos and memories. In this first video, we'll cover how to take photos using your iPad's camera, how to view what you've taken, and the basic editing tools that can make any photo look better in seconds."
[VISUAL CUE]: Camera app icon. Sample iPad photo displayed. Title card.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 7 · Video 1 of 3: Taking and Viewing Photos

---

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: Opening the Camera App**
[VOICEOVER]: "The Camera app is the grey icon with a camera image on it. Tap it and your camera opens immediately — it's designed to be fast because you don't want to miss a moment. You'll see the view through your camera in the main part of the screen. The large round white button at the bottom is the shutter button — tapping it takes the photo. The small square in the bottom-left corner shows a thumbnail of your most recent photo — tap it to go directly to the Photos app and see what you've taken."
[VISUAL CUE]: Camera app opened. Shutter button highlighted with large label. Recent photo thumbnail labelled in bottom-left.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Camera app → large round button = shutter · Small square = recent photos

**SEGMENT 2: Rear Camera vs Front Camera**
[VOICEOVER]: "Your iPad has two cameras. The rear camera — on the back — takes the highest quality photos and is what you'll use most of the time. The front camera — on the front, the side you look at — is for selfies and video calls. To switch between them, tap the icon that looks like two arrows in a circle, or a camera with arrows. The image on screen will flip to show whichever camera you've selected. For family gatherings and landscapes, use the rear camera. For video calls with family, the front camera is activated automatically."
[VISUAL CUE]: Rear camera view, then switch icon tapped, front camera view shown.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Rear camera = best quality photos · Front camera = selfies and video calls · Switch icon = rotate arrows

**SEGMENT 3: Taking a Great Photo — Tips**
[VOICEOVER]: "A few simple tips for better photos. Hold your iPad steady — rest your elbows against your body or place the iPad on a surface. Make sure the subject of the photo is in the centre of the frame, or tap the screen on the subject to focus on them specifically — you'll see a yellow square appear where you tapped, and the camera will sharpen the focus there. Avoid taking photos with a bright window or light source directly behind the subject — this creates a dark silhouette. Move so the light is beside or in front of them instead."
[VISUAL CUE]: Steady hold demonstrated. Tap-to-focus shown with yellow square appearing. Before/after comparison of backlit vs. well-lit subject.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Tap the screen to focus on your subject — yellow square shows focus point

**SEGMENT 4: Viewing Your Photos**
[VOICEOVER]: "To see your photos, open the Photos app — the multi-coloured pinwheel icon. You'll see your most recent photos in the Library view. Tap any photo to view it full screen. To go back to the library, tap the back arrow in the top-left corner. Use two fingers to pinch outward to zoom in on a photo, and pinch inward to zoom back out. To see all your photos as small thumbnails, pinch inward until you see the grid view. Swipe left and right when a photo is open to move to the next or previous photo."
[VISUAL CUE]: Photos app opened → library grid → photo tapped → full screen → pinch zoom → swipe between photos.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Photos app: tap to view full screen · Pinch to zoom · Swipe left/right to browse

**SEGMENT 5: Quick Editing — Improve Any Photo**
[VOICEOVER]: "Your iPad has powerful editing tools built right in to Photos. Open any photo and tap the Edit button in the top-right corner. You'll see automatic adjustment options — tap the magic wand icon for Auto and your iPad will improve the brightness, contrast, and colour automatically. If you don't like it, tap it again to undo. You can also manually adjust brightness and other settings using the sliders. Tap Done to save your edits or Cancel to discard them. Every edit is non-destructive — you can always tap Revert to Original to undo everything."
[VISUAL CUE]: Photo opened → Edit tapped → Auto (magic wand) tapped → before/after comparison → Done tapped.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Edit → magic wand for Auto improve → Done to save · Revert to Original to undo everything

---

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Two photo mistakes. First: taking photos with your fingers over part of the camera lens — usually the rear lens on the back of the iPad. Hold the iPad by the edges, not the back, when shooting. Second: deleting a photo by accident. If you tap the trash icon while viewing a photo, it will ask you to confirm. But if you do delete something you wanted to keep, don't worry. Deleted photos go to the Recently Deleted album and stay there for 30 days before being permanently removed. Go to Albums, scroll down to Recently Deleted, and restore anything you want back."
[VISUAL CUE]: Finger covering lens — photo dark. Correct hold shown. Recently Deleted album location in Albums view.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Deleted by accident? Check Albums → Recently Deleted — photos stay 30 days

---

### RECAP (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Today's key points. One: Camera app — big round button takes the photo. Two: tap the screen to focus on your subject. Three: Photos app — tap to view, pinch to zoom, swipe to browse. Four: tap Edit and the magic wand for automatic photo improvement. Five: deleted photos stay in Recently Deleted for 30 days."
[VISUAL CUE]: Five-point recap list.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Camera button · Tap to focus · Photos app to view · Edit → magic wand · Recently Deleted = 30-day safety net

---

### CALL TO ACTION (15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "In Video 2, we'll look at organising your photo library — albums, Memories, and how to find any photo quickly using search. See you in the playlist."
[VISUAL CUE]: End screen — Video 2 card.

## Video 2 of 3: Organising Your Photo Library
**Target Duration:** 7 minutes
**Difficulty:** Beginner

### HOOK (First 15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "If your photo library has grown to hundreds or thousands of photos and finding one specific picture feels like searching for a needle in a haystack — today's video is going to transform that. Your iPad has incredible tools for organising, searching, and surfacing memories automatically."

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "In this video, we'll cover albums — how to create them and add photos — the Memories feature that automatically creates mini slideshow movies from your photos, and how to use the search function to find any photo instantly by what's in it."
[VISUAL CUE]: Photos app Albums tab. Title card.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 7 · Video 2 of 3: Organising Your Photo Library

---

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: Understanding the Photos App Layout**
[VOICEOVER]: "The Photos app has five tabs at the bottom: Library, For You, Albums, Search, and Shared. Library shows everything in chronological order — All Photos at the bottom zoom level, or organised by Years, Months, and Days if you pinch in to zoom out. For You shows Memories and suggested photos. Albums is where you organise and find your own collections. Search lets you find photos by typing what you're looking for. We'll explore all of these today."
[VISUAL CUE]: Photos app with five tabs labelled. Each tab briefly highlighted.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Photos tabs: Library · For You · Albums · Search · Shared

**SEGMENT 2: Creating an Album**
[VOICEOVER]: "To create a new album, tap the Albums tab, then tap the plus (+) icon in the top-left corner. Tap 'New Album.' Give your album a name — like 'Christmas 2025' or 'Garden Photos' — and tap Save. You'll then be shown your photo library and asked to select photos to add. Tap each photo you want to include. After selecting, tap Add or Done. Your new album appears under My Albums. To add more photos to it later, open the album, tap the plus icon, and select more."
[VISUAL CUE]: Albums tab → plus icon → New Album → named → photos selected → album created and shown.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Albums → + icon → New Album → name it → select photos → Add

**SEGMENT 3: The Memories Feature**
[VOICEOVER]: "Tap the For You tab. You'll see Memories — these are automatically created slideshows that your iPad builds from your photos, organised around events, places, or dates. You might see 'This Time Last Year' or 'Best of Summer' — your iPad recognises where and when photos were taken and creates a short movie from them. Tap any Memory to watch it. You can save it as a video by tapping the share icon, or edit the music and length by tapping Edit. These make wonderful gifts to share with family."
[VISUAL CUE]: For You tab → Memories shown → one tapped → Memory video playing with music. Edit option demonstrated.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: For You → Memories → tap to watch · Edit music and length · Share as a video

**SEGMENT 4: Searching Your Photos by Content**
[VOICEOVER]: "This is genuinely magical: you can search your photos by what's in them. Tap the Search tab, type 'dog' and it finds all photos of dogs. Type 'birthday' and it finds cake and celebration photos. Type 'Ottawa' and it finds photos taken there. Type a person's name if you've labelled them, and it finds every photo with that person. This works because your iPad uses on-device artificial intelligence to analyse every photo you take — and it does this completely privately, without sending your photos anywhere."
[VISUAL CUE]: Search tab → "birthday" typed → results showing party photos. Then "Ottawa" typed → location-based results.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Search your photos by content: type "birthday," "dog," a place, or a person's name

**SEGMENT 5: Shared Albums — Creating a Family Album**
[VOICEOVER]: "Shared Albums let you create a photo album that multiple Apple ID users can all view and add to — perfect for a family album that grandchildren, children, and you all contribute to. Tap the Shared tab, then the plus icon, create a name like 'Family Photos 2025,' and add people by their Apple ID email addresses. They'll receive an invitation. Once they accept, everyone can add photos and comment on them — like a private family social media feed, just for photos."
[VISUAL CUE]: Shared tab → plus icon → album named → email addresses entered → invitation sent → shared album shown with photos from multiple contributors.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Shared Albums: + icon → name album → invite family by email → everyone adds photos

---

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Two organising pitfalls. First: creating duplicate albums for the same event because you forgot the first one was already there. Before creating a new album, scroll through Albums to see what's already there. Second: confusing iCloud Photos with regular albums. If you have iCloud Photos turned on, all your photos are backed up automatically to the cloud — your albums are really just viewing filters, not separate storage locations. Deleting a photo from the Library deletes it from all albums and from iCloud."
[VISUAL CUE]: Duplicate album graphic. iCloud Photos vs. album distinction explained with simple graphic.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Deleting from Library removes the photo everywhere — from albums and iCloud

---

### RECAP (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Today's key points. One: create albums in the Albums tab with the plus icon. Two: For You shows automatic Memories slideshows — shareable as videos. Three: Search finds photos by content — people, places, things. Four: Shared Albums let your whole family contribute to one collection."
[VISUAL CUE]: Four-point recap list.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Albums → Memories → Search by content → Shared Albums for family

---

## Video 3 of 3: Sharing Photos With Family
**Target Duration:** 7 minutes
**Difficulty:** Beginner

### HOOK (First 15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "A photo of a grandchild's first steps, a snapshot from a family gathering, a picture of your garden in bloom — these moments deserve to be shared. Today we'll cover every way to share your photos from your iPad, and how to make sure those precious memories are safely backed up."

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "In this final video of Module 7, we'll cover sharing individual photos by email or message, how to print photos directly from your iPad using a compatible printer, and how to set up iCloud Photos for automatic backup so your memories are never lost."
[VISUAL CUE]: Share icon on a photo. Title card.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 7 · Video 3 of 3: Sharing Photos With Family

---

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: The Share Button**
[VOICEOVER]: "When you're viewing any photo in full screen, look for the Share button — it's a square with an arrow pointing upward, usually in the bottom-left corner. Tap it and a panel slides up from the bottom of the screen showing all the ways you can share that photo. The top row shows recent contacts you might want to share with. Below that are app options — Messages, Mail, and others. The bottom section has more options like Save to Files, Print, and Copy."
[VISUAL CUE]: Photo in full screen. Share button (box with arrow) highlighted and tapped. Share panel sliding up.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Share button = box with upward arrow · Tap for all sharing options

**SEGMENT 2: Sharing by Message or Email**
[VOICEOVER]: "To send a photo by Messages, tap the share button, then tap the Messages icon. A new message will open with the photo already attached — just add the recipient and optionally a caption, then send. For email, tap the Mail icon instead. The photo will be attached to a new email. Enter the recipient's address and subject. Note: if you're sending to someone who doesn't have an Apple device, email or regular SMS messaging will work better than iMessage. For multiple photos, select them all in the library first before tapping Share."
[VISUAL CUE]: Share → Messages → photo in message → recipient added → sent. Then share → Mail → email with photo attached.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Share → Messages or Mail → add recipient → send · Select multiple photos before sharing

**SEGMENT 3: Printing a Photo From Your iPad**
[VOICEOVER]: "Your iPad can print photos wirelessly to any AirPrint-compatible printer on the same Wi-Fi network. Tap the share button, scroll down, and tap Print. You'll be asked to choose a printer — tap Select Printer and your iPad will search for compatible printers on your Wi-Fi. Select yours, choose how many copies and which paper size, then tap Print. Most home printers sold in the last 5 to 10 years support AirPrint. If printing for a photo album or as a gift, you can also order prints through apps like Shutterfly or Nations Photo Lab, both available in the App Store."
[VISUAL CUE]: Share → Print → Select Printer → printer selected → Print tapped. Printed photo emerging from printer graphic.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Share → Print → Select Printer (AirPrint) → choose copies → Print

**SEGMENT 4: Setting Up iCloud Photos Backup**
[VOICEOVER]: "This is the most important thing in this video: making sure your photos are backed up. If your iPad is lost, stolen, or broken, you want to know that every photo is safe. iCloud Photos automatically backs up every photo you take to your iCloud account over Wi-Fi. To turn it on, go to Settings, tap your name, then iCloud, then Photos, and make sure 'Sync this iPad' is turned on. Your Apple ID comes with 5 gigabytes of free storage — enough for thousands of photos. If you have very many photos, you may need to upgrade to iCloud+ for a small monthly fee."
[VISUAL CUE]: Settings → Apple ID → iCloud → Photos → Sync this iPad toggle on.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Settings → your name → iCloud → Photos → Sync this iPad → turn on

**SEGMENT 5: Checking Your iCloud Storage**
[VOICEOVER]: "To check how much iCloud storage you've used, go to Settings, tap your name, then iCloud. You'll see a bar showing how much of your storage is used and what's using it. If it says you're almost full, tap Manage Storage to see your options. Upgrading to iCloud+ 50GB costs about $1.29 per month in Canada — less than a cup of coffee. This gives you plenty of room for thousands of photos and videos. If you'd rather not pay, you can also back up photos to a computer or an external hard drive using the Photos app on a Mac."
[VISUAL CUE]: Settings → Apple ID → iCloud → storage bar shown → Manage Storage option.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Settings → your name → iCloud → storage bar shows how much you've used

---

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Two sharing mistakes. First: sharing a photo by copying it and then pasting it in a message without the context of who it came from — some people find this confusing. Using the share button directly keeps everything clean and clear. Second: assuming iCloud Photos means the photos are only in the cloud, not on your iPad. With iCloud Photos on, every photo is both on your device AND in the cloud — you have two copies. Deleting from your device will also delete from iCloud, so be intentional about what you delete."
[VISUAL CUE]: iCloud Photos as two copies graphic — iPad and cloud both have the photo.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: iCloud Photos = a copy on your iPad AND a copy in iCloud — delete intentionally

---

### RECAP (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Today's key points. One: the share button is the box with an upward arrow — tap it to see all sharing options. Two: share by Messages or Mail directly from the share panel. Three: print wirelessly using AirPrint via the share panel. Four: turn on iCloud Photos in Settings for automatic backup. Your memories are safe."
[VISUAL CUE]: Four-point recap list.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Share button → Messages/Mail/Print · iCloud Photos = automatic backup
