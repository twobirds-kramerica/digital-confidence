## Video 1 of 3: How Ride-Sharing Works
**Target Duration:** 6 minutes
**Difficulty:** Beginner

### HOOK (First 15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Imagine being able to get a safe, reliable ride to your doctor's appointment, to visit a friend across town, or to the airport — all from your phone, without calling a taxi company and waiting to find out if anyone is available. Ride-sharing apps have changed transportation for millions of people, and they're genuinely straightforward once you know how they work."

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Welcome to Module 11 of the Digital Confidence Centre. This module covers ride-sharing apps — primarily Uber, which is the most widely available in Ontario cities. In this first video, we'll look at how ride-sharing works, how your safety is protected, and how it compares to a traditional taxi."
[VISUAL CUE]: Uber app icon. Car with location pin graphic. Title card.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 11 · Video 1 of 3: How Ride-Sharing Works

---

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: What Is a Ride-Sharing App?**
[VOICEOVER]: "A ride-sharing app connects you — the passenger — with a nearby driver who uses their own car. You open the app, enter your destination, and the app finds the closest available driver. Your payment is handled automatically through the app — no cash changes hands, no waiting for change. The app also shows you the driver's name, photo, and licence plate before they arrive, so you know exactly who to look for."
[VISUAL CUE]: Graphic showing passenger, app, and driver connected by lines. Driver profile shown with name, photo, plate number.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: App connects you to a nearby driver · payment automatic · driver details shown before arrival

**SEGMENT 2: Safety Features Built Into Ride-Sharing**
[VOICEOVER]: "Ride-sharing apps have several safety features that traditional taxis don't always offer. First, every trip is tracked by GPS — the route is recorded and accessible to you and to Uber's safety team. Second, before you get in, verify the driver's name, car colour, and licence plate match what the app shows you — ask the driver 'What's my name?' rather than announcing it yourself. Third, you can share your trip live with a family member — they see exactly where you are and when you'll arrive. Fourth, every driver is rated by passengers after every trip — consistently low-rated drivers are removed."
[VISUAL CUE]: Four safety features shown as icons: GPS tracking, driver verification, share trip live, driver ratings.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Before getting in: check driver name + car colour + licence plate · Ask "what's my name?"

**SEGMENT 3: How Pricing Works — Surge and Upfront Fares**
[VOICEOVER]: "Before you confirm a ride, the app shows you the exact price you'll pay — this is called an upfront fare. You know the cost before you commit. Prices fluctuate based on demand — during busy times like a Friday evening or after a major event, prices go up. This is called surge pricing and the app will clearly show you the higher price. If the price seems high, you can wait a few minutes and request again when demand decreases. The app will also offer different options — UberX is the standard, UberXL fits larger groups, Uber Comfort has more leg room."
[VISUAL CUE]: Uber fare screen shown with price clearly visible. Surge pricing indicator shown. Ride type options: UberX, UberXL, Comfort.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Price shown upfront before you confirm · Surge = busy time pricing · Wait or accept

**SEGMENT 4: After the Ride — Rating and Tipping**
[VOICEOVER]: "After your ride ends, the app will ask you to rate your driver on a scale of one to five stars. Please take a moment to do this — driver ratings affect their livelihood and help maintain quality for everyone. You'll also be offered the option to add a tip through the app — this is separate from the fare and goes entirely to the driver. A tip of a dollar or two for a short trip, or a few dollars for a longer one, is appreciated. You can also tip in cash if you prefer."
[VISUAL CUE]: Post-ride rating screen with star selector. Tip options shown (no tip, $1, $2, $5, custom).
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: After your ride: rate your driver · Tip through the app or in cash — appreciated always

**SEGMENT 5: Uber vs Traditional Taxi in Ontario**
[VOICEOVER]: "Both Uber and traditional taxis are legitimate and regulated options in Ontario. Traditional taxi companies — Beck Taxi in Toronto, London Taxi in London, and similar — have phone and app booking options. Some seniors prefer taxis because they're familiar, sometimes because drivers are more local, and some municipalities outside major cities have more taxi coverage than Uber. The key difference: Uber is almost always available immediately through the app; taxis may require a phone call and may have a longer wait. In smaller communities, Lyft and Uber availability may be limited — check your local options."
[VISUAL CUE]: Map showing Uber coverage (larger cities highlighted) vs taxi coverage (more local icons). Side-by-side comparison table.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Uber: immediate app booking · Taxi: phone or app, more familiar · Smaller cities: check local options

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Two things that cause confusion. First: getting into the wrong car. Always verify the driver's name, car colour and model, and licence plate against what the app shows. If anything doesn't match, do not get in. Second: cancelling after the driver has already started driving toward you — this may incur a small cancellation fee and is unfair to the driver who has already committed their time. If you need to cancel, do so within two minutes of booking."
[VISUAL CUE]: Driver verification checklist: name, car colour, licence plate — all matched. Cancellation fee warning.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Verify driver details before getting in · Cancel within 2 minutes to avoid a fee

---

Three: your trip is GPS-tracked and you can share your live location with family. Four: rate your driver after every ride."
[VISUAL CUE]: Four-point recap list.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Upfront price · Verify driver · Share trip live · Rate after

## Video 2 of 3: Your First Uber Ride — Complete Walkthrough
**Target Duration:** 8 minutes
**Difficulty:** Beginner

### HOOK (First 15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "The first time you book an Uber is the hardest — after that, you'll wonder how you ever managed without it. Today we're going to walk through every single step together, from opening the app to arriving safely at your destination."

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "In this video, we'll do a complete, step-by-step walkthrough of booking an Uber: downloading the app, creating an account, entering your destination, choosing your ride type, watching your driver arrive, and getting to your destination. Take your time with each step — there's no rush."
[VISUAL CUE]: Uber app icon. Phone with Uber map open. Title card.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 11 · Video 2 of 3: Your First Uber Ride

---

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: Downloading Uber and Creating an Account**
[VOICEOVER]: "Search for 'Uber' in the App Store — the official app is by 'Uber Technologies, Inc.' Download it for free. Open the app and tap 'Get Started.' Enter your mobile phone number — Uber will send you a text with a verification code to confirm it's really you. Enter the code, then create your profile with your name and email address. Next, add a payment method — your credit or debit card, or you can link PayPal. Once set up, this information is saved and you won't need to enter it again."
[VISUAL CUE]: App Store → Uber download → Get Started → phone number → verification code → profile → payment method.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Uber setup: phone number → verification code → name and email → add payment method

**SEGMENT 2: Setting Your Pickup Location**
[VOICEOVER]: "When you open Uber, it shows a map centred on your current location — determined by your iPad's GPS. At the bottom, there's a search field that says 'Where to?' Tap it. First, confirm your pickup location — the blue dot on the map should be at your current location. If it's not quite right, tap the 'Edit Pickup' option and drag the pin to your exact spot, or type your address. Your pickup location is where the driver will come to collect you — be specific, especially in busy areas."
[VISUAL CUE]: Uber map shown. Blue dot at current location. "Where to?" tapped. Pickup location confirmed or adjusted.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Blue dot = your location · Tap "Where to?" · Confirm your pickup address first

**SEGMENT 3: Entering Your Destination**
[VOICEOVER]: "After confirming pickup, type your destination in the search field. Start typing the address — Uber will suggest options as you type. Tap the correct address from the suggestions. If you're going somewhere well-known — like St. Thomas Elgin General Hospital or your local Tim Hortons — you can type the name rather than the full address. The app will show you the route on the map and the upfront price. Review both before proceeding."
[VISUAL CUE]: Destination typing → suggestions appearing → correct address tapped → route shown on map → price displayed.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Type destination → select from suggestions → review route and price on map

**SEGMENT 4: Choosing Your Ride and Confirming**
[VOICEOVER]: "Below the price, you'll see ride type options. UberX is the standard option — fits up to 4 passengers, most cost-effective. UberXL is a larger vehicle for 5 or more people or lots of luggage. Uber Comfort has more legroom and a quieter ride. Select UberX for most trips. Then tap the large button at the bottom — usually says the price or 'Request UberX.' Your request goes to nearby drivers. The app will show you 'Looking for a driver...' for usually 30 to 60 seconds."
[VISUAL CUE]: Ride type selection → UberX chosen → Request button tapped → searching animation.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: UberX = standard, most affordable · Tap the price button to confirm your request

**SEGMENT 5: Watching Your Driver Arrive and the Ride**
[VOICEOVER]: "Once a driver accepts, you'll see their name, photo, car colour, make, model, and licence plate on your screen. You'll also see their rating and an estimated arrival time. The map shows their car moving toward you in real time. When they're close, you'll receive a notification. Go to your pickup spot and look for the car matching the details on your screen. Verify the licence plate, greet the driver, and confirm your destination. During the ride, you can track the route on your phone. When you arrive, the fare is automatically charged to your payment method."
[VISUAL CUE]: Driver details screen → car moving on map → driver arrives → licence plate verified → in-car ride view → arrival and automatic payment.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Driver arrives: check name + car + licence plate · Payment automatic on arrival — no cash needed

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Two first-ride pitfalls. First: not being at the correct pickup spot when the driver arrives. Uber drivers typically wait 2 to 5 minutes before the app allows them to cancel — if you're delayed, tap the driver's name in the app to see a phone or message icon and let them know you're coming. Second: entering the destination before you're ready to request. You can build your trip in advance, but don't tap 'Request' until you're actually ready to be picked up — drivers will arrive promptly."
[VISUAL CUE]: Driver waiting → passenger contact button in app. Request button and timing visual.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Running late? Tap driver's name → message or call them from the app

[VISUAL CUE]: Five-point step summary.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Setup once → confirm pickup → enter destination → Request UberX → verify driver → arrive automatically charged

## Video 3 of 3: Accessibility Features and Ride Alternatives
**Target Duration:** 7 minutes
**Difficulty:** Beginner

### HOOK (First 15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Getting around shouldn't require perfect mobility, a driver's licence, or living in a big city. Today we'll look at the accessibility features built into ride apps, alternative transportation services, and what to do if something goes wrong with a ride."

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "In this final video for Module 11, we'll cover Uber's accessibility features for passengers with mobility or vision needs, alternatives to Uber for smaller Ontario communities, community transportation programs, and how to report a problem with a ride."
[VISUAL CUE]: Accessibility icon. Title card.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 11 · Video 3 of 3: Accessibility and Alternatives

---

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: Uber Accessibility — WAV and Assist**
[VOICEOVER]: "Uber offers two accessibility options in many Ontario cities. Uber WAV — Wheelchair Accessible Vehicle — connects you with drivers who have vehicles equipped to accommodate wheelchairs and scooters. To request it, tap the ride type options and scroll to find Uber WAV. In some cities, Uber Assist is available — drivers who have completed training in helping passengers with accessibility needs get in and out of the vehicle. If WAV or Assist aren't available in your area, the app will tell you — and we'll cover alternatives in a moment."
[VISUAL CUE]: Uber ride type menu scrolled to show WAV option. Accessibility icons shown.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Uber WAV: wheelchair accessible vehicles · Uber Assist: trained drivers · Check availability in your city

**SEGMENT 2: Telling Your Driver About Your Needs**
[VOICEOVER]: "If you have mobility, vision, or hearing needs, you can communicate these to your driver through the app before they arrive. Once a driver is matched, tap their name, then the message icon to send a text. For example: 'I use a cane and need a moment to get settled before we leave.' Most drivers are genuinely happy to accommodate — and leaving a note means they're prepared rather than surprised. You can also contact Uber's accessibility support team through the Help section of the app."
[VISUAL CUE]: Driver matched → message icon tapped → note typed and sent. Help section → accessibility support shown.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Message your driver after matching · Help → Accessibility → contact accessibility support

**SEGMENT 3: Alternatives in Smaller Ontario Communities**
[VOICEOVER]: "Uber and Lyft are not available everywhere in Ontario. In smaller cities and rural areas, several alternatives exist. Many Ontario municipalities have community transportation or seniors' transportation programs — contact your local municipality or call 211 (Ontario's community and social services helpline) to find out what's available in your area. Belleville, St. Thomas, Woodstock, and many other cities have local taxi companies with phone or app booking. Some communities have volunteer driver programs coordinated through community centres or churches."
[VISUAL CUE]: Map showing smaller Ontario cities. "211" helpline number displayed prominently. Local taxi graphic.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Dial 211 for Ontario community transportation services in your area

**SEGMENT 4: Wheel-Trans and Accessible Transit**
[VOICEOVER]: "For Toronto residents, TTC Wheel-Trans is a door-to-door accessible transit service for registered passengers with physical, cognitive, or mental health disabilities. Registration is done through the TTC website or by phone. Many other Ontario transit authorities — Hamilton, Ottawa, London — have similar paratransit services. These are designed for regular use by people with disabilities and are generally more affordable than ride-sharing for multiple trips. Check your local transit authority's website for eligibility and how to register."
[VISUAL CUE]: TTC Wheel-Trans logo. List of Ontario cities with paratransit services.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Paratransit services: Toronto Wheel-Trans · Ottawa Paratranspo · Hamilton DARTS · London Transit

**SEGMENT 5: Reporting a Problem With a Ride**
[VOICEOVER]: "If something goes wrong — the driver was rude, the route was significantly wrong, you were overcharged, or you feel unsafe — report it through the app. Open Uber, tap the menu, go to Your Trips, select the affected trip, and tap Help. Choose the most relevant issue from the options. Uber's support team responds by email, usually within 24 hours. For serious safety incidents, there's an emergency option right in the Help menu. You can also report issues via the web at help.uber.com."
[VISUAL CUE]: Uber menu → Your Trips → select trip → Help → issue categories shown.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Problem with a ride: menu → Your Trips → Help → report the issue

---

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "One important thing to be aware of: sharing your Uber account login with a family member so they can book rides for you is convenient, but be cautious. Their payment method and rating history will be attached to your account. A better approach is to use Uber's Family Profiles feature — this lets a family member set up and manage rides for you from their own account, with the trip appearing on your Uber account and charged to their payment method. Ask a family member to help you set this up."
[VISUAL CUE]: Uber Family Profiles option shown in Account settings. How the booking-for-someone-else flow works.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Family member can book rides for you using Uber Family Profiles — ask them to set it up
