## Video 1 of 3: Getting Started With Facebook
**Target Duration:** 8 minutes
**Difficulty:** Beginner

### HOOK (First 15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Over 2 billion people use Facebook — including a remarkable number of people over 65 who use it to stay connected with family, keep up with local events, and find their communities. Whether you're brand new or you've had an account for years but never felt comfortable with it, today we'll get you grounded in the essentials."

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Welcome to Module 13 of the Digital Confidence Centre — Social Media. This first video covers Facebook: what it is, how to set up a profile, how to connect with family and friends, and — very importantly — how to manage your privacy so you share what you want to share, with who you want to share it."
[VISUAL CUE]: Facebook app icon. Title card.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 13 · Video 1 of 3: Getting Started With Facebook

---

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: What Facebook Is For**
[VOICEOVER]: "Facebook is a social network — a place where you create a profile, connect with people you know, and share updates, photos, and messages. Think of it like a community bulletin board combined with a personal scrapbook that you can share selectively with family and friends. You can post a photo of your garden, write a short message, see what your grandchildren are up to, follow your favourite local organisations, and join groups of people who share your interests — whether that's gardening, crafts, local news, or your church community."
[VISUAL CUE]: Facebook home feed shown with family updates, a local events page, and a community group. Simple, warm visual.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Facebook: connect with family · share updates and photos · follow local organisations · join interest groups

**SEGMENT 2: Creating a Facebook Account**
[VOICEOVER]: "Download the Facebook app from the App Store. Tap 'Create New Account.' Enter your name — use your real name so family and friends can find you. Enter your email address or phone number, create a password, add your date of birth — this is kept private unless you choose to share it — and select your gender. Facebook will then suggest finding friends who already have accounts. You can do this step now or skip it and come back later. Once your account is created, you'll be taken to your new profile."
[VISUAL CUE]: App Store → Facebook → Create Account → step-by-step form screens.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Create account: name → email → password → date of birth → done

**SEGMENT 3: Privacy Settings — The Most Important First Step**
[VOICEOVER]: "Before anything else, let's set your privacy. Tap the menu icon — three horizontal lines — in the bottom-right corner of the Facebook app. Scroll down and tap Settings & Privacy, then Settings. Scroll to the Audience and Visibility section. Under 'Who can see what you share,' set your future posts default to 'Friends' — not 'Public.' 'Public' means anyone in the world can see your posts. 'Friends' means only people you've accepted as friends can see them. This single setting is the most important privacy choice you'll make on Facebook."
[VISUAL CUE]: Settings path → Audience and Visibility → future posts → 'Friends' selected, not 'Public.'
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Privacy: Settings → future posts → set to "Friends" not "Public" — do this first

**SEGMENT 4: Adding Friends and Family**
[VOICEOVER]: "To find people you know on Facebook, tap the search icon — the magnifying glass — and type their name. If you find the right person, tap their name and then tap 'Add Friend.' They'll receive a notification and can accept or decline. Once accepted, you'll be able to see each other's posts and message each other privately. You can also tap People You May Know on the home screen — Facebook suggests people based on your contacts and mutual friends. Only add people you actually know in real life."
[VISUAL CUE]: Search bar → name typed → profile found → Add Friend tapped → pending notification.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Search name → Add Friend → they accept → you can see each other's posts

**SEGMENT 5: Your News Feed and Posting an Update**
[VOICEOVER]: "Your home feed — the scrolling list when you open Facebook — shows posts from your friends and pages you follow, most recent at the top. Scroll down to read through them. To post something yourself, tap 'What's on your mind?' at the top of the feed. A text box opens — type your message. You can also tap the photo icon below the text to add a photo. Before tapping Post, check the audience button below your name — it should say 'Friends' if you set your default correctly. Tap Post when you're ready."
[VISUAL CUE]: News feed scrolling. "What's on your mind?" tapped → text entered → photo added → audience showing "Friends" → Post tapped.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Tap "What's on your mind?" → type → add photo → check audience = Friends → Post

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Two Facebook mistakes that are very common. First: accepting friend requests from people you don't know. Scammers create fake profiles and send friend requests specifically to older adults — once you're 'friends' they can message you privately with scams or see personal information in your profile. If you don't recognise someone, decline the request. Second: posting information you'd rather not make public. Before every post, check the audience setting. It's easy to accidentally post to 'Public' if the setting didn't default correctly."
[VISUAL CUE]: Unknown person's friend request — Decline option highlighted. Post audience setting — Friends vs Public comparison.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Don't recognise the person? Decline the friend request · Always check audience before posting

---

### RECAP (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Today's key points. One: set future posts to 'Friends' in Privacy Settings before anything else. Two: use search to find and add friends and family. Three: your news feed shows friends' posts. Four: 'What's on your mind?' is how you post — always check the audience setting."
[VISUAL CUE]: Four-point recap list.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Privacy first → add friends you know → read news feed → post with Friends audience

## Video 2 of 3: Instagram — Sharing and Enjoying Photos
**Target Duration:** 7 minutes
**Difficulty:** Beginner

### HOOK (First 15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Instagram is where your grandchildren are most likely posting photos of their lives. It's a beautifully visual platform — mostly photos and short videos — and once you know your way around it, following the people you love feels like looking into their world every day."

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "In this video, we'll cover Instagram — how it differs from Facebook, how to create an account and follow family members, and how to share your own photos. Instagram is owned by the same company as Facebook, but it has a different feel — more visual, less text-heavy, and used heavily by younger generations."
[VISUAL CUE]: Instagram app icon. Beautiful photo grid. Title card.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 13 · Video 2 of 3: Instagram — Sharing Photos

---

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: What Makes Instagram Different From Facebook**
[VOICEOVER]: "Instagram is built entirely around photos and short videos. There's no news feed of text updates — just a feed of images from people you follow. Instagram uses 'following' rather than 'friends' — you can follow someone without them following you back. Most people's accounts are public, meaning anyone can see their posts, but you can set your account to private so only approved followers see your content. Instagram is popular for sharing travel photos, food, family moments, gardens, crafts, and visual hobbies."
[VISUAL CUE]: Instagram feed shown — grid of beautiful photos. 'Following' vs. 'Friends' distinction shown.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Instagram: photos and videos only · Following = one-way · Set to private for family-only sharing

**SEGMENT 2: Creating an Instagram Account**
[VOICEOVER]: "Download Instagram from the App Store — it's free. Tap 'Create New Account.' You can sign up with your email address or link it to your Facebook account if you have one. Choose a username — this is how people find you, so something simple and recognisable works well, like your name or initials. Add a profile photo if you like — this can be a photo of yourself, your garden, or anything you want. Set your account to Private in the Account Privacy settings before you start following people."
[VISUAL CUE]: App Store → Instagram → Create Account → username choice → profile photo → account privacy set to private.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Instagram setup: username → profile photo → set account to Private before following

**SEGMENT 3: Finding and Following Family Members**
[VOICEOVER]: "Tap the search icon — the magnifying glass at the bottom. Type a family member's name or username. When you find them, tap 'Follow.' If their account is public, you'll immediately see their posts in your feed. If their account is private, they'll receive a follow request — once they approve it, you'll see their posts. Your family members can also find and follow you. Ask them to share their Instagram username with you if you're unsure what to search."
[VISUAL CUE]: Search bar → name typed → profile found → Follow tapped. Follow request sent for private account.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Search name → Follow · For private accounts: request → they approve → you see their posts

**SEGMENT 4: Your Instagram Feed and Interacting With Posts**
[VOICEOVER]: "Your Instagram feed shows the most recent posts from people you follow. Scroll down to see them. To like a post, tap the heart icon below the photo. To leave a comment, tap the speech bubble icon and type your message. To send a private message about a post, tap the paper airplane icon. You can also double-tap the photo itself to quickly like it. When someone likes or comments on your posts, you'll see a notification with a heart icon at the bottom of the screen."
[VISUAL CUE]: Instagram feed → like button tapped → comment typed → direct message sent. Double-tap to like demonstrated.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Like = heart icon · Comment = speech bubble · Double tap photo = quick like

**SEGMENT 5: Sharing Your Own Photo on Instagram**
[VOICEOVER]: "To share a photo, tap the plus icon at the bottom of the screen. Choose 'Post.' Select a photo from your library — tap it to select it. Crop or adjust if needed — or just tap Next. Add a caption describing the photo. You can also add a location or tag people by their username. Tap 'Share.' Your photo will appear in your followers' feeds and on your profile grid. If your account is private, only your approved followers will see it."
[VISUAL CUE]: Plus icon → Post → photo library → photo selected → caption typed → Share tapped → photo on profile grid.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: + icon → Post → choose photo → caption → Share · Private account = only followers see it

---

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Two Instagram mistakes. First: starting with a public account and not realising strangers can see everything. Set your account to Private in settings before your first post. Go to Settings — the gear or menu icon on your profile — then Privacy, then Account Privacy, and turn on 'Private Account.' Second: engaging with sponsored posts thinking they're from friends. Posts labelled 'Sponsored' are advertisements — not posts from people you follow. You can tap the three dots on a sponsored post and choose 'Hide Ad' to see fewer of that type."
[VISUAL CUE]: Privacy → Account Privacy → Private Account toggle. Sponsored post label shown with 'Hide Ad' option.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Settings → Privacy → Account Privacy → turn on Private Account · "Sponsored" = advertisement

[VISUAL CUE]: Four-point recap list.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Set to Private → search and follow → like + comment → + to post your own photos

## Video 3 of 3: Facebook Marketplace — Buying and Selling Locally
**Target Duration:** 7 minutes
**Difficulty:** Intermediate

### HOOK (First 15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Facebook Marketplace has become one of the most popular places to buy and sell second-hand items locally — furniture, gardening tools, books, crafts, clothing. It's like a digital garage sale where you can browse thousands of items near you from the comfort of your home. Today we'll show you how to use it safely."

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Facebook Marketplace is a feature within the Facebook app where local people buy and sell used goods. In this video, we'll cover how to browse and buy items safely, how to list something for sale, and the safety rules that make Marketplace transactions go smoothly."
[VISUAL CUE]: Facebook Marketplace icon — shop-front symbol. Title card.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 13 · Video 3 of 3: Facebook Marketplace

---

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: Finding the Marketplace**
[VOICEOVER]: "Marketplace is accessed from within the Facebook app. Look for the shop-front icon — two vertical rectangles, looks like a store building — in the bottom row of icons. Tap it to open Marketplace. The home screen shows items near your location. Use the search bar to find specific things — 'rocking chair,' 'bread maker,' 'garden hose.' Use the Filters option to set a maximum price, distance from your home, or category. Items listed in your area appear with photos, prices, and the seller's first name."
[VISUAL CUE]: Facebook app → Marketplace icon → search → filter panel → local listings shown.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Facebook → shop icon → Marketplace → search or browse by category and location

**SEGMENT 2: Evaluating a Listing Before Contacting a Seller**
[VOICEOVER]: "Before contacting a seller, look at their profile. A trustworthy seller profile typically has: a real-looking name, a profile photo, joined Facebook more than a few months ago, some posts or activity visible, and positive feedback from previous buyers. Listings with many clear photos are better than those with one blurry image. If a price seems unbelievably low for the item — a $40 leather couch when similar ones are $300 — be cautious. And never pay in advance through an app or wire transfer for something you haven't seen in person."
[VISUAL CUE]: Seller profile evaluation — each element highlighted: profile photo, join date, feedback, post history. Price comparison graphic.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Check seller: real profile · joined months ago · positive feedback · prices that make sense

**SEGMENT 3: Messaging a Seller and Arranging Pick-Up**
[VOICEOVER]: "To express interest, tap the item and then tap 'Send Message' or 'Is this available?' Facebook opens a private message to the seller. Keep your messages simple: 'Hi, I'm interested in the bread maker. Is it still available, and can I pick it up on Saturday?' Arrange to pick up in a public, well-lit location — a Tim Hortons parking lot, a community centre, or a police station parking lot, which many police services in Ontario designate as official safe trade zones. Never invite a stranger to your home for a first Marketplace transaction."
[VISUAL CUE]: Marketplace message being sent. Safe trade location graphic — Tim Hortons, community centre, police station.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Meet in a public place · Ontario police services have Safe Trade Zones · Never meet alone at home

**SEGMENT 4: Selling an Item on Marketplace**
[VOICEOVER]: "To sell something, tap Marketplace, then the plus (+) icon or 'Create New Listing.' Choose 'Item for Sale.' Take several clear, well-lit photos of the item from different angles. Write an accurate title — 'Bread Maker, barely used.' Set a fair price. Add a description including the condition, dimensions if relevant, and any relevant details. Choose your location. Tap Publish. Your listing will appear in local searches. Potential buyers will message you — respond promptly and arrange a safe pick-up location."
[VISUAL CUE]: Create listing → photos taken → title and price → description → Publish → listing live.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: New Listing → clear photos → honest title and description → fair price → Publish

**SEGMENT 5: Payment — What's Safe and What Isn't**
[VOICEOVER]: "For Marketplace transactions, cash is the safest payment for in-person pick-ups — you inspect the item, you pay, done. Interac e-Transfer is acceptable if you know and trust the person, but confirm it's received before handing over the item. What to avoid: Facebook Pay for strangers you haven't met, cryptocurrency, wire transfers, and mailed cheques. Scammers on Marketplace often ask to ship the item and pay in advance, or claim to send more money than the price and ask for change — both classic scam patterns."
[VISUAL CUE]: Payment options shown: cash = green checkmark, e-transfer = acceptable, wire/crypto = red X. Overpayment scam graphic with red X.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Marketplace payment: cash is safest · Never accept overpayment · No wire transfers or crypto

---

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Two Marketplace scams to know. First: the 'I'll buy it but I need to ship it' message, followed by a fake PayPal or Interac notification showing payment. The payment is fake. Never release an item until you've confirmed funds in your bank account. Second: the buyer who sends too much money and asks you to send the excess back. The original payment will bounce — and your refund of the 'extra' will be real money out of your pocket. If a transaction seems complicated or unusual, trust your instincts and walk away."
[VISUAL CUE]: Fake payment notification screenshot with red X. Overpayment scam scenario illustrated.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Overpayment request = always a scam · Confirm money in your account before releasing any item

---

### RECAP (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Today's key points. One: access Marketplace from the shop icon in Facebook. Two: check seller profiles before engaging. Three: meet in a public safe trade location — never alone at home. Four: cash is the safest payment for in-person pick-ups. Five: overpayment requests and advance payment for shipping are scams."
[VISUAL CUE]: Five-point recap list.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Shop icon → check seller → public meeting place → cash → no overpayment or advance payment
