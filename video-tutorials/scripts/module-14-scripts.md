### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: What Makes a Device 'Smart'?**
[VOICEOVER]: "A smart home device is one that connects to your home Wi-Fi network and can be controlled from a smartphone or tablet app, rather than just a physical switch or dial. Smart devices can also be automated — set to do things on a schedule, respond to conditions like sunlight or motion, or be controlled by voice through a voice assistant like Siri, Amazon Alexa, or Google Assistant. The key benefit for daily life is convenience and, for heating and cooling, significant energy savings."
[VISUAL CUE]: Traditional thermostat → smart thermostat. Traditional switch → smart bulb. App control and voice control icons.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Smart = connects to Wi-Fi · controlled by app or voice · can be automated on a schedule

**SEGMENT 2: Smart Thermostats — What They Do**
[VOICEOVER]: "A smart thermostat replaces your existing thermostat and connects to your Wi-Fi. You can adjust the temperature from the thermostat itself, from the manufacturer's app on your iPad, or by voice. More importantly, smart thermostats learn your routines over time — they notice when you leave the house, when you return, and when you go to bed, and adjust the temperature automatically. Most people who switch to a smart thermostat save 10 to 20 percent on their heating and cooling bills within a year. The Nest and Ecobee are the most popular options in Canada."
[VISUAL CUE]: Ecobee thermostat shown. App interface with temperature adjustment slider. Energy savings graphic.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Smart thermostat: control by app or voice · learns your routine · saves 10–20% on energy bills

**SEGMENT 3: Controlling a Smart Thermostat From Your iPad**
[VOICEOVER]: "After your thermostat is installed and connected — which a licensed electrician or HVAC technician can do — download the manufacturer's app. For Ecobee, it's the Ecobee app. For Nest, it's the Google Home app. Log in with the account you created during setup. The app shows your current temperature, your setpoint, and your schedule. To adjust the temperature, tap the plus or minus buttons, or drag the temperature dial. Changes take effect within seconds. You can also view your energy usage history and make manual schedule adjustments."
[VISUAL CUE]: Ecobee app → current temperature shown → temperature adjusted by tap → change reflected on thermostat.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Open manufacturer's app → tap to adjust temperature → see energy history

**SEGMENT 4: Smart Lights — Basics**
[VOICEOVER]: "Smart lightbulbs screw into your existing light sockets and connect to your Wi-Fi. Popular brands in Canada include Philips Hue, IKEA TRÅDFRI, and Kasa by TP-Link. Once connected, you control them through an app or by voice. Key features: turn lights on or off from anywhere, dim them to any level, set schedules — like having entrance lights come on at dusk automatically — and some models change colour. Smart lights in hallways or at the top of stairs can be set to turn on automatically when motion is detected at night, which can significantly improve safety."
[VISUAL CUE]: Smart bulb in a standard socket. App interface showing on/off and brightness slider. Motion-triggered hallway light scenario.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Smart lights: app-controlled · dimmable · schedulable · motion-triggered for night safety

**SEGMENT 5: Controlling Smart Lights From Your iPad**
[VOICEOVER]: "After installing smart bulbs, download the app for your brand — Philips Hue app, Kasa app, etc. The app shows all your connected bulbs by room. Tap a bulb or room to turn it on or off. Slide the brightness bar to adjust. Set a schedule by tapping Schedule or Automation — for example, 'turn on at sunset, turn off at 11pm.' In Apple's Home app, all compatible smart home devices are brought together in one place — this is Apple's hub for smart home control across brands, and it works with Siri commands too."
[VISUAL CUE]: Philips Hue app → room selected → lights on/off → brightness adjusted → schedule set. Apple Home app shown as unified option.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Manufacturer app → control lights · Apple Home app brings all smart devices together

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Two smart home pitfalls. First: buying devices that are not compatible with each other or with Apple's Home app if you plan to use Siri. Before buying, check the box or description for 'Works with Apple HomeKit' for Apple compatibility, or 'Works with Alexa' or 'Works with Google Home' for those platforms. Second: relying on smart devices without a manual backup. Smart bulbs that only work through the app can be frustrating if the app or Wi-Fi has an issue. Keep at least one traditional lamp accessible in every room as a backup."
[VISUAL CUE]: HomeKit logo on product packaging. Manual lamp as backup graphic.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Check for HomeKit, Alexa, or Google Home compatibility before buying · Keep a manual lamp as backup

[VISUAL CUE]: Four-point recap list.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Wi-Fi connected · Thermostat saves energy · Smart lights for safety · Apple Home app as central hub

## Video 2 of 3: Smart Doorbells and Home Security
**Target Duration:** 7 minutes
**Difficulty:** Intermediate

### HOOK (First 15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Imagine being inside watching television, your doorbell rings, and from your iPad you can see who's at the front door, talk to them, and even tell a delivery driver where to leave a parcel — without getting up or even going to the door. Smart doorbells make this possible, and they add a genuine layer of home security."

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "In this video, we'll cover smart video doorbells and home security cameras. We'll look at how they work, the most popular options in Canada, how to review footage from your iPad, and the privacy considerations you should be aware of."

[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 14 · Video 2 of 3: Smart Doorbells and Security

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: How Smart Video Doorbells Work**
[VOICEOVER]: "A smart video doorbell replaces or augments your existing doorbell and includes a camera, microphone, and speaker. When someone presses the bell — or even just walks up to your door — you receive a notification on your iPad with live video. Tap the notification to see and talk to whoever is there using your iPad's speaker and microphone. Popular options in Canada include Ring, Nest Doorbell, and Arlo. Most require a monthly subscription for video recording history — typically $4 to $10 per month — though live viewing is usually free."
[VISUAL CUE]: Smart doorbell on front door. Notification received on iPad. Live two-way video call shown.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Smart doorbell: see and speak to visitors from anywhere · Notification → tap → live video

**SEGMENT 2: Setting Up a Ring Doorbell**
[VOICEOVER]: "Ring is one of the most popular and straightforward options. After mounting the device and connecting the wires — Ring provides detailed instructions and many people hire a handyman for this part — download the Ring app from the App Store. Create an account or sign in, tap Set Up a Device, and follow the in-app instructions to connect it to your home Wi-Fi. The app will walk you through testing the camera and audio. You'll also set up motion zones — areas where motion triggers a notification — so passing cars don't alert you constantly."
[VISUAL CUE]: Ring app → Set Up a Device → Wi-Fi connection → motion zone configuration.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Ring app: Set Up Device → connect to Wi-Fi → set motion zones to avoid false alerts

**SEGMENT 3: Reviewing Recorded Footage**
[VOICEOVER]: "With a Ring Protect subscription, every motion or doorbell event is recorded and stored for 60 days. To review footage, open the Ring app and tap the History or Event History tab. You'll see a timeline of all recorded events — doorbell rings, motion detections. Tap any event to watch the clip. This is useful for seeing if a delivery was made while you were out, reviewing activity around your home, or identifying someone who rang when you were unavailable."
[VISUAL CUE]: Ring app → Event History → timeline of events → clip played.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Ring app → Event History → tap any clip to review recordings

**SEGMENT 4: Indoor Security Cameras**
[VOICEOVER]: "Indoor security cameras let you check in on your home when you're away — a kitchen, a main hallway, or wherever you want visibility. The Blink Indoor camera and the Wyze Cam are both affordable Canadian-available options. Setup is similar to Ring — download the app, create an account, plug in the camera, connect to Wi-Fi. Many people use indoor cameras to check on a pet while out, monitor whether medications were taken by a visiting caregiver, or simply to see whether they left a light on."
[VISUAL CUE]: Wyze Cam plugged in. App showing live indoor camera view.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Indoor cameras: Blink or Wyze Cam · check on home when away · plug-in, no installation needed

**SEGMENT 5: Privacy Considerations**
[VOICEOVER]: "Smart cameras come with important privacy responsibilities. In Ontario, you have the right to record your own property, but pointing a camera at a neighbour's property or a public sidewalk where it captures individuals other than those approaching your door may have privacy implications — keep cameras focused on your own property. Be transparent with visitors — a small sign noting video recording is both courteous and, for some surveillance in commercial contexts, legally required. Also review where your video is stored — US-based cloud services may have different privacy laws than Canada."
[VISUAL CUE]: Camera angle diagram — pointing at front door vs angling to neighbour's yard. Small 'Video Monitoring' sign on door.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Point cameras at your own property only · Consider a "Video Monitoring" notice sign · Check where data is stored

---

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Two setup pitfalls. First: mounting the doorbell or camera before fully testing it in its intended location. Some spots have Wi-Fi dead zones. Test the connection before permanently mounting — stand at the location and check Wi-Fi signal strength on your iPad first. Second: not adjusting motion sensitivity. Default settings often result in constant alerts from passing cars, falling leaves, or a cat. Spend a few minutes after setup adjusting motion zones and sensitivity until you get alerts only when someone actually approaches your door."
[VISUAL CUE]: Wi-Fi signal check at doorbell location. Motion sensitivity settings in Ring app.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Test Wi-Fi signal at installation spot · Adjust motion sensitivity to reduce false alerts

Two: Ring is a popular option — set up through the Ring app with motion zones configured. Three: recorded history lets you review footage. Four: point cameras at your own property and consider a monitoring notice."
[VISUAL CUE]: Four-point recap list.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Notification → live doorbell view · Ring app setup · Event history · Camera at own property only

**Target Duration:** 7 minutes

### HOOK (First 15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "What if your home could respond to your voice? Turn on the kitchen lights. Play some music. Tell me the weather. Add eggs to my shopping list. Add a reminder to take my medication at noon. Voice assistants can do all of this and more — and for anyone who finds typing or tapping difficult, they can be genuinely life-changing."

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "In this final video of Module 14, we'll cover the three main voice assistants — Apple's Siri, Amazon's Alexa, and Google Assistant — how they differ, how to set them up, and practical ways to use them in your daily home life."
[VISUAL CUE]: Three voice assistant logos: Siri waveform, Alexa blue ring, Google Assistant. Title card.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 14 · Video 3 of 3: Voice Assistants at Home

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: Siri on Your iPad — Always With You**
[VOICEOVER]: "You already have Siri on your iPad — we covered it in Module 9. For smart home use, Siri works through the Apple Home app to control any HomeKit-compatible device. If you have HomeKit devices, you can say 'Hey Siri, turn off the living room lights' or 'Hey Siri, set the thermostat to 21 degrees.' Siri also handles reminders, timers, music, calls, and questions — all hands-free. Siri on your iPad requires the iPad to be plugged in or recently used for the 'Hey Siri' wake word to work consistently."
[VISUAL CUE]: Siri activated on iPad. Voice command given to control Apple Home devices. Thermostat and lights responding.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Siri + HomeKit: "Hey Siri, turn off the lights" · Siri handles reminders, timers, calls

**SEGMENT 2: Amazon Echo and Alexa — The Smart Home Specialist**
[VOICEOVER]: "Amazon Echo is a standalone smart speaker — a hockey-puck or cylindrical device you plug into any outlet — with Amazon's Alexa voice assistant built in. Alexa is particularly strong for smart home control because it works with a very wide range of devices. Setup: plug in the Echo, download the Alexa app from the App Store, sign in with your Amazon account, and follow the in-app setup. Once connected, just say 'Alexa' followed by your command. You can also control Alexa from the app on your iPad."
[VISUAL CUE]: Amazon Echo device. Alexa app setup flow. Voice command demonstration.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Amazon Echo: plug in + Alexa app setup · "Alexa" then your command · Works with vast range of devices

**SEGMENT 3: Google Nest and Google Assistant**
[VOICEOVER]: "Google Nest Hub and Google Home are Google's smart home speaker products with Google Assistant built in. If you use Google services — Gmail, Google Calendar, Google Photos — Google Assistant integrates well with them. For example: 'Hey Google, show me today's events' on a Nest Hub with a screen will show your Google Calendar. Google Assistant is also available as an app on your iPad. Setup is similar to Alexa — download the Google Home app and follow the instructions."
[VISUAL CUE]: Google Nest Hub with screen showing Google Calendar. Google Home app on iPad.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Google Nest Hub: great if you use Gmail and Google Calendar · Google Home app for setup

**SEGMENT 4: Practical Voice Commands for Daily Life**
[VOICEOVER]: "Here are voice commands that people find especially valuable for daily home life. 'Alexa, set a timer for 20 minutes.' 'Hey Siri, remind me to call the pharmacy at 3pm.' 'Hey Google, add milk and bread to my shopping list.' 'Alexa, turn on the kitchen light.' 'Hey Siri, what's the temperature outside?' 'Alexa, play CBC Radio.' 'Hey Google, how many centimetres in an inch?' 'Alexa, call [person's name] on my contact list.' The key is to phrase commands naturally — these assistants understand conversational language."
[VISUAL CUE]: Each command shown as text as it is spoken. Corresponding response shown briefly.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Timer · Reminder · Shopping list · Lights · Weather · Radio · Measurement · Call — all hands-free

**SEGMENT 5: Privacy and Voice Assistants**
[VOICEOVER]: "Voice assistants listen for their wake word constantly — 'Hey Siri,' 'Alexa,' or 'Hey Google.' This raises privacy questions that are worth knowing about. These devices do record short clips when they detect the wake word, which may be reviewed by company employees to improve accuracy. You can review and delete your voice history: in the Alexa app under Settings > Alexa Privacy, in the Google app under My Activity, and in your iPhone/iPad Settings under Siri & Search > Siri History. You can also mute the microphone physically on Echo and Nest Hub devices using the mute button."
[VISUAL CUE]: Alexa Privacy settings shown. Mute button on Echo device highlighted.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Review your voice history: Alexa app → Alexa Privacy · Physical mute button on Echo device

---

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Two voice assistant frustrations. First: the assistant mishearing the wake word and activating unexpectedly — especially during TV shows. This is normal, and you can simply say 'Alexa, stop' or let it time out. To reduce false activations, the Echo has a sensitivity setting in the Alexa app. Second: assuming any connected smart home device works with your voice assistant. Always check the box for compatibility: 'Works with Alexa,' 'Works with Google Home,' or 'Works with Apple HomeKit' before buying."
[VISUAL CUE]: Echo unexpectedly activating during TV. "Alexa, stop" spoken to dismiss. Compatibility logo check on product box.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Unexpected activation: say "Alexa, stop" · Check product boxes for compatibility logos
