Digital Literacy 101 — Words & Actions

Your safe place to learn

Have you ever heard words like tap, scroll, or icon and felt unsure what they actually mean? You are not alone. Technology has its own language — and nobody was born knowing it.

This page is your friendly reference guide. No rush, no tests, no wrong answers. Just plain English explanations of the words you will hear most often. Come back whenever you need a reminder — this page will always be here.

Tip: You can use the font size buttons at the top to make the text larger.

Common Words Explained

Here are five of the most common words you will see and hear when using a tablet, phone, or computer. Each one is explained simply, without jargon.

📌 Button

What it is: A shape on your screen — usually a rectangle or circle — that you tap to make something happen. Buttons often have words on them like Send, Save, or OK.

Think of it like: A doorbell. You press it once, and something happens on the other side.

Example: When you tap Send to send a text message, you are tapping a button.

👆 Tap

What it is: Touching your screen once with your finger and lifting it quickly. It is the same as clicking with a mouse, but you use your fingertip instead of a mouse.

Think of it like: Tapping a friend on the shoulder — a quick, gentle touch. You do not need to press hard. A light touch is all it takes.

Example: To open an app, you tap its picture (called an icon) on your home screen.

📜 Scroll

What it is: Moving up or down through a long page by dragging your finger on the screen. When a page has more content than fits on the screen at once, you scroll to see the rest.

Think of it like: Unrolling a long piece of paper. You move it up to see more at the bottom, or down to go back to the top.

Example: To read the rest of a news article that goes off the bottom of your screen, you scroll down by slowly dragging your finger upward.

🖼️ Icon

What it is: A small picture that represents an app, file, or action. The little pictures on your home screen — the camera, the phone, the calendar — are all icons. Each icon opens something when you tap it.

Think of it like: Signs on a highway. A picture of a knife and fork means restaurant ahead. A small phone picture on your screen means phone app.

Example: The green square with a white phone receiver on it is the icon for your Phone app.

📋 Menu

What it is: A list of choices that appears when you tap a button or icon. Menus let you pick what you want to do next. You can always close a menu by tapping somewhere else on the screen — nothing will break.

Think of it like: A restaurant menu. You look at the options, pick what you want, and tap it. If you change your mind, you tap somewhere else and the menu closes.

Example: In many apps, tapping the three little lines (☰) in a corner opens a menu with more choices.
