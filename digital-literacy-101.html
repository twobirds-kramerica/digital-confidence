Digital Literacy 101 — Words & Actions

Your safe place to learn

Have you ever heard words like tap, scroll, or icon and felt unsure what they actually mean? You are not alone. Technology has its own language — and nobody was born knowing it.

This page is your friendly reference guide. No rush, no tests, no wrong answers. Just plain English explanations of the words you will hear most often. Come back whenever you need a reminder — this page will always be here.

Tip: You can use the font size buttons at the top to make the text larger.

Common Words Explained

Here are 20 of the most common words you will see and hear when using a tablet, phone, or computer. Each one is explained simply, without jargon. Bookmark this page — it will always be here when you need it.

📌 Button

A shape on your screen — usually a rectangle or oval — that you tap to make something happen. Buttons often have words on them like Send, Save, or OK.

For example: When you want to send a text message, you tap the Send button — that little shape at the end of your message.

🍏 Try it on your iPad: Look for a button that says OK or Done the next time a small message pops up on your screen.

👆 Tap

Touching your screen once with your finger and lifting it quickly. It is the same as a mouse click, but you use your fingertip. You do not need to press hard — a gentle touch is all it takes.

For example: When you want to open the Photos app, you tap its icon once and the app opens.

🍏 Try it on your iPad: Gently tap the Photos app icon on your home screen to open it. Then tap the back arrow (←) to close it again.

👋 Swipe

Moving your finger across the screen in one direction — left, right, up, or down — without lifting it. A swipe is used to flip between photos, dismiss a notification, or go back to the previous screen.

For example: When you want to look through your photos one by one, you swipe left or right to move to the next picture.

🍏 Try it on your iPad: Open the Photos app, tap a photo, then swipe left or right with one finger to see the next or previous photo.

📜 Scroll

Moving up or down through a long page by dragging your finger on the screen. When a page has more content than fits on the screen at once, you scroll to see the rest.

For example: When you want to read the rest of a news article, you scroll down by slowly dragging your finger upward — as if you are pulling the page up toward you.

🍏 Try it on your iPad: Open Safari and visit any news website. Place your finger in the middle of the screen and drag upward slowly to scroll down the page.

🖼️ Icon

A small picture that represents an app, file, or action. The little pictures on your home screen — the camera, the phone, the calendar — are all icons. Tap an icon to open what it represents.

For example: The green square with a white telephone receiver is the icon for the Phone app. The yellow square with a magnifying glass is the icon for Contacts search.

🍏 Try it on your iPad: Look at your home screen. Each small picture you see is an icon. Can you find the icon for Settings? It looks like a grey square with interlocking circles.

📋 Menu

A list of choices that appears when you tap a button or icon. Menus let you pick what you want to do next. You can always close a menu by tapping somewhere else on the screen — nothing will break.

For example: In many apps, tapping the three little lines (☰) in a corner opens a menu with more choices — like the navigation menu on this very website.

🍏 Try it on your iPad: In the Safari browser, tap the Share button (a square with an upward arrow). A menu appears. Tap anywhere outside it to close it safely.

📱 App

Short for "application" — a programme designed to do one specific thing. Your iPad comes with many apps already installed, and you can add more free ones from the App Store. Each app icon on your screen is a different app.

For example: The Maps app shows you where places are. The Weather app tells you the forecast. Each is a separate tool.

🍏 Try it on your iPad: Look for the blue icon with a white compass — that is the Safari app for browsing the internet. Tap it to open it.

🌐 Browser

The app that lets you visit websites on the internet. On an iPhone or iPad, the browser is called Safari (the blue compass icon). Other browsers include Chrome and Firefox, but Safari is the one Apple devices use.

For example: When you want to look up a recipe, check the news, or visit the Digital Confidence Centre, you open your browser and type or tap the address.

🍏 Try it on your iPad: Tap the blue Safari icon. At the top of the screen, tap the long white bar (the address bar) and type a word like weather. Tap Go to search.

📶 Wi-Fi

A wireless internet connection that allows your device to connect to the internet without any cables. At home, your Wi-Fi comes from a small box called a router. When connected, your iPad uses Wi-Fi instead of mobile data.

For example: When you are at home and see a small fan-shaped symbol (📶) at the top of your screen, your device is connected to Wi-Fi and you can browse the internet freely.

🍏 Try it on your iPad: Look at the very top-right corner of your screen. If you see a fan-shaped Wi-Fi symbol, you are connected. If not, go to Settings → Wi-Fi to connect.

🔐 Password

A secret combination of letters, numbers, and symbols that only you know. A password protects your accounts so that only you can get in. Your device may also use a PIN (a short number) or your fingerprint as a password.

For example: When you open your banking app, you enter a password or PIN — your personal secret code — before your account opens.

🍏 Try it on your iPad: Go to Settings → Face ID & Passcode (or Touch ID & Passcode) to see how your iPad is protected. You do not need to change anything — just explore.

🔔 Notification

A message or alert from an app telling you something has happened. Notifications can appear at the top of your screen, or on your lock screen. You can choose to ignore them, act on them, or turn them off entirely.

For example: When you receive a text message, a notification banner appears at the top of your screen showing who sent it.

🍏 Try it on your iPad: Go to Settings → Notifications to see which apps are sending you notifications. You can turn off notifications from apps you do not want to hear from.

🔄 Update

A new, improved version of an app or your device's software. Updates fix problems, add new features, and improve security. When you see a red number badge on the Settings or App Store icon, an update is available.

For example: Apple releases an update for your iPad once or twice a year. Installing it keeps your device running safely and smoothly.

🍏 Try it on your iPad: Go to Settings → General → Software Update to see if an update is available. You do not have to install it right now — just check.

⬇️ Download

Saving something from the internet to your device so you can use it later, even without internet. Apps are downloaded from the App Store. Photos, documents, and music can also be downloaded.

For example: When you install a new app like Instacart or YouTube, you are downloading it from the App Store to your iPad.

🍏 Try it on your iPad: Open the App Store (blue icon with a white "A"). Tap Search at the bottom and type any app name. You will see a GET button — that is the download button. You do not need to tap it unless you want the app.

⚙️ Settings

The place on your device where you can change how things work — your Wi-Fi, screen brightness, sounds, passwords, and much more. Settings does not connect to the internet on its own; it just controls your device's behaviour.

For example: If your screen is too bright or too dim, you go to Settings → Display & Brightness and move the slider until it is comfortable.

🍏 Try it on your iPad: Tap the grey Settings icon on your home screen. Look through the menu on the left side. You do not need to change anything — just explore what is there.

🔍 Search

Looking up information by typing a word or question. You can search the internet in a browser, search for apps on the App Store, or search for a word inside a document. Most search bars look like a white rectangle with a magnifying glass icon.

For example: When you want to know what time the pharmacy closes, you tap the search bar in Safari and type "pharmacy hours St. Thomas" — and the answer appears.

🍏 Try it on your iPad: Open Safari, tap the address bar at the top, and type a question like "weather today" or "local library hours." Tap Go and see the results.

🔗 Link

A piece of text, a button, or an image that you can tap to jump to another page or website. Links are usually blue and underlined, but they can look like any button. Tapping a link takes you somewhere new.

For example: The blue text that says Home Page in this sentence is a link. Tap it to go to the Digital Confidence Centre home page.

🍏 Try it on your iPad: Look for any underlined or blue text on a webpage. That is a link. Tap it gently to follow it. Use the back arrow (←) in your browser to come back.

📎 Attachment

A file — such as a photo, a document, or a PDF — that is sent along with an email. When you receive an email with a paperclip icon (📎), it means a file is attached. You can tap the attachment to view it.

For example: When your grandchild emails you a copy of their school report, the document arrives as an attachment in your email.

🍏 Try it on your iPad: Open the Mail app and look for any email with a small paperclip or image preview at the bottom. Tap on the attached file to open it.

🔎 Zoom

Making content on your screen larger so it is easier to read or see. On a touchscreen, you zoom in by placing two fingers on the screen and spreading them apart (like opening a pair of scissors). Pinch inward to zoom back out.

For example: When you receive a photo of the grandchildren and want to see their faces more clearly, you pinch outward on the photo to zoom in.

🍏 Try it on your iPad: Open any photo in the Photos app. Place your thumb and index finger close together on the photo, then spread them apart slowly. The photo gets larger. Pinch inward to zoom back out.

📸 Screenshot

A picture of exactly what is showing on your screen at that moment. Screenshots are useful for saving important information, sharing something you see on screen, or getting help with a problem. The screenshot is saved to your Photos app.

For example: When you want to save a recipe you found online, you take a screenshot and it is saved to your Photos app so you can look at it later.

🍏 Try it on your iPad: Press the top button and the volume up button at the same time. You will see a white flash and hear a camera click — that is your screenshot saved to Photos.

🏠 Home Screen

The main starting screen you see when you turn on your device or press the home button. It shows all your app icons and is where you return when you want to start fresh. Think of it as your device's living room.

For example: No matter where you are in any app, pressing the Home button (the round button at the bottom, or swiping up from the bottom on newer iPads) always brings you back to your home screen.

🍏 Try it on your iPad: Open any app. Now press the Home button at the bottom of the iPad, or swipe up from the very bottom edge of the screen. You are back on your home screen — safe and ready to start again.

😄 Comic Corner

A little laughter makes learning stick. Here are three gentle jokes for anyone who has ever felt bewildered by technology. You are not alone — and it is allowed to be funny.

😅

Why did the senior close all her browser tabs?

Because she could not remember why she opened them — just like walking into the kitchen!

📱

What did the phone say to its owner?

"Please update me."
What did the owner say?
"Me too, dear. Me too."

🦉

Why do seniors make the best tech learners?

Because they have the patience, the wisdom, and the life experience to know that nothing worth doing comes without a little practice — and nothing is ever as scary as it first looked.
