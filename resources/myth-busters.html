Senior Tech Myth Busters

Heard something scary about technology and wondered if it was true? Here are 10 common myths — explained honestly. Each one rated FALSE, NUANCED, or COMPLICATED.

Ratings: FALSE — Not true NUANCED — Partly true COMPLICATED — It depends
💡 How to use this page: Each myth is followed by a plain-language explanation. If you want to learn more, follow the link to the relevant training module.
1 FALSE

“I'll break it if I tap the wrong thing”

Nothing you tap on a phone or tablet will permanently break it. The worst that can happen is an app opens that you didn't want — and you can always press the Home button to leave. Devices are designed to be forgiving. Manufacturers know that everyone makes mistakes, and they build in easy ways to undo them.

2 MOSTLY FALSE

“Someone can see everything on my screen through the camera”

Your camera only activates when an app opens it — and your iPhone or iPad shows an orange dot at the top of the screen when the camera is on. Apps cannot secretly activate your camera without your knowledge on Apple devices. Apple has built strong protections against this. If you ever see that orange dot and don't know why, you can check which app is using it in your Settings.

→ Module 9: Understanding AI
3 FALSE

“I need to understand how the internet works to use it safely”

You don't need to know how the engine works to drive safely. You just need to know the warning signs. Online: don't tap links in unexpected texts, don't give passwords over the phone, and look for the padlock icon in your browser's address bar before entering personal information. A few simple rules go a very long way.

→ Module 2: The Security Shield
4 NUANCED

“Online banking is dangerous”

The risk isn't the bank's website — it's scammers pretending to be your bank. Your bank's real website is very secure. The golden rule: always type your bank's address yourself into the browser (e.g. td.com or scotiabank.com). Never click links in emails or texts claiming to be from your bank. If you follow that one rule, online banking is extremely safe.

→ Module 6: Banking & Transactions
5 FALSE

“AI can read my thoughts through my microphone”

AI voice assistants like Siri only activate when they hear their wake word — “Hey Siri.” They cannot read your thoughts and do not record conversations continuously. What your microphone actually captures is audio when deliberately activated by you or a wake word. Current AI has no ability to read thoughts — it can only process spoken words it hears.

→ Module 9: Understanding AI
6 FALSE

“If I get scammed, it's my fault”

These scams fool very intelligent, educated people every day — including doctors, lawyers, and financial advisers. Scammers are professional criminals who dedicate their careers to deceiving people. They are highly skilled at creating urgency, fear, and confusion. Being scammed is not a sign of stupidity — it is a sign that you encountered a professional criminal. If it happens to you, please report it to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre: 1-888-495-8501.

→ Module 2: The Security Shield
7 NUANCED

“My phone is listening to my conversations to show me ads”

The ads that seem uncannily relevant are more likely due to location tracking, browsing history, and purchase patterns — not microphone activation. However, voice assistants do listen for wake words. What feels like “my phone heard me” is more often your phone knowing your habits, location, and what your friends search for. You can limit ad tracking in Settings › Privacy › Tracking.

→ Module 9: Understanding AI
8 FALSE

“Older people can't learn new technology”

Neuroscience shows that adult brains continue to form new connections throughout life — this is called neuroplasticity. The real issue is that most technology is poorly designed for people who didn't grow up with it. It assumes knowledge that wasn't taught to previous generations. That's a design problem, not a learning problem. Proof that it isn't true? You're here.

→ Digital Literacy 101
9 FALSE

“Free apps are always safe”

Free apps make money in other ways — often by collecting and selling your personal data, or through in-app advertising. Some free apps are perfectly fine; others ask for more permissions than they need (like a flashlight app wanting access to your contacts). Always check what permissions an app requests, read the reviews, and ask yourself: why would this be free?

→ Module 4: App Store Safety
10 FALSE

“I need to restart my iPad every day”

You don't need to restart daily. A restart can help if an app is misbehaving or your device feels slow — but modern iPads are designed to run for days without restarting. Closing apps you're not using is also generally unnecessary; in fact, constantly force-closing apps can make your iPad work harder and use more battery.
