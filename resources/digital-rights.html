Your Digital Rights as a Canadian Senior

You have rights online — to privacy, to protect your data, to be treated fairly by companies, and to disconnect. This guide explains those rights in plain language.

Your Privacy Rights

The right to know what is collected about you
Under Canada's Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA) and Ontario's privacy laws, companies must tell you what personal information they collect, why they collect it, and how they use it. You have the right to ask any company what data they hold about you.
To exercise this right: contact the company in writing and request access to your personal information.
The right to consent
Companies cannot collect your personal information without your knowledge and consent, except in specific circumstances defined by law. Consent must be meaningful — not buried in a long terms-of-service document that no one reads.
If you feel your consent was not properly obtained, you can complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada: priv.gc.ca or 1-800-282-1376.
The right to unsubscribe from marketing emails
Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation (CASL) requires every commercial email to include a clearly visible unsubscribe link. When you click it, the company must honour your request within 10 business days. It is illegal for Canadian companies to continue sending you marketing emails after you unsubscribe.
To report CASL violations: fightspam.gc.ca or call 1-888-SPAM-LAW.

Your Consumer Rights Online

The right to a refund for online purchases
Under Ontario's Consumer Protection Act, online purchases that are not delivered, are significantly different from what was described, or are charged more than the displayed price, are eligible for a chargeback through your credit card company. You have the right to dispute any charge on your credit card.
The right to contest telecom billing errors
If your phone, internet, or cable company charges you for services you did not agree to, you can dispute the charge. The CRTC's Wireless Code and Internet Code set out your rights as a consumer. You can file a complaint with the Commission for Complaints for Telecom-television Services (CCTS).
CCTS: ccts-cprst.ca or 1-888-221-1687.
The right to cancel a door-to-door contract
Ontario's Consumer Protection Act gives you a 10-day cooling-off period to cancel most contracts signed at your door — for internet, energy, or any service over $50. You do not need to give a reason. Send a cancellation notice in writing within 10 days.

Who to Contact If Your Rights Are Violated

  • Privacy violations: Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada — priv.gc.ca — 1-800-282-1376
  • Fraud and scams: Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre — 1-888-495-8501 — antifraudcentre.ca
  • Telecom complaints: CCTS — ccts-cprst.ca — 1-888-221-1687
  • Consumer protection (Ontario): Ministry of Public and Business Service Delivery — 1-800-889-9768
  • Investment fraud: Ontario Securities Commission — osc.ca — 1-877-785-1555
  • Spam: Spam Reporting Centre — fightspam.gc.ca
