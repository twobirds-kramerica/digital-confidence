# Understanding Interac e-Transfer
**Module:** Module 6: Banking & Transactions
**Duration Target:** 3-4 minutes
**Audience:** Ontario seniors aged 70+

---

## SCRIPT

**[OPEN]**
[NARRATOR] Welcome back. You're doing great.

**[VISUAL CUE: Warm title card — "Understanding Interac e-Transfer"]**
[NARRATOR] Interac e-Transfer is a Canadian way to send and receive money electronically — directly from one bank account to another. Many Canadians use it to split bills with friends, pay for services, or send money to family members. Today we'll look at how it works, how to use it safely, and the one scam to watch out for.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the Interac logo and a simple animation — money moving from one phone to another]**
[NARRATOR] Think of e-Transfer like an electronic envelope. You put money in it, address it to someone using their email address or phone number, and send it. They receive a notification, and the money moves into their bank account. No cash needed. No cheque. No trip to the bank.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the bank app — navigate to Transfers section]**
[NARRATOR] To send an e-Transfer, open your bank app and look for "Transfers" or "Send Money" in the menu. Different banks organise this slightly differently, but it's usually easy to find. Tap it.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show selecting "Send Interac e-Transfer"]**
[NARRATOR] Select "Send Interac e-Transfer." You'll be asked to choose which account the money comes from — your chequing account, most likely. Then enter the amount you want to send.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show entering the recipient's email address or phone number]**
[NARRATOR] Next, you'll enter the recipient's email address or phone number. This is how Interac knows where to deliver the money. Make sure you have the right address — double-check with the person you're sending to before you do this the first time.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the optional security question — "What colour is my car?" — and the answer field]**
[NARRATOR] You'll have the option to set a security question and answer. This is an extra layer — the recipient needs to answer your question correctly to accept the money. Choose something your recipient will know but that isn't obvious to a stranger. Then share the answer with them separately — not in the same message as the transfer.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the "Autodeposit" feature explanation]**
[NARRATOR] Many people set up something called Autodeposit — which means they've told their bank to automatically accept any e-Transfer sent to their email address, skipping the security question entirely. If the person you're sending to has Autodeposit, the money will just arrive in their account automatically.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the review and send screen — confirm the amount and recipient]**
[NARRATOR] Before you send, you'll see a summary screen. Review the amount and the recipient carefully. When you're confident everything is correct, confirm with Face ID or your passcode and the transfer is sent.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show a red warning — "The e-Transfer Scam"]**
[NARRATOR] Now, the important safety warning. There is a common scam involving e-Transfer. You receive an email or text saying someone has sent you money. The message has a link to click to accept it. Before you click anything, ask yourself: were you expecting this? Do you know who supposedly sent it?

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the scam email — fake Interac email with suspicious sender address]**
[NARRATOR] Scammers send fake e-Transfer notification emails that look very convincing. But they're not from Interac — the sender's email address will be suspicious if you look carefully. If you click their link and enter your banking credentials, they get your login information.

**[VISUAL CUE: Text on screen — "To accept a real e-Transfer, log in to your bank app directly — never follow email links"]**
[NARRATOR] The safest way to accept any e-Transfer is to log directly into your bank app or website yourself — not by clicking the link in the email. Once you're logged in, look for a notification about incoming transfers. A real transfer will be right there waiting for you.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show a successful e-Transfer received — money added to balance]**
[NARRATOR] When used safely, e-Transfer is a fast, convenient, and genuinely useful tool. Millions of Canadians use it every day. Now you know how it works and how to protect yourself while using it.

**[CLOSE]**
[NARRATOR] Remember the 3-Second Rule: if something feels wrong, stop, breathe, and wait three seconds before tapping anything. You can always come back to something, but you can't un-tap a link. See you in the next video.
