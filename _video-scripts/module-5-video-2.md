# Spotting a Phishing Email
**Module:** Module 5: Email & Messages
**Duration Target:** 3-4 minutes
**Audience:** Ontario seniors aged 70+

---

## SCRIPT

**[OPEN]**
[NARRATOR] Welcome back. You're doing great.

**[VISUAL CUE: Warm title card — "Spotting a Phishing Email"]**
[NARRATOR] One of the most common ways that scammers try to get into people's accounts is through something called phishing — spelled P-H-I-S-H-I-N-G. It's a play on the word fishing. The scammer drops a bait — a fake email — and hopes you'll bite. Today we're going to learn exactly what that bait looks like, so you never take it.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show a convincing-looking fake email — supposedly from a bank, asking to "verify your account"]**
[NARRATOR] Here's an example of a phishing email. At first glance, it looks legitimate. It has a bank's logo. It uses professional language. It says your account has been compromised and asks you to click a link to verify your information. This is bait.

**[VISUAL CUE: Zoom in to the "From" address — shows a suspicious domain like "support@td-bank-secure.net"]**
[NARRATOR] The first thing to check is who sent it. Not the display name — that can say anything — but the actual email address. Tap on the sender's name at the top of the email. The full email address will appear. If it says something like "service@td-bank-secure.net" instead of "@td.com" — that is not TD Bank. That is a fake.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show real TD Bank email address vs fake side by side]**
[NARRATOR] Real organisations use their own domain names in email addresses. TD Bank uses @td.com. The CRA uses @canada.ca. Your local hospital might use their hospital's proper name. If the domain name in the address looks complicated, hyphenated, or adds extra words — it's fake.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the email's link — hovering over it reveals a suspicious URL]**
[NARRATOR] The second thing to check is any link in the email. On your iPad, you can press and hold a link — don't tap it, just hold your finger on it for a moment. A preview will appear showing you where the link actually goes. If the address in that preview looks strange or doesn't match the organisation's proper website — don't tap it.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show urgency language highlighted in a phishing email — "Within 24 hours or your account will be closed"]**
[NARRATOR] The third warning sign is urgency. Phishing emails almost always create a time pressure. "Your account will be closed within 24 hours." "Immediate action required." "Verify now or lose access." Real organisations do not communicate this way. They send letters in the mail. They give you time to respond.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show an email with a generic greeting — "Dear Valued Customer" highlighted]**
[NARRATOR] A fourth clue: look at how the email addresses you. Your bank knows your name. A real email from your bank will usually say "Dear Margaret" or "Dear Mr. Chen." A phishing email will say "Dear Customer" or "Dear Account Holder" because the scammer sent it to thousands of people at once and doesn't know your name.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the Delete button being tapped on a suspicious email]**
[NARRATOR] If you receive an email that sets off any of these alarm bells — strange sender, suspicious link, urgency, generic greeting — delete it. Don't reply to it. Don't tap any links. Don't call any numbers in the email.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show someone calling their bank on the phone instead]**
[NARRATOR] If you're worried the email might be legitimate — for example, you're genuinely not sure about your bank account — call your bank directly using the number on the back of your card. Don't use any number provided in the email. Just call your bank and ask them if they sent you anything.

**[VISUAL CUE: Text on screen — "When in doubt, do not click. Delete and call."]**
[NARRATOR] Your rule for suspicious emails: do not click, delete, and call. That three-step response will protect you every single time.

**[CLOSE]**
[NARRATOR] Remember the 3-Second Rule: if something feels wrong, stop, breathe, and wait three seconds before tapping anything. You can always come back to something, but you can't un-tap a link. See you in the next video.
