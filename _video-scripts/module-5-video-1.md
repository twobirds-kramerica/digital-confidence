# Reading and Sending Emails
**Module:** Module 5: Email & Messages
**Duration Target:** 3-4 minutes
**Audience:** Ontario seniors aged 70+

---

## SCRIPT

**[OPEN]**
[NARRATOR] Welcome back. You're doing great.

**[VISUAL CUE: Warm title card — "Reading and Sending Emails"]**
[NARRATOR] Email is one of the most powerful communication tools available to us. Once you're comfortable with it, you can stay connected with family, receive receipts and appointment reminders, and communicate with doctors and businesses — all at your own pace. Today we'll walk through the basics of reading and sending emails on your iPad.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the Mail app icon — the white envelope on a blue background]**
[NARRATOR] The Mail app on your iPad looks like a white envelope on a blue background. Tap it to open. If you've already set up an email account, you'll see your inbox — a list of all the emails you've received.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the inbox — unread emails are shown with a blue dot on the left]**
[NARRATOR] Emails you haven't read yet will have a small blue dot on the left side. Tap on any email to read it. It will open in the main part of the screen, or on larger iPads, in a panel on the right side.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show an open email — from name, subject, date, and body text all labelled]**
[NARRATOR] Every email has a few parts. At the top, you'll see who it's from. Below that, the subject — which tells you what the email is about. Then the date and time it was sent. And below all that, the body — the actual message.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the reply button — the curved arrow icon at the bottom or top of the email]**
[NARRATOR] To reply to an email, look for the curved arrow icon — it looks like a backwards C with an arrow. Tap it. A menu will appear with options: Reply, Reply All, or Forward. Tap "Reply" to write back to the person who sent it.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the reply screen — keyboard open, cursor in the message body]**
[NARRATOR] Your reply window opens. The cursor is waiting for you in the body of the message. Start typing your reply. Take your time — there's no rush. Email is not like a phone call. You can write your message, re-read it, fix any mistakes, and then send it when you're ready.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the Send button — the blue upward arrow icon]**
[NARRATOR] When you're happy with your message, tap the Send button — it looks like a blue arrow pointing upward. Your email is sent. You'll see it appear in your Sent folder.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show composing a new email — tap the pen-and-paper icon]**
[NARRATOR] To write a brand new email, look for the icon that looks like a pen writing on a piece of paper — it's usually in the bottom-right corner of the screen. Tap it and a new blank email opens.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the "To" field being filled in — typing an email address]**
[NARRATOR] The "To" field is where you type the email address of the person you're writing to. If you've emailed them before, their name may appear as a suggestion as you start typing — just tap their name if it appears and your iPad fills in the address automatically.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the "Subject" field being filled in]**
[NARRATOR] Fill in the Subject — a brief description of what your email is about. Something like "Appointment question" or "Hello from Toronto." This helps the person know what to expect before they open it.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show typing in the email body]**
[NARRATOR] Then tap in the large body area and write your message. When you're done, tap that blue send arrow.

**[VISUAL CUE: Text on screen — "Tip: You can make text larger in Mail"]**
[NARRATOR] If the text in your emails is too small to read comfortably, go to Settings, then Accessibility, then Display & Text Size, and increase the text size. This will make everything in Mail easier to read.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show a senior writing and sending an email with a smile — perhaps to a grandchild]**
[NARRATOR] Email is yours to use at your own pace. There's no pressure to respond instantly. Write when you're comfortable, send when you're ready, and enjoy staying connected on your own terms.

**[CLOSE]**
[NARRATOR] Remember the 3-Second Rule: if something feels wrong, stop, breathe, and wait three seconds before tapping anything. You can always come back to something, but you can't un-tap a link. See you in the next video.
