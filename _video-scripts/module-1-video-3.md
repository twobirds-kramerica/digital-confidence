# Restarting Your iPad: When and How
**Module:** Module 1: Mastering the Escape Hatch
**Duration Target:** 3-4 minutes
**Audience:** Ontario seniors aged 70+

---

## SCRIPT

**[OPEN]**
[NARRATOR] Welcome back. You're doing great.

**[VISUAL CUE: Warm title card — "Restarting Your iPad: When and How"]**
[NARRATOR] There's a phrase that technology people use all the time. You've probably heard it before. "Have you tried turning it off and turning it back on again?" As simple as it sounds, this genuinely fixes a surprising number of problems. Today we're going to do exactly that — and I'll show you when you need to and when you don't.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show an iPad sitting on a table, looking perfectly normal]**
[NARRATOR] Your iPad is a very capable computer. It runs quietly in the background, managing dozens of tasks at once. But sometimes, after days or weeks of use, things can start to feel sluggish — apps are slow to open, the screen is a little unresponsive, something just feels off. That is a great time for a restart.

**[VISUAL CUE: Text on screen listing three situations — "Slow iPad / App keeps crashing / Screen acting strange"]**
[NARRATOR] A restart is a good idea when your iPad feels slow, when an app keeps crashing even after you've force quit it, or when the screen seems to be acting strangely. You don't need to restart every day — but once a week is a healthy habit.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the side button and volume buttons on an iPad — label them clearly]**
[NARRATOR] Now let me show you how to restart. On most iPads, you'll use the button on the side — sometimes called the Sleep/Wake button or the Power button. It's on the top edge or the right side of your iPad, depending on your model.

**[VISUAL CUE: Demonstrate pressing and holding the side button until "slide to power off" appears]**
[NARRATOR] Press and hold that side button — hold it down firmly for about three seconds. Don't tap it, hold it. You'll see a screen appear with a slider that says "slide to power off."

**[VISUAL CUE: Close-up of the "slide to power off" slider]**
[NARRATOR] Now use your finger to slide that slider from left to right. It's like pushing a door bolt open. Slide it all the way across.

**[VISUAL CUE: Screen goes black — iPad is off]**
[NARRATOR] The screen goes black. Your iPad is now off. It's not broken — it's just resting. Give it about ten seconds.

**[VISUAL CUE: Press the side button again — Apple logo appears]**
[NARRATOR] Now press and hold the side button again. After a moment, you'll see the Apple logo appear in the middle of the screen. That means your iPad is starting back up. This takes about thirty seconds to a minute — no need to worry if it takes a little while.

**[VISUAL CUE: iPad home screen loads — everything looks normal and fresh]**
[NARRATOR] And there you are. Your iPad is back, fresh and ready. Everything is exactly as you left it. Your apps are all there. Your photos are safe. Nothing was deleted.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show a newer iPad — demonstrate holding side button plus volume button together]**
[NARRATOR] On some of the newest iPads without a Home button, you'll need to hold the side button and a volume button at the same time — then use that same "slide to power off" slider. If your iPad has a Home button, you can use just the side button on its own.

**[VISUAL CUE: Text on screen: "Not sure? Just use Settings → General → Shut Down"]**
[NARRATOR] And here's an even easier method that works on every iPad: go to Settings — that's the grey icon with gears. Tap General, then scroll down to the very bottom. You'll see a button that says "Shut Down." Tap it, use the slider, and your iPad turns off. Then press the side button to turn it back on. Simple and reliable.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show someone restarting their iPad and smiling as it comes back on]**
[NARRATOR] A restart is one of the most powerful tools you have. It's free, it's quick, and it solves more problems than you might expect. Make it a habit — restart your iPad once a week, and it will serve you much better.

**[CLOSE]**
[NARRATOR] Remember the 3-Second Rule: if something feels wrong, stop, breathe, and wait three seconds before tapping anything. You can always come back to something, but you can't un-tap a link. See you in the next video.
