# Unsubscribing Safely from Emails
**Module:** Module 5: Email & Messages
**Duration Target:** 3-4 minutes
**Audience:** Ontario seniors aged 70+

---

## SCRIPT

**[OPEN]**
[NARRATOR] Welcome back. You're doing great.

**[VISUAL CUE: Warm title card — "Unsubscribing Safely from Emails"]**
[NARRATOR] Does your inbox feel like it's overflowing with emails you never asked for? Newsletters, promotions, sale announcements — all piling up every day? Today we're going to clean that up. But we're also going to talk about when unsubscribing is safe, and when it actually makes things worse.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show an overflowing inbox — dozens of unread promotional emails]**
[NARRATOR] First, a reassuring thought: those promotional emails are annoying, but they usually aren't dangerous. Most of them come from actual companies that you've done business with — a store where you shopped, a service you signed up for. You can safely unsubscribe from these.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show a legitimate promotional email scrolled to the very bottom — unsubscribe link visible]**
[NARRATOR] To unsubscribe from a legitimate email, scroll all the way to the very bottom of the email. You'll almost always find a small, light-coloured link that says "Unsubscribe" or "Manage your email preferences." Tap it.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the unsubscribe confirmation page in Safari — "You've been unsubscribed" message]**
[NARRATOR] A page will open in your browser, usually confirming that you've been removed from the mailing list. Sometimes it asks you to confirm once more by tapping a button. After that, you're done. It may take a few days for the emails to stop, which is normal.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the Mail app's "Unsubscribe" banner at the top of an email — a quick option]**
[NARRATOR] Your iPad actually helps with this. For many mailing lists, the Mail app will show a small blue "Unsubscribe" link right at the very top of the email — above everything else. This is Apple's way of making unsubscribing easy. If you see it, you can simply tap that link and confirm, without even needing to scroll down.

**[VISUAL CUE: Red warning — "When NOT to unsubscribe"]**
[NARRATOR] Now here's the important caution. Do not unsubscribe from emails that look suspicious. If you receive an email that seems like it might be spam or a phishing attempt, do not scroll down and tap the unsubscribe link. On a real mailing list, unsubscribing removes you. But in a scam email, tapping "unsubscribe" actually tells the scammer that your email address is real and active — which leads to more scam emails, not fewer.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show two emails side by side — one from a recognisable shop, one suspicious — label which to unsubscribe from]**
[NARRATOR] The rule is: only unsubscribe from emails you recognise. If you know the company — you've shopped there, you signed up for their newsletter — unsubscribe safely. If the email is from someone you don't know and feels suspicious, just delete it. Don't engage with it at all.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the Mail app's "Mark as Junk" option]**
[NARRATOR] For suspicious emails, use the Mark as Junk feature instead. While you have the email open, tap the flag icon at the bottom of the screen. Then tap "Mark as Junk." This moves the email to your Junk folder and helps your Mail app learn to filter similar emails in the future.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show a much cleaner inbox after a round of unsubscribing]**
[NARRATOR] After a few weeks of unsubscribing from legitimate newsletters and marking suspicious ones as junk, your inbox will start to feel much more manageable. You'll find it easier to spot emails that actually matter — messages from family, appointment reminders, and things you genuinely want to read.

**[VISUAL CUE: Text on screen — "Recognise it? Unsubscribe. Suspicious? Delete. Never engage with scam emails."]**
[NARRATOR] Your quick guide: If you recognise the sender, unsubscribe at the bottom. If it looks suspicious, delete it without engaging. You're in charge of your inbox.

**[CLOSE]**
[NARRATOR] Remember the 3-Second Rule: if something feels wrong, stop, breathe, and wait three seconds before tapping anything. You can always come back to something, but you can't un-tap a link. See you in the next video.
