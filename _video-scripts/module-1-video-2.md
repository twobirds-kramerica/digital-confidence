# Force Quitting Apps When Things Go Wrong
**Module:** Module 1: Mastering the Escape Hatch
**Duration Target:** 3-4 minutes
**Audience:** Ontario seniors aged 70+

---

## SCRIPT

**[OPEN]**
[NARRATOR] Welcome back. You're doing great.

**[VISUAL CUE: Warm title card — "Force Quitting Apps When Things Go Wrong"]**
[NARRATOR] Sometimes an app gets stuck. The screen freezes, nothing responds to your taps, or something just looks wrong. When that happens, there is a solution — and it's called force quitting. Today I'll show you exactly how to do it, step by step.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show an iPad screen with an app that appears frozen — a spinning wheel]**
[NARRATOR] Force quitting simply means closing an app completely — like closing a book and putting it back on the shelf. The app isn't broken. Your iPad is fine. You're just going to shut that app down and give it a fresh start.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show an older iPad with a Home button]**
[NARRATOR] If you have an older iPad with the round Home button, here's how you do it. Press the Home button twice quickly — two taps in a row, fairly fast. Not slow, not too fast. Double-tap.

**[VISUAL CUE: Demonstrate double-tap on Home button — App Switcher appears showing card-style app previews]**
[NARRATOR] You'll see something called the App Switcher. It shows you all the apps that are currently open in the background — they look like cards, like a hand of playing cards spread out. You can swipe left and right to see them all.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show swiping through app cards, then finding the stuck app]**
[NARRATOR] Find the app that was giving you trouble. Now here's the move: put your finger on that card and swipe it upward — like you're flicking a piece of paper off a table. Swipe it up and off the screen.

**[VISUAL CUE: Demonstrate the upward swipe — app card flies off screen]**
[NARRATOR] That's it. The app is now force quit. You can press the Home button again to go back to your normal home screen.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show a newer iPad without a Home button]**
[NARRATOR] If you have a newer iPad without the Home button, the steps are slightly different. Start by slowly swiping up from the bottom of the screen — but this time, pause in the middle. Hold your finger there for a second, right in the middle of the screen.

**[VISUAL CUE: Demonstrate the slow swipe-up and pause — App Switcher appears]**
[NARRATOR] The App Switcher appears the same way — those playing cards spread out. Find the app you want to close. Swipe it upward off the screen. Done.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the app being reopened after force quit — it loads fresh]**
[NARRATOR] Now here's the good news. After you force quit an app, you can simply tap its icon and open it again. It starts fresh, like you just woke it up from a good sleep. Usually, whatever was frozen will be gone and everything will work normally again.

**[VISUAL CUE: Text on screen: "Force quit = a fresh start. Nothing is lost."]**
[NARRATOR] Force quitting does not delete anything from the app. Your photos are still there. Your messages are still there. Your notes are safe. You are simply restarting that one app, not erasing it.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show someone practising the force quit gesture calmly]**
[NARRATOR] This is a great skill to practise. Try it now on an app you use regularly — maybe Photos or Settings. Open the App Switcher and just have a look. Then swipe it away and reopen it. You'll feel much more confident the next time something actually freezes.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show text — "Double Home-tap → swipe up = problem solved"]**
[NARRATOR] Two taps on Home. Swipe up. Back to normal. You can handle this.

**[CLOSE]**
[NARRATOR] Remember the 3-Second Rule: if something feels wrong, stop, breathe, and wait three seconds before tapping anything. You can always come back to something, but you can't un-tap a link. See you in the next video.
