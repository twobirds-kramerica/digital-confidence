# What to Do If You've Already Responded to a Scam
**Module:** Module 2: The Security Shield
**Duration Target:** 3-4 minutes
**Audience:** Ontario seniors aged 70+

---

## SCRIPT

**[OPEN]**
[NARRATOR] Welcome back. You're doing great.

**[VISUAL CUE: Warm title card — "What to Do If You've Already Responded to a Scam"]**
[NARRATOR] Today's video is an important one. We're going to talk about what to do if you've already clicked a link, given out information, or sent money in response to something that turned out to be a scam. First, I want to say this clearly: it is not your fault. These scams are designed by very clever people to trick very intelligent adults. Being deceived by a scam does not mean you made a foolish mistake — it means someone with bad intentions worked hard to fool you.

**[VISUAL CUE: Text on screen — "It is not your fault." in large, warm text]**
[NARRATOR] Now let's talk about what to do, because acting quickly matters.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show a step-by-step checklist appearing on screen]**
[NARRATOR] Step one: Stop all contact. If you're on the phone with the scammer, hang up. If you're in the middle of sending money, stop. Don't send more thinking you'll get back what you already sent — that is another common trick.

**[VISUAL CUE: Step 1 checkmark — "Stop all contact"]**

**[VISUAL CUE: Show a bank app or someone calling their bank]**
[NARRATOR] Step two: Call your bank. Do this right away if any financial information or payments were involved. Tell them what happened. Banks deal with this regularly — they are not going to judge you. They may be able to freeze a transaction or flag your account for protection. The phone number is on the back of your card or on your bank's official website.

**[VISUAL CUE: Step 2 checkmark — "Call your bank immediately"]**

**[VISUAL CUE: Show a phone with a new password screen]**
[NARRATOR] Step three: Change your passwords. If you gave anyone your password, or if you visited a suspicious website and typed anything in, change the passwords for your email, your bank, and any other account that might be affected. We'll cover passwords in detail in Module 3 — but for now, just know this step is important.

**[VISUAL CUE: Step 3 checkmark — "Change affected passwords"]**

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre logo and phone number]**
[NARRATOR] Step four: Report it. In Canada, you can report scams to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre at 1-888-495-8501, or online at antifraudcentre.ca. You can also report to your local police if money was involved. Reporting helps protect other Canadians from the same scam.

**[VISUAL CUE: Step 4 checkmark — "Report to Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre: 1-888-495-8501"]**

**[VISUAL CUE: Show someone talking to a trusted family member]**
[NARRATOR] Step five: Tell someone you trust. A family member, a friend, your doctor, anyone. Not because you need to confess something — but because having support makes everything easier to navigate. And they may be able to help you with some of these steps.

**[VISUAL CUE: Step 5 checkmark — "Tell someone you trust"]**

**[VISUAL CUE: Show an iPad being restarted]**
[NARRATOR] If you tapped a link on your iPad, it's a good idea to restart your iPad as well. In most cases, simply visiting a scam website on an iPad does not install harmful software — Apple's security is quite strong. But a restart is a reasonable precaution and costs you nothing.

**[VISUAL CUE: Text on screen — "Reporting protects the next person"]**
[NARRATOR] Here's why reporting matters so much. When you report a scam, that information goes to law enforcement. It helps them track patterns and protect other Canadians — perhaps someone else's grandmother or grandfather who might receive the same call next week. Your report is not just paperwork. It is a contribution to your community's safety.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show a calm, reassuring scene — a cup of tea, a comfortable chair]**
[NARRATOR] If this has happened to you recently, take a breath. Take it one step at a time. You don't have to handle all of this in one hour. Do what you can today, then rest.

**[CLOSE]**
[NARRATOR] Remember the 3-Second Rule: if something feels wrong, stop, breathe, and wait three seconds before tapping anything. You can always come back to something, but you can't un-tap a link. See you in the next video.
