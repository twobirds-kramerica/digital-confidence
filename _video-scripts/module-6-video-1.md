# Getting Your Bank's Real App
**Module:** Module 6: Banking & Transactions
**Duration Target:** 3-4 minutes
**Audience:** Ontario seniors aged 70+

---

## SCRIPT

**[OPEN]**
[NARRATOR] Welcome back. You're doing great.

**[VISUAL CUE: Warm title card — "Getting Your Bank's Real App"]**
[NARRATOR] Mobile banking is one of the most useful things your iPad can do. Check your balance, transfer money, pay bills — all from your chair at home, no need to drive to a branch. But to do it safely, you need to start with the right app. Today we'll walk through how to find and download your bank's legitimate, official app.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show logos of major Canadian banks — TD, RBC, BMO, Scotiabank, CIBC, Credit unions]**
[NARRATOR] All of Canada's major banks — TD, RBC, BMO, Scotiabank, CIBC — as well as credit unions like FirstOntario and many others, have official apps in the App Store. These apps are made by the banks themselves and are the safest way to do your banking on an iPad.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show someone on their bank's official website — looking at the footer for the app download link]**
[NARRATOR] Here is the safest way to find your bank's app. First, open Safari and go to your bank's official website. You already know your bank's web address, and if not, it's on the back of your debit card or on any statement you've received. Type it directly — don't follow a link.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the bank's website with an "App Store" badge in the footer — tap it]**
[NARRATOR] On your bank's website, usually in the footer at the very bottom or on a "Mobile Banking" page, you'll find an App Store badge — the one with the Apple logo and "Download on the App Store." Tap it. This takes you directly to the correct listing in the App Store.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the App Store listing with the bank's name as developer — verified]**
[NARRATOR] You'll land on the bank's official App Store page. Check the developer name matches your bank's full legal name. For example, TD Bank's app is developed by "TD Bank." RBC's is by "Royal Bank of Canada." This confirms you're in the right place.

**[VISUAL CUE: Tap "Get" — Face ID confirmation — download begins]**
[NARRATOR] Tap "Get" to download. Confirm with Face ID or your passcode. The app downloads to your iPad. Find it on your home screen and tap to open.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the bank app's login screen — asking for username and password]**
[NARRATOR] The first time you open your bank's app, you'll be asked to log in. This is the same username and password you might use for online banking on a computer. If you've never done online banking before, the app will have an option to enrol — or you can call your bank first to set up online access before using the app.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show setting up Face ID within the banking app — a separate Face ID permission for the app]**
[NARRATOR] Once you're logged in, most banking apps will offer to set up Face ID for future logins — so you just look at your iPad and you're in. This is very convenient and safe. I recommend setting it up.

**[VISUAL CUE: Text on screen — "Your bank will NEVER call you to download an app"]**
[NARRATOR] An important safety note: your bank will never call you and ask you to download an app or give you instructions for downloading one over the phone. If someone calls you claiming to be from your bank and gives you a link or a name to search for in the App Store, hang up. Find the app yourself using the method I've just shown you.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show a senior comfortably using their bank app at home]**
[NARRATOR] Once you have the real app installed and logged in, you have one of the safest and most convenient banking tools available. You can check your balance anytime, see recent transactions, and stay on top of your account without leaving the house.

**[VISUAL CUE: Text on screen — "Start on the bank's website → follow their link to the App Store"]**
[NARRATOR] Remember: start on your bank's official website, not in the App Store. Follow their link to the App Store. That route guarantees you end up with the real app.

**[CLOSE]**
[NARRATOR] Remember the 3-Second Rule: if something feels wrong, stop, breathe, and wait three seconds before tapping anything. You can always come back to something, but you can't un-tap a link. See you in the next video.
