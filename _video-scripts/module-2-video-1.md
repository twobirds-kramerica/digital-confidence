# Recognising a CRA Phone Scam
**Module:** Module 2: The Security Shield
**Duration Target:** 3-4 minutes
**Audience:** Ontario seniors aged 70+

---

## SCRIPT

**[OPEN]**
[NARRATOR] Welcome back. You're doing great.

**[VISUAL CUE: Warm title card — "Recognising a CRA Phone Scam"]**
[NARRATOR] The Canada Revenue Agency — the CRA — handles income taxes for all Canadians. And because so many people deal with the CRA, it has become one of the most imitated organisations in phone scam history. Today we're going to look at exactly how these scams work — so you can spot them immediately.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show a phone ringing — unknown number displayed]**
[NARRATOR] It usually starts with a phone call. The caller says they're from the CRA. They sound very official, maybe even a little stern. They tell you that you owe back taxes. That if you don't pay immediately, you'll be arrested. That police are already on their way.

**[VISUAL CUE: Text on screen — "ARREST. IMMEDIATELY. POLICE." in bold red — then a big red X over it]**
[NARRATOR] Stop right there. The real CRA does not call you to threaten arrest. The real CRA does not demand immediate payment. The real CRA does not send police to your door. These are the words of a scammer, not a government employee.

**[VISUAL CUE: Split screen — "Real CRA" on left vs "Scam Call" on right]**
[NARRATOR] Here's how to tell them apart. The real CRA will mail you letters before calling. They will give you time to respond. They will never demand gift cards, wire transfers, or cryptocurrency. They have a website you can verify at canada.ca. A scammer does all the opposite — they call out of nowhere, create panic, and demand payment in unusual ways.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show a fake CRA call scenario — caller pushes hard for gift card payment]**
[NARRATOR] One of the most common tricks is demanding payment by gift card. The scammer will tell you to go to a drugstore or grocery store, buy an iTunes gift card or a Google Play card, and read them the numbers on the back. No government agency in Canada will ever ask you to pay with a gift card. Ever. Full stop.

**[VISUAL CUE: Text on screen — "Gift cards are for gifts. Not for taxes."]**
[NARRATOR] Gift cards are for gifts. Not for taxes. If anyone asks you to pay anything with a gift card — government, bank, police, anyone — hang up. It is a scam.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show a senior calmly putting the phone down]**
[NARRATOR] Now here's the really important part. You are allowed to hang up. You do not owe that caller anything. You do not need to explain yourself. You do not need to be polite to someone who is trying to steal from you. Simply say, "I'm ending this call," and hang up.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show someone dialling 1-800-959-8281]**
[NARRATOR] If you're ever genuinely worried about your taxes, you can call the CRA yourself at 1-800-959-8281. That is their real number. Look it up at canada.ca. When you call them yourself, you know exactly who you're talking to.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show a list of CRA scam warning signs]**
[NARRATOR] To summarise: it's a scam if they threaten arrest, demand immediate payment, ask for gift cards or wire transfers, won't let you call back, or pressure you to stay on the line. Real government agencies never do these things.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the word "PAUSE" on screen]**
[NARRATOR] If you ever receive a call like this, use the 3-Second Rule. Pause. Breathe. Don't let them rush you. A legitimate organisation will always give you time. A scammer never will.

**[CLOSE]**
[NARRATOR] Remember the 3-Second Rule: if something feels wrong, stop, breathe, and wait three seconds before tapping anything. You can always come back to something, but you can't un-tap a link. See you in the next video.
