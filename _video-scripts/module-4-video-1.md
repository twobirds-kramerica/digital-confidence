# Finding the Real App Store
**Module:** Module 4: App Store Safety
**Duration Target:** 3-4 minutes
**Audience:** Ontario seniors aged 70+

---

## SCRIPT

**[OPEN]**
[NARRATOR] Welcome back. You're doing great.

**[VISUAL CUE: Warm title card — "Finding the Real App Store"]**
[NARRATOR] Your iPad can only install apps from one place — the Apple App Store. This is actually one of the most important safety features of your iPad, and today we're going to learn how to find the real App Store, what it looks like, and why it matters so much.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the App Store icon — the blue square with the white letter A made of a stick]**
[NARRATOR] The App Store icon looks like this — a blue square with a white stylised letter A, made to look like a pencil or a stick drawing a star. That is the only App Store you ever need. It comes pre-installed on every iPad. You cannot accidentally delete it.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the home screen — tap the App Store icon — it opens]**
[NARRATOR] Tap that icon. The App Store opens. At the top you'll see a search bar, and below it you'll see featured apps and categories. This is where all legitimate apps live.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the App Store's Today tab, then the Search tab at the bottom]**
[NARRATOR] There are tabs at the bottom of the App Store. "Today" shows featured apps. "Apps" shows categories you can browse. "Games" is just for games. And "Search" — which you'll use most often — lets you type in exactly what you're looking for.

**[VISUAL CUE: Demonstrate searching for "CBC News" in the App Store]**
[NARRATOR] Let's say you want to download the CBC News app. Tap "Search" at the bottom. Tap the search bar at the top and type "CBC News." You'll see a list of results appear.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the search results — point to the official CBC app with the checkmark badge]**
[NARRATOR] Now here's something important. In the search results, look for the app made by the actual organisation. For CBC News, you're looking for the app made by "CBC/Radio-Canada." You can see who made an app by looking at the small text just below the app name.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show two apps side by side — one real, one imitator — highlight the developer name difference]**
[NARRATOR] Sometimes there are multiple apps with similar names. The real app is usually at or near the top of results. Check the developer name. A news app from CBC will say "CBC/Radio-Canada." A bank app from RBC will say "Royal Bank of Canada." If the developer name looks odd or unfamiliar — be cautious and do a bit more research before downloading.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show what a scam website looks like trying to imitate an app store — red warning overlay]**
[NARRATOR] One thing to be very aware of: you might sometimes come across a website that looks like the App Store. Maybe you clicked a link in an email, and it took you to a page with buttons that say "Download Now." This is not the App Store. The real App Store is an app on your iPad, not a website. Never download anything from a website claiming to be the App Store.

**[VISUAL CUE: Text on screen — "The App Store is an APP — not a website"]**
[NARRATOR] The App Store is an app, not a website. If you're looking at a website with the App Store logo, close it.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the App Store showing the developer page for a well-known app]**
[NARRATOR] The safest way to download an app is to search for it in the App Store directly. Don't follow links from emails or text messages. Don't scan QR codes to get to an app store. Just open the App Store, search for what you want, and download it from there.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the App Store with an app's rating, number of reviews, and age rating]**
[NARRATOR] Before you download any app, take five seconds to look at three things: the rating out of five stars, the number of reviews, and whether a large, well-known company made it. A trusted app will typically have thousands of reviews and a rating above 4 stars.

**[CLOSE]**
[NARRATOR] Remember the 3-Second Rule: if something feels wrong, stop, breathe, and wait three seconds before tapping anything. You can always come back to something, but you can't un-tap a link. See you in the next video.
