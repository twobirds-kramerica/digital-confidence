# Checking Your Balance Online
**Module:** Module 6: Banking & Transactions
**Duration Target:** 3-4 minutes
**Audience:** Ontario seniors aged 70+

---

## SCRIPT

**[OPEN]**
[NARRATOR] Welcome back. You're doing great.

**[VISUAL CUE: Warm title card — "Checking Your Balance Online"]**
[NARRATOR] One of the most practical things your iPad can do is let you check your bank balance anytime you like — without waiting for a paper statement, without calling the bank, without driving anywhere. Today we're going to do exactly that, step by step.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show a bank app icon on the home screen — tap it]**
[NARRATOR] Open your bank's app. Tap the icon on your home screen. If you've set up Face ID for the app, it will unlock as soon as you look at the screen. If not, type your password.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the bank app's main screen — accounts listed with balances]**
[NARRATOR] After you log in, you'll usually land on a summary screen that shows all your accounts. You might see your chequing account, savings account, and possibly a credit card or line of credit — all listed with their current balances.

**[VISUAL CUE: Tap on the chequing account — transaction history appears]**
[NARRATOR] Tap on your chequing account to see more details. You'll see your current balance at the top, and below it, a list of recent transactions — every purchase, withdrawal, and deposit, with dates and amounts.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show a transaction list — grocery store, utility payment, pension deposit labelled]**
[NARRATOR] Scroll through your recent transactions. You'll see things like grocery store purchases, bill payments, and any deposits — perhaps your pension or OAS payment. This is all normal activity. Getting in the habit of reviewing these regularly is one of the best ways to catch any unusual charges early.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show an unfamiliar charge highlighted — a small note: "Contact your bank if you don't recognise a charge"]**
[NARRATOR] If you see a transaction you don't recognise, don't panic. Sometimes the name a store uses in banking is different from the name you know — for example, a pharmacy might show up under a parent company name. But if something genuinely doesn't look right, call your bank using the number on the back of your card.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show navigating back to main menu — tapping the back arrow or the account summary tab]**
[NARRATOR] To go back to the main account summary, tap the back arrow or the home icon within the app. Navigation is similar to other apps — tap Back to go back, tap the tabs at the bottom to switch sections.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the Statements section of a banking app]**
[NARRATOR] Most bank apps also have a Statements section where you can view your monthly statements as PDF documents. This is useful if you need to check a specific month, or if you want to keep a record for taxes or budgeting.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show logging out — tap profile or menu icon, then "Sign Out"]**
[NARRATOR] When you're done, log out. Don't just close the app — actually sign out. Look for a menu icon, often three lines or your initials in a corner, and find the "Sign Out" or "Log Out" option. Tap it.

**[VISUAL CUE: Text on screen — "Always log out when finished. Especially on shared devices."]**
[NARRATOR] This is especially important if your iPad is ever used by others in your household. Logging out means the next person to open the app won't automatically be in your account.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show a Wi-Fi symbol — and the tip about not using public Wi-Fi for banking]**
[NARRATOR] One important safety habit: only check your banking when you're connected to your home Wi-Fi, not on public Wi-Fi at a café or library. Your home Wi-Fi is private. Public Wi-Fi can be monitored by others.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show a senior comfortably checking their balance over morning coffee]**
[NARRATOR] Checking your balance regularly is a good financial habit and a safety habit rolled into one. You'll feel more in control of your money, and you'll catch any problems early. This is something you can do every week from the comfort of your own home.

**[CLOSE]**
[NARRATOR] Remember the 3-Second Rule: if something feels wrong, stop, breathe, and wait three seconds before tapping anything. You can always come back to something, but you can't un-tap a link. See you in the next video.
