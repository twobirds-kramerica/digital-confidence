# Using iCloud Keychain
**Module:** Module 3: Passwords & Biometrics
**Duration Target:** 3-4 minutes
**Audience:** Ontario seniors aged 70+

---

## SCRIPT

**[OPEN]**
[NARRATOR] Welcome back. You're doing great.

**[VISUAL CUE: Warm title card — "Using iCloud Keychain"]**
[NARRATOR] In our last video we talked about why strong, unique passwords matter. But here's the honest problem: how is anyone supposed to remember a different strong password for every single account? The answer is — you don't have to. Apple has a built-in tool called iCloud Keychain that remembers all of them for you.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show a physical keychain with many keys, then an iPhone/iPad doing the same job digitally]**
[NARRATOR] Think of iCloud Keychain as a very secure key cabinet on your iPad. It stores all your usernames and passwords, locked behind your Face ID or your passcode. Only you can open it. And it automatically fills in your passwords when you need them — so you never have to type them.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show Settings → Passwords screen]**
[NARRATOR] To find Keychain, open Settings. Scroll down and tap "Passwords." You might need to use Face ID or your passcode to get in — that's the security working. Inside, you'll see a list of every account whose password your iPad has saved.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the list of saved passwords — website names and usernames visible]**
[NARRATOR] You can tap any entry in the list to see the username and password for that account. You can also edit or delete entries if something has changed. If you tap the eye icon, the password will appear on screen. This is useful if you need to use your password on a different device, like a computer.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show a login screen in Safari — username and password auto-fill with a yellow highlight]**
[NARRATOR] Here's where Keychain really shines. When you visit a website you've logged into before, your iPad will often offer to fill in your password automatically. A small suggestion will appear just above the keyboard — it shows your username and says "use password from Keychain." Tap it, and your details are filled in instantly.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show Safari suggesting a strong password when creating a new account]**
[NARRATOR] When you create a new account on a website, Safari will suggest a strong password for you — one of those long, random ones we talked about. It might look like something like "xK7d-mRtP-2qBn." You don't need to remember it. Tap "Use Strong Password" and your iPad saves it automatically.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show iCloud logo — syncing between iPhone and iPad]**
[NARRATOR] Because it's connected to your iCloud account, Keychain works across all your Apple devices. If you have both an iPhone and an iPad, a password you save on one will appear on the other. It keeps everything in sync.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show Settings → Passwords → AutoFill Passwords — toggle turned on]**
[NARRATOR] To make sure AutoFill is turned on, go to Settings, then "Passwords," then "Password Options." Make sure iCloud Passwords and Keychain is selected and the AutoFill switch is turned on. If it's already on, leave it as is.

**[VISUAL CUE: Text on screen — "Keychain = one master key that opens every lock"]**
[NARRATOR] The beauty of Keychain is that you only need to remember one thing: your iPad passcode or set up Face ID. Keychain handles everything else. Every other password — your email, your bank, your online shopping — is stored securely and filled in for you.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show a senior logging into a website quickly and easily — just a tap on the suggestion]**
[NARRATOR] If you haven't used Keychain before, start today. The next time you log into a website, tap the suggestion your iPad offers and let it save the password. After a few weeks, you'll have a collection of strong, unique passwords for all your accounts — and you won't have had to remember a single one.

**[VISUAL CUE: Text on screen — "Your passwords are safer in Keychain than on a sticky note"]**
[NARRATOR] Your passwords are safer in Keychain than written on a piece of paper, safer than saved in a note on your phone, and infinitely safer than reused across multiple sites. Apple's security for this is excellent.

**[CLOSE]**
[NARRATOR] Remember the 3-Second Rule: if something feels wrong, stop, breathe, and wait three seconds before tapping anything. You can always come back to something, but you can't un-tap a link. See you in the next video.
