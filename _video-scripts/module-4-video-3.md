# Spotting a Fake App
**Module:** Module 4: App Store Safety
**Duration Target:** 3-4 minutes
**Audience:** Ontario seniors aged 70+

---

## SCRIPT

**[OPEN]**
[NARRATOR] Welcome back. You're doing great.

**[VISUAL CUE: Warm title card — "Spotting a Fake App"]**
[NARRATOR] Even inside the App Store, it pays to be a little careful. While Apple reviews apps before listing them, fake or misleading apps do occasionally slip through. Today we're going to look at the warning signs that an app might not be what it claims to be.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show two banking apps side by side — one real TD Bank app, one suspicious imitation]**
[NARRATOR] Imagine you search for your bank's app and two results come up. One looks right. One looks almost right. How do you tell the difference? This is exactly what we're going to practise.

**[VISUAL CUE: Point to the developer name under the app title — highlight it]**
[NARRATOR] First, look at the developer name — the small text just below the app name in the search results. For TD Bank, it should say "TD Bank." For RBC, it should say "Royal Bank of Canada." If the developer name is something generic like "Top App Developer" or "Best Finance Tools," that is a warning sign.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show rating and number of reviews — e.g., 4.8 stars from 47,000 reviews vs 2.1 stars from 4 reviews]**
[NARRATOR] Next, look at the ratings. A legitimate app from a major Canadian bank will have tens of thousands of reviews. An app that was downloaded by very few people will have far fewer reviews, and often a lower rating. Low review count plus high star rating can also be suspicious — fake apps sometimes buy fake reviews.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show a fake app with screenshots that look blurry or amateurish]**
[NARRATOR] Tap on the app to open its detail page and look at the screenshots. A real app from a professional organisation will have polished, professional screenshots that clearly show how the app works. Blurry images, spelling mistakes in the description, or screenshots that don't quite match what a bank app should look like — these are red flags.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show an app description with typos and poor grammar highlighted]**
[NARRATOR] Read the description. A real app from a major institution will have clean, professional writing. If the description has spelling errors, awkward sentences, or makes grand promises — "best bank app in Canada, completely free forever!" — be suspicious.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show a fake app asking for unusual permissions — e.g., access to microphone, camera, contacts]**
[NARRATOR] Once an app is installed, watch what it asks for. A weather app asking for access to your contacts is strange. A flashlight app asking for access to your microphone is very suspicious. Legitimate apps only ask for permissions they genuinely need.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the App Store entry for a known real bank app — verified developer, thousands of reviews]**
[NARRATOR] Here's the safest approach for banking apps in particular. Don't search in the App Store first. Instead, go to your bank's official website on your iPad — type the address yourself, don't follow a link. On their website, they will have a link directly to their official app. That is the most reliable route.

**[VISUAL CUE: Text on screen — "When in doubt, call your bank"]**
[NARRATOR] And if you're ever genuinely unsure whether an app is legitimate — call your bank. The number is on the back of your card. Tell them you're looking for their app and ask them to confirm the exact name in the App Store. That call takes two minutes and saves you a lot of worry.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show how to delete an app — press and hold, tap "Remove App"]**
[NARRATOR] If you've downloaded an app and now you're not sure about it, deleting it is easy. Press and hold the icon until a small menu appears. Tap "Remove App," then "Delete App." Gone. No damage done.

**[VISUAL CUE: Summary card — "Developer name / Reviews / Description / Permissions = your checklist"]**
[NARRATOR] Your checklist: check the developer name, check the number of reviews, read the description, and watch what permissions it asks for. Those four steps will protect you from the vast majority of fake apps.

**[CLOSE]**
[NARRATOR] Remember the 3-Second Rule: if something feels wrong, stop, breathe, and wait three seconds before tapping anything. You can always come back to something, but you can't un-tap a link. See you in the next video.
