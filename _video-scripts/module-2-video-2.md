# The 3-Second Rule in Action
**Module:** Module 2: The Security Shield
**Duration Target:** 3-4 minutes
**Audience:** Ontario seniors aged 70+

---

## SCRIPT

**[OPEN]**
[NARRATOR] Welcome back. You're doing great.

**[VISUAL CUE: Warm title card — "The 3-Second Rule in Action"]**
[NARRATOR] Throughout these videos you've heard me mention the 3-Second Rule. Today we're going to look at it properly — what it is, why it works, and how to use it in real situations. This one habit can protect you better than almost anything else online.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the number "3" large on screen, then a slow countdown — 3, 2, 1]**
[NARRATOR] The 3-Second Rule is simple. When something unexpected appears on your screen — a pop-up, a message, a warning, a link — you stop. You breathe. You wait three seconds before you do anything. Three seconds. That's all.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show a scary-looking pop-up on an iPad — "YOUR DEVICE HAS A VIRUS! CALL IMMEDIATELY!"]**
[NARRATOR] Let's put it into practice. Imagine you're reading the news on your iPad and suddenly this appears. A red warning. Loud text. "Your device has a virus! Call this number immediately!" Your heart might skip a beat. That feeling is exactly what scammers are counting on.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show a hand about to tap the pop-up — then pulling back]**
[NARRATOR] Stop. Don't tap. Breathe. Count three seconds. One — two — three. Now let's think about this calmly.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show Apple logo — text: "Apple does not send virus warnings through websites"]**
[NARRATOR] Apple does not send virus warnings through websites. Your iPad does not display official warnings from inside a web browser. If a website is showing you a virus warning, it is not a real warning — it is a trick. Apple communicates with you through the Settings app, not through pop-ups.

**[VISUAL CUE: Demonstrate pressing the Home button to escape the pop-up]**
[NARRATOR] What do you do? Press Home. That gets you out of the browser immediately. If the browser itself seems stuck, force quit it. Problem solved without tapping a single scary link.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show a text message — "Your bank account has been locked. Tap here to verify."]**
[NARRATOR] Here's another scenario. You get a text message saying your bank account has been locked. There's a link to tap. This looks urgent. Your stomach drops.

**[VISUAL CUE: Same countdown — 3, 2, 1]**
[NARRATOR] Three-second rule. Stop. Breathe. Count.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show someone picking up the phone to call their bank directly]**
[NARRATOR] Your bank has a phone number printed on the back of your debit or credit card. Call that number. Don't tap the link in the message. The real bank will tell you if there's actually a problem with your account. Nine times out of ten, your account is perfectly fine.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show an email with urgent subject line — "ACTION REQUIRED — Your account expires today"]**
[NARRATOR] Same rule applies to emails. Any message that creates urgency — "act now," "expires today," "immediate action required" — is using a technique scammers rely on. Urgency short-circuits our thinking. The 3-Second Rule gives your thinking brain a chance to catch up to your startled reaction.

**[VISUAL CUE: Text on screen — "Urgency = Warning Sign"]**
[NARRATOR] Remember this: urgency itself is a warning sign. Legitimate companies and government agencies are not trying to panic you. Scammers are. Anytime you feel rushed, that is reason to slow down, not speed up.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show three scenarios side by side — each with a 3-second pause, then calm action]**
[NARRATOR] Three seconds gives you time to ask these questions: Did I expect this? Does this make sense? Can I verify this another way? Those three questions will protect you from the vast majority of scams.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show a relaxed senior confidently using their iPad]**
[NARRATOR] You are more capable of handling these situations than you realise. The 3-Second Rule isn't a trick — it's a reflex you're building. Every time you pause, you get better at it.

**[CLOSE]**
[NARRATOR] Remember the 3-Second Rule: if something feels wrong, stop, breathe, and wait three seconds before tapping anything. You can always come back to something, but you can't un-tap a link. See you in the next video.
