# Setting Up Face ID
**Module:** Module 3: Passwords & Biometrics
**Duration Target:** 3-4 minutes
**Audience:** Ontario seniors aged 70+

---

## SCRIPT

**[OPEN]**
[NARRATOR] Welcome back. You're doing great.

**[VISUAL CUE: Warm title card — "Setting Up Face ID"]**
[NARRATOR] Today we're going to set up something that many people find almost magical when they first use it — Face ID. This is a feature on newer iPads and iPhones that lets you unlock your device just by looking at it. No password to type, no PIN to remember. Just your face.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show an iPad being unlocked with Face ID — the lock icon appears then opens]**
[NARRATOR] Face ID uses a small camera at the top of your iPad to create a detailed map of your face. It does this once during setup, and after that it recognises you every time you look at your iPad. It works in the dark, with glasses, and even if you've changed your hairstyle.

**[VISUAL CUE: Text on screen — "Your face data never leaves your iPad"]**
[NARRATOR] Here is something important to know. Your face data is stored only on your iPad, in a secure part of the chip. It is never sent to Apple. It never goes to the internet. It cannot be accessed by any app. This is one of the most private and secure ways to protect your iPad.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the Settings app icon being tapped]**
[NARRATOR] Let's set it up. Open the Settings app — it's the grey icon that looks like a set of gears. Scroll down and tap "Face ID & Passcode."

**[VISUAL CUE: Show Settings → Face ID & Passcode screen]**
[NARRATOR] You'll be asked to enter your current passcode — the number you use to unlock your iPad. If you've never set one up, it will ask you to create one now. Type in those numbers.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the Face ID setup screen with the circle frame]**
[NARRATOR] Now tap "Set Up Face ID." You'll see a circle on the screen with a face outline. Hold your iPad at eye level, as if you're about to take a selfie. Keep it about 25 to 35 centimetres from your face — roughly the distance you'd hold a book to read it.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show the scanning animation — green arcs moving around the face outline]**
[NARRATOR] Slowly move your head in a circle — like you're tracing the edge of a clock with your nose. The green arcs on screen show the progress. Take your time. If your neck makes it difficult to rotate your head, you can tilt the iPad instead.

**[VISUAL CUE: "First scan complete — Now do it again" message appears]**
[NARRATOR] When the first scan is done, you'll be asked to do a second scan. This helps Face ID work at different angles — like when your iPad is flat on a table and you look down at it. Go through the motion again, nice and slowly.

**[VISUAL CUE: "Face ID is now set up" confirmation screen]**
[NARRATOR] And that's it. Face ID is set up. Now whenever you wake your iPad, just look at it — and it unlocks. You can also use it to confirm purchases in the App Store and Apple Pay if you set those up later.

**[VISUAL CUE: Demonstrate unlocking — press the side button, look at the screen, lock icon opens]**
[NARRATOR] Press the side button to wake your iPad. Look at it. You'll see the lock icon at the top briefly shimmer, then open. Swipe up from the bottom and you're in. It takes less than a second.

**[VISUAL CUE: Text on screen — "Tip: If it doesn't recognise you right away, just look again"]**
[NARRATOR] Sometimes Face ID doesn't recognise you on the very first try — maybe your angle was off or the light is unusual. If that happens, it will ask for your passcode. That is perfectly normal. Just type your passcode and carry on. Face ID learns from these moments and improves over time.

**[VISUAL CUE: Show a senior confidently picking up their iPad and it unlocking for them]**
[NARRATOR] Setting up Face ID means you never have to type a password to unlock your iPad, which makes you more likely to keep a strong, secure passcode — because you'll rarely have to type it manually.

**[CLOSE]**
[NARRATOR] Remember the 3-Second Rule: if something feels wrong, stop, breathe, and wait three seconds before tapping anything. You can always come back to something, but you can't un-tap a link. See you in the next video.
