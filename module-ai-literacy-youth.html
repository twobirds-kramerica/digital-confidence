AI in Real Life

Four real situations happening to young Canadians right now. What would you do?

Scenario 1 of 4
1
Academic Integrity
The Essay That Got Flagged
A Grade 10 student used an AI chatbot to write a paragraph for a history assignment. The teacher's AI detection tool flagged it. The student argued: "I didn't think it was cheating — I just used it to start."

What's the difference between using AI as a tool and having AI do your thinking for you?

The key distinction

AI is a tool — like a dictionary or a calculator. Using it to brainstorm, get unstuck, or understand a concept is a legitimate skill. Submitting AI-generated text as your own thinking is misrepresentation — the assignment is testing your analysis, not your ability to prompt an AI. Most schools are updating their policies, but the ethical line does not move: did you do the thinking?

2
Deepfakes & Verification
The Deepfake in the Group Chat
A video circulated in a school group chat showing the principal saying something embarrassing. It looked completely real. Students who shared it faced consequences — even though they didn't create it. An AI image tool made it in under five minutes.

What should you do before sharing something like this?

Sharing is amplifying

When you share something — even if you did not create it — your name goes on it. AI-generated images and videos can be created in minutes and look completely real. A 30-second search on CBC.ca, CTV News, or a basic Google search with "site:cbc.ca" can tell you whether something significant actually happened. If you cannot verify it in 30 seconds, do not share it. The original video got 4,000 shares. The correction got 80.

3
Access & Equity
The AI That Levelled the Playing Field
A 13-year-old with dyslexia found an AI text-to-speech tool that read books, articles, and assignments aloud to him. He started completing more reading than anyone in his class.

What does this example show about AI?

The equity angle

AI accessibility tools — text-to-speech, translation, real-time captioning, image description — remove barriers that have always existed for some learners. This is one of the most meaningful applications of AI: not replacing human capability, but giving everyone access to the same information regardless of how their brain processes it. The student's reading comprehension was never the issue. The format was.

4
Voice Cloning & Scams
The Call That Sounded Like Dad
A 16-year-old received a call that sounded exactly like her father saying he'd been in an accident and needed money transferred immediately. AI voice cloning used recordings from social media.

What is the correct response?

The voice clone rule

AI voice cloning can recreate anyone's voice from a few minutes of audio — and social media gives scammers a library of recordings to work from. The voice sounding real proves nothing. The correct response is always: hang up, call back on a number you already have saved. A real emergency will still be a real emergency in 60 seconds. Anyone who says there is no time to verify is using urgency as a weapon.
