# Ontario Trillium Foundation (OTF)
## Grant Application Template — Digital Confidence Centre

**Grant Program:** Ontario Trillium Foundation — Seed, Grow, or Resilient Communities grants
**Amount:** Seed: up to $50,000 (1–2 years) | Grow: $50,000–$150,000 (up to 3 years) | Resilient: varies
**Eligibility:** Registered charities and qualifying non-profit organizations in Ontario
**Application Portal:** otf.ca/our-grants
**Intake:** Rolling — no fixed deadline, but assessment takes 3–6 months
**Note:** OTF does not fund for-profit entities. A registered charity or non-profit partner is required.

---

## Which Grant Stream Fits DCC?

| Stream | Amount | Duration | Best Fit |
|--------|--------|----------|---------|
| Seed | Up to $50,000 | 1–2 years | First-time OTF applicant, new programme |
| Grow | $50,001–$150,000 | Up to 3 years | Scaling an established programme |
| Resilient Communities | Varies | Project-specific | Post-crisis recovery, infrastructure |

**Recommendation for DCC deployment:** Start with **Seed** grant for initial white-label deployment with a non-profit partner.

## Application Narrative Template

**Issue / Need:**

Ontario's seniors — particularly those aged 70+ — face accelerating digital exclusion as essential services migrate online. Banking, healthcare appointments, government benefit access, and social connection now require digital competence that many older Ontarians have not had opportunity to develop.

The consequences are severe:
- Millions lost annually to digital fraud targeting seniors
- Missed telehealth appointments due to inability to navigate video platforms
- Reduced access to OAS, GIS, and provincial benefit programmes (increasingly online-only)
- Social isolation as family connections move to social media and video calling

In [REGION], Ontario, [DESCRIBE LOCAL CONTEXT — number of seniors, specific gap, partner organization's reach].

**How the Project Addresses the Need:**

[ORGANIZATION NAME] will deploy the Digital Confidence Centre — a proven, accessible, Canadian-built digital literacy platform — reaching [X] seniors in [REGION] over [X] months.

The programme:
- Meets seniors where they are (no prior digital knowledge assumed)
- Uses Ontario-specific examples, resources, and phone numbers
- Is fully accessible (adjustable text, dyslexia font, dark mode, WCAG AA compliant)
- Protects privacy (no personal data collected or transmitted)
- Includes scam awareness as the first learning priority

**Outcomes:**

By the end of the grant period:
- [X] seniors will have completed at least 3 modules (primary metric)
- [X%] will report increased confidence with digital tools (self-assessment)
- [X] seniors will demonstrate fraud recognition skills (quiz completion)
- [X] seniors will successfully use at least one online service (banking, telehealth, or government) for the first time

## Budget Guidance

OTF reviews budget carefully for value and reasonableness. Include:

| Category | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| Platform costs (licence) | Clearly justified as "infrastructure" |
| Staff (up to 75% of Seed budget) | Coordinator salary or contract |
| Training and capacity building | Staff and volunteer training |
| Community engagement / outreach | How you'll reach seniors |
| Evaluation | How you'll measure outcomes |
| Indirect costs | OTF allows up to 15% overhead |

**Do not include:** Capital purchases over $1,000 without specific justification.

---

## OTF-Specific Tips

- **Use plain language.** OTF reviewers are community-focused, not technical.
- **Show community voice.** Include quotes or advisory input from seniors in your region.
- **Demonstrate sustainability.** How does the programme continue after OTF funding ends?
- **Be specific about geography.** OTF is regionally organised — know your region (e.g., Southwest Ontario, Central Ontario).
- **Attach financial statements** of the applying organization.
- **Letters of support** strengthen the application significantly.
