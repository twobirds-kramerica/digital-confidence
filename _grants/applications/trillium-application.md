## Section A — Project Summary (250 words)

The Digital Confidence Centre (DCC) is a free, bilingual digital literacy platform for Canadian seniors, currently serving learners across Southwestern Ontario through 19 self-paced modules covering foundational digital skills. The platform is WCAG 2.1 AA accessible, iPad-optimised, and available in English and French at no cost.

This project — **Confident and Connected: Expanding Digital Literacy Access for Seniors in Southwest Ontario** — will:

1. Expand the reach of DCC to underserved rural communities in Elgin, Oxford, and Middlesex counties through a structured partnership program with 20 community organisations
2. Deliver 36 facilitated learning sessions for seniors who benefit from supported (rather than fully independent) engagement with the platform
3. Create a sustainable self-delivery model by training 40 frontline workers and volunteers as DCC facilitators
4. Expand DCC content to address two underserved topic areas identified through user feedback: health technology (telehealth apps, health portals) and civic participation (government services, voter registration, local engagement online)

By project end, we project that 300 Southwestern Ontario seniors will have meaningfully engaged with DCC — building digital skills that reduce their isolation, improve their safety online, and enable them to participate more fully in their families, communities, and the province's digital economy.

The project will also produce a replicable facilitation model and a resource package that any community organisation in Ontario can adopt — extending impact well beyond the project's immediate geography.

## Section B — Community Need

**The problem:**
A 2024 National Institute on Ageing report identified social isolation as the most significant health risk facing Canadian seniors over 75, outpacing physical inactivity in health impact. Digital exclusion is both a cause and a consequence of this isolation: seniors who lack digital confidence are cut off from services, communication tools, and communities that increasingly exist primarily online.

In Southwestern Ontario, this problem is acute. Rural communities in Elgin and Oxford counties have limited access to in-person digital help services. The nearest public library in some communities is a 30-minute drive. Seniors who cannot afford transportation — or who rely on others for it — face compounding barriers to skill development.

The primary barrier is not device access. Most seniors in our region have received a smartphone or iPad from family members. The barrier is confidence. They do not trust themselves to "get it right," fear making costly mistakes, and have no safe, low-pressure environment to learn.

**Why existing programs don't solve it:**
- Most digital literacy programs for seniors are in-person only and concentrated in urban centres
- Digital programs that are available online assume a baseline skill level that many seniors do not have
- Most platforms are English-only; our region includes significant Francophone populations in some communities
- Most programs are not designed for the iPad, which is the dominant device among seniors we serve

**What DCC does differently:**
DCC was built from the ground up with seniors as the primary user. Every module was designed assuming no prior digital experience. The platform is self-paced, forgiving of mistakes, and structured around real scenarios seniors actually face — not abstract technology concepts. Brenda, our learner persona, is 72 years old, lives in St. Thomas, and just wants to video call her grandchildren.

## Section C — Project Description

**Goal:** By August 31, 2028, 300 seniors in Southwestern Ontario will have meaningfully engaged with the Digital Confidence Centre, and 20 community organisations will be equipped to continue delivering DCC programming independently.

### Activity 1 — Community Partnership Program

We will formally onboard 20 community partner organisations who will:
- Refer clients to DCC (all 20 partners)
- Facilitate structured group sessions using DCC modules (10 of 20 partners)
- Host a DCC access point — a dedicated device or designated space — for seniors without home internet (5 of 20 partners)

Partners will include public libraries, credit unions, community health centres, VON chapters, and senior centres across Elgin, Oxford, and Middlesex.

**Deliverable:** Partner onboarding package (DCC overview, facilitator guide, referral cards, module recommendation guide)

### Activity 2 — Facilitated Learning Sessions

We will deliver 36 facilitated learning sessions — typically 90 minutes, groups of 6–10 seniors — across the project period. Sessions will be hosted by partner organisations and facilitated by Aaron Patzalek or a trained co-facilitator.

Sessions will focus on the three module clusters most commonly requested by seniors and frontline workers:
- Online safety and scam recognition (Modules 1–5)
- Staying connected with family (Modules 7–8, 18)
- Daily life digital skills (Modules 10–11, 15–16)

**Deliverable:** 36 sessions delivered; session attendance records; pre/post confidence surveys

### Activity 3 — Facilitator Training Program

We will deliver 4 facilitator training workshops — one per quarter — for frontline workers and volunteers at partner organisations. Each workshop will be a half-day (3 hours) in-person session covering:
- How to navigate DCC and select appropriate modules
- How to facilitate a group session (including with participants with varying skill levels)
- How to support learners with accessibility needs
- How to handle common questions and technology problems

Trained facilitators will receive a complete printed Facilitator Kit including session guides, activity handouts, troubleshooting cards, and module summary sheets.

**Deliverable:** 40 trained facilitators; Facilitator Kit (print-ready PDF)

### Activity 4 — Content Expansion: Two New Module Clusters

Based on user feedback collected through our platform feedback form, two topic areas are consistently underserved in the existing 19-module curriculum:

**Health Technology (Modules 20–21):**
- Module 20: Your Health Online — using patient portals, booking appointments, accessing test results
- Module 21: Telehealth — video appointments with your doctor, setting up for success

**Civic Participation (Module 22):**
- Module 22: Your Voice Online — accessing government services, understanding local government online, voter information

These three modules will be developed in full — bilingual, WCAG 2.1 AA, iPad-optimised — following the same production standards as the existing 19 modules.

**Deliverable:** 3 new modules live on platform by March 2028

## Section E — Outcomes and Evaluation

**Immediate outcomes (by August 2028):**
- 300 seniors have meaningfully engaged with DCC (module completion or session attendance)
- 20 community organisations are actively referring clients to DCC
- 40 frontline workers and volunteers are trained and equipped to facilitate DCC sessions

- Seniors who completed DCC programming report increased digital confidence and reduced isolation
- Partner organisations continue delivering DCC-based programming without Aaron Patzalek's direct involvement
- DCC facilitation model is adopted by organisations outside the original project geography

**Evaluation approach:**
- Pre/post digital confidence survey (5-point self-assessment scale) — all workshop participants
- 30-day follow-up survey — sample of 30 workshop participants
- Partner satisfaction survey — end of project
- Platform analytics — unique learners, module completions, returning users during project period
- External evaluation summary — submitted with final report
