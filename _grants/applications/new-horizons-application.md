## Section 1 — Organisation Description

Two Birds Innovation is a social enterprise based in St. Thomas, Ontario, dedicated to building accessible technology tools for underserved communities. Our flagship project, the Digital Confidence Centre (DCC), is a free, bilingual digital literacy platform designed specifically for Canadian seniors.

Since launch, the Digital Confidence Centre has grown to include 19 self-paced learning modules covering foundational digital skills — from device safety and recognising online scams to video calling, online banking, and understanding artificial intelligence. The platform is:

- **Free** — no cost to learners or partner organisations
- **Bilingual** — fully available in English and French
- **Accessible** — WCAG 2.1 AA compliant, iPad-optimised, and designed for adults with limited prior technology experience
- **Self-paced** — learners can work through modules independently, at home, or supported by a facilitator

Our primary service area is Elgin County and the broader Thames Valley region of Southwestern Ontario, with learners accessing the platform through referrals from public libraries, community centres, and senior services organisations.

## Section 2 — Project Description

**Project Title:** Digital Confidence Centre — Community Outreach and Workshop Expansion (2027–2028)

**Project Overview:**

This project will expand the reach of the Digital Confidence Centre through three interconnected activities:

**Activity 1 — Community Partner Expansion**
We will formally onboard 15 new community partner organisations in Southwestern Ontario — including public libraries, credit unions, community health centres, and senior services organisations — who will refer clients to DCC and, where appropriate, integrate DCC modules into their existing programming.

**Activity 2 — Facilitated Workshop Series**
We will deliver 24 in-person facilitated workshops across Elgin, Middlesex, and Oxford counties, using DCC modules as the curriculum framework. Each workshop will be designed for groups of 6–10 seniors and delivered in partnership with a host organisation (e.g., a branch library or community centre). Workshops will cover the modules most requested by seniors and frontline workers: online safety, video calling, and using smartphones for daily life.

**Activity 3 — Facilitator Training and Resources**
To enable sustainable delivery beyond the project period, we will develop a facilitator training package — including a printed Facilitator Guide, a module-by-module activity kit, and a 3-hour training workshop — for staff and volunteers at partner organisations who wish to deliver DCC programming independently.

## Section 3 — How the Project Meets Program Objectives

The New Horizons for Seniors Program supports projects that encourage seniors to share their knowledge, skills, and experiences with others; support the social participation and inclusion of seniors; and help prevent and reduce social isolation among seniors.

This project addresses these objectives as follows:

**Reducing social isolation:**
A 2024 National Institute on Ageing report identified social isolation as the most significant health risk facing Canadian seniors over 75. Technology — when seniors have the confidence to use it — demonstrably reduces isolation through regular video calls with family, access to online communities, and connection to digital services. The Digital Confidence Centre directly addresses the primary barrier: not access to technology, but confidence in using it.

**Social participation and inclusion:**
Many seniors in our region are excluded from participating fully in civic, family, and community life because they lack digital skills that are increasingly assumed. A senior who cannot use email, video calling, or a smartphone is disadvantaged in scheduling medical appointments, staying in contact with family, and accessing community services. This project equips them to participate more fully.

**Knowledge sharing:**
The facilitator training component specifically positions seniors who complete the DCC curriculum as potential peer educators, enabling them to share their new skills with friends, neighbours, and community members — creating a multiplier effect that extends beyond the project's direct reach.

## Section 4 — Target Population

Our primary target population is Canadian seniors aged 65 and older living in Elgin, Middlesex, and Oxford counties in Southwestern Ontario. Within this group, we prioritise:

- Seniors living alone, particularly those in rural or small-town settings where digital access to services is increasingly critical
- Seniors who are recent recipients of smartphones or tablets (often as gifts from family) but lack the confidence to use them effectively
- Seniors who have experienced recent life transitions — retirement, bereavement, a move to a new community — that have reduced their social connections

Our facilitator training component will also benefit frontline workers and volunteers at partner organisations — typically 30–50 people across the project period — who will carry the skills and tools into their ongoing work with seniors.

## Section 7 — Evaluation

We will evaluate the project using the following measures:

**Output measures:**
- Number of partner organisations onboarded (target: 15)
- Number of workshops delivered (target: 24)
- Number of participants attending workshops (target: 200)
- Number of facilitators trained (target: 30)
- Number of unique learners accessing DCC during project period (tracked via platform analytics)

**Outcome measures:**
- Pre/post workshop survey: self-reported digital confidence score (5-point scale) before and after workshop
- Follow-up survey at 30 days: continued use of learned skills
- Partner feedback survey: partner satisfaction, module usefulness, facilitator readiness rating

**Reporting:**
We will submit a mid-term progress report in September 2027 and a final evaluation report in April 2028 in accordance with NHSP reporting requirements.

## Section 8 — Sustainability

The Digital Confidence Centre is designed for long-term sustainability without dependency on ongoing grant funding:

- The platform itself is free to host on GitHub Pages (no server costs)
- All 19 modules and bilingual content are complete and will remain available at no cost indefinitely
- Partner organisations trained in this project period will continue to deliver DCC programming using the facilitator resources developed — no ongoing Aaron Patzalek involvement required
- Future grant applications (NHSP, OTF, local community foundations) may support additional expansion phases, but the core resource will continue without them
