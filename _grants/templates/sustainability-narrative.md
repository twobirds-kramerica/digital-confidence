**Owner:** Two Birds Innovation

## How to Use This Template

Most grant funders want to know that a programme will continue after their funding ends. This document provides three scenarios and multiple language variants you can adapt to any application.

**Read the funder's guidelines first.** Some funders emphasise financial self-sufficiency; others want community ownership and integration. Choose the language that matches what the funder is asking for.

## Core Sustainability Statement (Standard Version)

*~200 words. Use as-is or as a base for customisation.*

---

Digital Confidence Centre is designed to be sustainable without ongoing grant dependency. The platform itself — a static, browser-based website hosted on GitHub Pages and Cloudflare — has negligible operational costs (under $100/year in hosting fees). Once developed, the content requires only periodic review and updates, which Two Birds Innovation is committed to providing as part of its ongoing operations.

The primary sustainability mechanism is a **white-label licensing model**. Libraries, credit unions, seniors' centres, and other community organisations can deploy the Digital Confidence Centre platform under their own branding, in exchange for an annual licence fee. This model is already in development and reflects conversations with potential partners in Southwestern Ontario and beyond. Revenue from white-label licences will cover the cost of platform maintenance, content updates, and participant support infrastructure — eliminating dependence on grant funding for ongoing operations.

Grant funding from [FUNDER NAME] is requested specifically to fund the initial community delivery, evaluation, and evidence-generation needed to demonstrate programme impact. This evidence base will, in turn, enable two-birds to negotiate white-label agreements with larger institutional partners, accelerating the transition to a self-sustaining revenue model.

Following the funded project period, [PARTNER ORGANISATION] commits to continuing to refer participants to Digital Confidence Centre through its existing community programmes, at no additional cost to the programme.

## White-Label Revenue Model Explanation

*For applications that require a detailed financial sustainability narrative.*

---

The white-label model works as follows:

**The Product:** Two Birds Innovation has developed and maintains the Digital Confidence Centre platform — a fully built digital literacy programme with 11+ modules, assessments, and participant support materials. This represents a significant prior investment in content development, accessibility design, and technical infrastructure.

**The Model:** Rather than charging individual participants (which would exclude the seniors most in need), Two Birds licences the programme to institutions that serve large senior populations and have existing trust relationships with their clients. Licensees pay an annual fee to deploy Digital Confidence Centre under their brand name, with white-label customisation of colours, logos, and organisation-specific content.

**Target licensees:**
- Public library systems (Ontario: 800+ branch locations)
- Credit unions (Canada: 300+ institutions with combined membership of 5M+)
- Provincial senior services networks
- YMCA and YWCA systems
- Municipal parks and recreation departments with senior programming

**Pricing approach:** Annual licence fees are tiered by organisation size and reach. Smaller community organisations (e.g., county library systems serving under 50,000 residents) would pay in the range of $1,500–$5,000/year. Larger provincial or national systems would pay $10,000–$50,000/year, reflecting the scale of their deployment and the value of the programme to their organisational mandate.

**Financial projections:** Securing 10 small to mid-sized licence agreements would generate $30,000–$80,000 annually — sufficient to cover platform maintenance, content review, and a part-time programme support role without grant dependency. This is a realistic target within a 2–3 year horizon from the current beta phase.

This model aligns with established practice in the EdTech sector, where free-to-end-user educational products are supported by institutional licensing — a model used successfully by platforms such as Khan Academy (US), Code.org, and numerous Canadian library technology providers.

## Three Sustainability Scenarios

### Scenario 1 — Minimal (Licence Renewals Only)

*Use for: Small grants, early-stage applications, conservative funders*

Following the funded project period, the Digital Confidence Centre platform will remain freely available to all participants at no cost. Two Birds Innovation will maintain the platform through its standard business operations, covering hosting costs (under $100/year) through existing revenue streams.

Institutional access will be formalised through low-cost annual licence agreements with [PARTNER ORG] and any other community organisations who wish to promote the programme to their clients. These agreements will, at minimum, cover operational costs and enable Two Birds Innovation to continue content updates and participant support.

The programme does not require continued grant funding to remain available and operational. Participant access is free and unconditional. Grant funding accelerates reach and quality; it is not a dependency.

---

### Scenario 2 — Moderate (Expanded Licensing)

*Use for: Medium grants, foundations interested in social enterprise, OTF, corporate funders*

The funded project period will produce the programme data and participant evidence base necessary to approach larger institutional partners — public library systems, credit unions, and provincial seniors' networks — with a compelling value proposition for white-label partnership.

Two Birds Innovation projects that by the end of the funded period, [PARTNER ORG] and Two Birds will jointly approach [NUMBER] additional organisations in [REGION / PROVINCE] about deploying DCC under white-label licence arrangements. Conservative licensing revenue from 5–10 small to mid-sized agreements would generate $15,000–$40,000 annually — sufficient to sustain the programme's operational costs and continue content development without further grant dependency.

[PARTNER ORGANISATION] will continue to refer senior participants to the programme through its existing community touchpoints (newsletter, bulletin boards, programming), at no incremental cost. The programme will remain free to all end users indefinitely.

---

### Scenario 3 — Ambitious (Provincial Scale)

*Use for: Large grants, government funders, Google.org, investors*

The long-term sustainability vision for Digital Confidence Centre is provincial and eventually national scale through the white-label licensing model.

Phase 1 (Years 1–2): Establish programme evidence base through beta delivery and early community partnerships. Secure first 2–5 white-label licence agreements with library systems and credit unions in Southwestern Ontario.

Phase 2 (Years 3–4): Expand licensing to Ontario Public Library Service network and Credit Union Central of Ontario. Target 20–30 institutional licensees covering 500,000+ seniors across Ontario. Revenue at this scale ($150,000–$400,000 annually) sustains a fully staffed programme team and continuous content development.

Phase 3 (Years 5+): National expansion through provincial library federations and Credit Union Central of Canada. French-language programme delivery through Quebec and Francophone Ontario licence partners. At 100+ licensees, the programme achieves financial independence and can begin cross-subsidising free access for under-resourced communities through a solidarity pricing model.

This is not a grant-dependent model. Grant funding in the early stages is investment capital — it funds the evidence generation and organisational development needed to build a self-sustaining enterprise delivering free, accessible digital literacy to millions of Canadian seniors.

## Language Variations for Different Funder Requirements

### When the funder wants "financial sustainability"

> "Following the grant period, Digital Confidence Centre will be financially sustained through white-label institutional licensing agreements. [PARTNER ORG] will continue to provide participant referrals at no cost. Platform hosting costs are negligible ($100/year). No continued grant funding is required to maintain programme availability."

### When the funder wants "community ownership"

> "Digital Confidence Centre is designed to become a community resource owned and advocated for by the organisations that use it. Through the white-label partnership model, institutions like [PARTNER ORG] develop an ongoing stake in the programme's success — they brand it, they promote it, and their clients rely on it. This distributed ownership model is more sustainable than dependency on a single organisation or a continued grant stream. The programme will outlast any individual funding relationship."

### When the funder wants "institutional embedding"

> "Following the project period, [PARTNER ORG] intends to formally integrate Digital Confidence Centre into its standard senior programming offerings — including mention in new member orientations, annual programme calendars, and staff training. This institutional embedding ensures the programme remains accessible to [PARTNER ORG]'s senior community as a standard, ongoing resource — not a one-time project."

### When the funder wants "fee-for-service after grant"

> "Upon completion of the funded project period, [PARTNER ORG] will enter into a low-cost annual programme access agreement with Two Birds Innovation ($[AMOUNT]/year). This fee covers ongoing platform maintenance, content updates, and participant support. This cost has been incorporated into [PARTNER ORG]'s programme budget for the following fiscal year, confirming its commitment to continued programme delivery."

## Important Notes on Transparency

Two Birds Innovation is a **for-profit entity**. Some funders will ask directly about this. Be prepared with this response:

> "Two Birds Innovation is a for-profit technology company. The Digital Confidence Centre programme is delivered in partnership with [PARTNER ORG], a registered charity / non-profit, which serves as the formal applicant for this grant. Two Birds provides programme development and delivery services in exchange for contracted service fees, which are included in the project budget. The white-label revenue model, if successful, will generate revenue for Two Birds Innovation as a social enterprise — enabling the company to continue investing in accessible technology for seniors without indefinite grant dependency."

This is not a liability — it is a feature. Funders who understand social enterprise and hybrid models will see this as more sustainable than pure charity dependency. Frame it accordingly.
