### Activities

*What we do with those inputs*

**Programme Delivery**
- Recruit senior participants (70+) through partner organisation's existing networks
- Administer pre-programme digital confidence survey
- Conduct welcome phone call with each participant (Day 2)
- Provide access to self-paced learning modules (15–20 minutes each)
- Send structured check-in communications (Days 7, 14, 21, 28)
- Administer mid-programme and post-programme feedback forms
- Offer optional 30-minute individual support calls
- Issue completion certificates

**Participant Support**
- Direct phone and email support for any access difficulties
- Assisted access sessions (at partner facility, if needed)
- Volunteer peer support (where applicable)

**Outreach and Engagement**
- Print and digital recruitment materials
- Partner organisation bulletin boards, newsletters, and direct referrals
- Social media outreach in target communities

**Evaluation and Learning**
- Collect pre/post confidence survey data
- Analyse module completion rates and quiz performance
- Compile qualitative feedback from open-ended survey questions
- Conduct optional post-programme participant interviews
- Prepare funder reports and programme improvement recommendations

### Short-Term Outcomes

*Changes we expect to see during and immediately after the programme (0–3 months)*

- **Increased digital confidence:** Participants report higher self-assessed confidence in using their iPhone or iPad safely (measured by pre/post survey)
- **Improved fraud awareness:** Participants can identify common digital scams and describe steps to protect themselves
- **Reduced anxiety:** Participants report feeling less overwhelmed or anxious about using their device
- **New skill acquisition:** Participants demonstrate ability to perform at least 2–3 previously unfamiliar digital tasks
- **Increased help-seeking:** Participants know where and how to get help when they encounter an unfamiliar situation online

---

### Medium-Term Outcomes

*Changes we expect 3–12 months after programme completion*

- **Financial independence:** Participants use online banking, e-transfers, or digital payment systems independently and safely without family assistance
- **Reduced isolation:** Participants use digital tools to maintain social connections (video calls, messaging, email) more regularly
- **Independent access to services:** Participants independently access healthcare appointments (telehealth), government services (MyServiceCanada, Ontario health portal), and online shopping
- **Reduced family caregiver burden:** Participants' family members report fewer requests for basic technology assistance
- **Sustained learning:** Participants continue exploring new digital capabilities beyond the modules covered in the programme

---

### Long-Term Outcomes

*Broader changes we expect to contribute to over 2–5 years, across all participants and cohorts*

- **Sustained digital participation:** A measurable increase in the proportion of seniors 70+ in [REGION] who independently use digital services for essential daily needs
- **Reduced fraud losses:** Decline in self-reported fraud incidents and financial losses among programme alumni communities
- **Reduced digital exclusion:** Seniors who previously identified as "non-users" of technology move to independent or assisted digital engagement
- **Healthcare system benefits:** Increased uptake of telehealth and digital health tools among seniors, reducing barriers to care access
- **Intergenerational trust:** Seniors and their families report reduced tension around technology use; seniors feel more capable and less dependent
- **Programme scale:** DCC's white-label model enables [N] partner organisations to deploy the programme to their own senior communities, multiplying reach without proportional resource increase

---

### External Factors and Assumptions

*Conditions outside our control that affect whether the logic model holds*

**Assumptions we are making:**
- Participants have or can access an iPhone or iPad and a reliable internet connection
- Participants are motivated to improve their digital skills (programme is self-selected)
- Partner organisation has established trust with the target population
- Programme content remains relevant as technology evolves (requires regular review)
- Participants can engage with self-paced learning without significant in-person facilitation

**External factors that could affect outcomes:**
- Device or internet access barriers for very low-income participants
- Health events or life disruptions interrupting programme completion
- Rapid changes in technology (new iOS versions, new scam types) that outpace programme content
- Funder requirements changing mid-project
- Partner organisation capacity constraints (staff turnover, competing priorities)

**Mitigating strategies:**
- Welcome call (Day 2) confirms access before participation formally begins
- Flexible timeline and no-pressure completion model reduces dropout from life disruptions
- Programme content reviewed and updated annually; new modules added as technology landscape changes
- Regular communication with partner organisation to monitor capacity and flag early concerns
