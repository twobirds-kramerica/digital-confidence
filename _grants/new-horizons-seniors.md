## Federal Grant Application Template — Digital Confidence Centre

**Grant Program:** New Horizons for Seniors Program
**Administering Department:** Employment and Social Development Canada (ESDC)
**Amount:** Community-based projects up to $25,000 (single year); Pan-Canadian up to $5M
**Application Portal:** canada.ca/en/employment-social-development/services/new-horizons-seniors.html
**Intake:** Two rounds per year — typically spring and fall

## Project Description Template

**Project Title:** Digital Confidence Centre — Community Digital Literacy for Elgin County Seniors

**Project Summary (500 words max):**

The Digital Confidence Centre is a free, self-paced digital literacy programme designed specifically for Ontario seniors aged 70 and above. The programme addresses one of the most significant barriers to senior independence, safety, and social participation in 2026: the rapid shift of essential services to digital platforms.

Our 15-module curriculum teaches seniors to:
- Recognise and avoid digital fraud (protecting the $1.6B/year lost to elder scams in Canada)
- Use online banking, telehealth, and government services independently
- Connect with family through social media and video calling
- Navigate their devices with confidence rather than fear

The programme has been designed with extensive input from Ontario seniors, and all content has been tested for accessibility and clarity with adults aged 70+. Key features include:
- 4-level adjustable font size
- Dark mode and dyslexia-friendly font options
- Canadian examples and Ontario-specific resources throughout
- No account creation, no personal data collection
- Fully accessible via any smartphone, tablet, or computer

In this project cycle, we will deploy the Digital Confidence Centre as a white-label service for [PARTNER ORGANISATION NAME], reaching approximately [X] seniors across [REGION], Ontario.

**Project Activities:**
1. Customise the Digital Confidence Centre platform with partner organisation branding (Month 1)
2. Train [X] staff members and volunteers to support programme participants (Month 1)
3. Conduct [X] group learning sessions at [VENUES] (Months 2–10)
4. Provide ongoing individual support for participants (Months 2–12)
5. Collect and analyse outcome data; prepare final report (Month 12)

## Objectives and Outcomes Template

**Objective 1:** Increase digital literacy confidence among senior participants

*Measurable target:* 80% of participants who complete at least 3 modules report increased confidence in using digital tools (measured by pre/post self-assessment)

**Objective 2:** Reduce vulnerability to digital fraud

*Measurable target:* 75% of participants demonstrate ability to identify common fraud tactics (measured by Module 2 quiz completion with passing score)

**Objective 3:** Improve access to online services

*Measurable target:* 60% of participants report successfully accessing at least one online service (banking, telehealth, or government services) by programme end

**Objective 4:** Reduce social isolation through digital connection

*Measurable target:* 50% of participants report connecting with family or friends through digital tools who they were not previously connected with

## Key Messages for Reviewers

Use these throughout your application narrative:

- **Urgency:** The digitisation of essential services has accelerated dramatically since 2020. Seniors who cannot navigate digital tools face real barriers to healthcare access, banking, government benefits, and social connection.
- **Evidence:** Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre data confirms $1.6B+ annual losses to fraud, with seniors disproportionately targeted. Digital literacy is the most effective preventive intervention.
- **Reach:** The programme is self-paced and online, extending reach beyond in-person sessions.
- **Sustainability:** After initial deployment, the platform operates at minimal ongoing cost. Partner organizations can maintain the programme with volunteer support after the grant period.
- **Canadian content:** All resources, phone numbers, and examples are Ontario-specific.

---

## Common Reasons for Rejection (Avoid These)

- Vague outcome metrics ("participants will feel better") — be specific and measurable
- No senior involvement in project design or governance
- Overlapping with another funded NHSP project at the same organisation
- Budget items not justified or clearly connected to activities
- Missing partnership letters
