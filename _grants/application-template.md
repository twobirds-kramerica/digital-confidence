## Section 2 — Project Description

### What We Are Building / Have Built

Digital Confidence Centre is an online learning platform designed specifically for older adults in Ontario. It teaches smartphone safety, scam recognition, online banking, video calling, and other essential digital skills — all in plain language, with large text, high contrast, and a pace suited to people who didn't grow up with this technology.

The platform currently includes [X] interactive learning modules, a Quick Answers resource library, and a Scam Awareness hub. It is free to access, mobile-first, and bilingual in English and French.

### The Problem We Are Solving

[Customise this paragraph for each grant's framing — examples below:]

**For seniors-focused grants:** Older adults in Ontario are disproportionately excluded from digital life — not because of unwillingness, but because most digital tools were not designed for them. This exclusion leads to social isolation, reduced access to healthcare and government services, and heightened vulnerability to financial fraud. In [region], [X]% of adults 65+ report lacking confidence with smartphones and online services.

**For financial literacy grants:** Financial fraud targeting older Canadians costs an estimated $[X] billion annually. Digital Confidence Centre's banking and scam modules directly address this gap — teaching older adults to recognise phishing, protect their banking credentials, and use online banking safely.

**For digital inclusion grants:** The digital divide is not primarily a connectivity problem — it is a confidence and literacy problem. Infrastructure investments bring broadband to communities; Digital Confidence Centre gives residents the skills to use it safely and effectively.

## Section 6 — Sustainability

Digital Confidence Centre is designed to be self-sustaining beyond this grant period through two revenue streams:

1. **White-label licensing:** Libraries, credit unions, and community organisations can licence the platform under their own brand for an annual fee. This model is currently in development and will generate ongoing revenue to support platform maintenance.

2. **B2B partnerships:** Organisations that wish to offer the platform as a staff benefit or member resource pay a licensing fee that subsidises free public access.

The public-facing version of Digital Confidence Centre will remain free to all Ontario residents indefinitely.
