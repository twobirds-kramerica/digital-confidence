## Section 4: Community Needs Assessment

### Ontario Context

- **Aging population:** Ontario's population of adults 65+ is projected to reach 4.1 million by 2030, representing 25% of the province's total population (Statistics Canada, 2022).
- **Digital divide:** 42% of Canadians aged 75+ have never used the internet (CRTC, 2023). Among those who do, confidence and safety remain significant barriers.
- **Fraud vulnerability:** Seniors account for 30% of fraud victims but up to 67% of total financial losses — an estimated $1.6 billion annually across Canada (Competition Bureau Canada, 2023).
- **Digital exclusion from essential services:** Banking, healthcare appointments, government services, and social connection have increasingly moved online. Seniors without digital confidence are excluded from these essential services.

### Elgin County / Southwestern Ontario Context

- Elgin County's population is proportionally older than the provincial average, with approximately 22% of residents aged 65 or older (Statistics Canada, 2021 Census).
- Rural geography means many seniors face transportation barriers to in-person services and programmes. Digital access is increasingly critical for independent living.
- St. Thomas, the county seat, serves as the regional hub for social services, but satellite communities (Aylmer, Dutton, Rodney, Shedden) have limited access to in-person digital literacy programming.
- Local library services report consistently high demand for basic technology assistance, which staff are unable to fully meet with existing resources.

### Identified Gaps

1. Existing digital literacy programmes (e.g., CARP, library workshops) are episodic, in-person, and inaccessible to seniors with mobility limitations or rural residency.
2. Commercial training resources (YouTube tutorials, manufacturer guides) are designed for tech-confident users, not seniors encountering these concepts for the first time.
3. No programme exists in Elgin County specifically designed for seniors 70+ using iPhone or iPad — the dominant device category among seniors who do own a smartphone.
4. Caregiver and family support for digital literacy is inconsistent; seniors should not rely on family availability to navigate essential services.

[PARTNER ORGANISATION], in partnership with Two Birds Innovation, proposes to deliver the **Digital Confidence Centre Community Digital Literacy Programme** to [TARGET NUMBER] adults aged 70 and older in [REGION] between [START DATE] and [END DATE].

Digital Confidence Centre (DCC) is a free, accessible, self-paced online learning platform developed specifically for older Canadians who use an iPhone or iPad. Unlike traditional digital literacy programmes — which typically require in-person attendance and assume a level of existing comfort with technology — DCC is designed from the ground up for seniors who are starting from the beginning. Every lesson is written in plain language, structured in short segments of 15–20 minutes, and tested for accessibility at high zoom levels and large text settings.

The programme addresses the topics seniors identify as most pressing: recognising and avoiding phone and email scams, using online banking safely, creating and managing passwords, understanding privacy settings, and navigating the apps most commonly used for healthcare, shopping, and staying in touch with family. Each module includes a brief comprehension check and a feedback form, allowing participants to reinforce their learning and contribute to ongoing programme improvement.

## Section 9: Evaluation Plan

### Data Collection Tools

**Pre-Programme Digital Confidence Survey (5 questions, 5-point scale)**
Administered at enrolment:
1. How confident do you feel using your iPhone or iPad to browse the internet? (1–5)
2. How confident do you feel recognising a scam or fraud attempt online or by phone? (1–5)
3. How confident do you feel using online banking or e-transfers safely? (1–5)
4. How confident do you feel managing your passwords? (1–5)
5. How confident do you feel asking for help when you're unsure about something on your device? (1–5)

**Mid-Programme Check-In (3 questions)**
Administered at 2-week mark:
1. Have you had any difficulty accessing the programme or completing a module?
2. Is there a topic you'd like to see explained differently?
3. How would you rate your experience so far? (1–5)

**Post-Programme Survey**
Same 5 questions as pre-survey, plus:
- What was the most useful thing you learned?
- Would you recommend this programme to a friend or family member? (Yes/No)
- Any other feedback for the programme team?

### Platform Data

- Module completion rates (pulled from platform analytics)
- Average time spent per module
- Quiz pass rates per module

### Reporting to Funder

- Interim report at [MONTH 6]: enrolment numbers, completion rates, mid-survey summary
- Final report at programme close: full outcomes data, pre/post confidence comparison, quotes/testimonials (with participant consent), sustainability summary

## Section 11: Tips Specific to Ontario Seniors Secretariat Applications

1. **Use the word "seniors" consistently** — the Secretariat's language does not prefer "older adults." Align your language with the funder's terminology throughout the narrative.

2. **Quantify everything you can.** The Secretariat responds well to specific numbers: projected participants, hours of programming, improvement percentages. Avoid vague language like "many seniors" or "significant impact."

3. **Emphasise Ontario-specific context.** Reference Ontario's aging population projections, Ontario-specific fraud statistics, and local/regional needs. Generic Canadian statistics are less persuasive than Ontario or regional data.

4. **Name your community clearly.** The OSCG prioritises community-based impact. Be specific: "Elgin County," "St. Thomas," "Southwestern Ontario" — not "rural Ontario" in general.

5. **Address sustainability upfront.** The Secretariat wants to fund projects that have a life beyond the grant. Even in the project description, briefly explain how the programme will continue after funding ends. Reference the sustainability plan (see `_grants/templates/sustainability-narrative.md`).

6. **Disability and accessibility language matters.** Frame digital exclusion as an accessibility and inclusion issue, not just a skills gap. This resonates with the Ministry for Seniors and Accessibility's dual mandate.

7. **Include in-kind contributions.** A strong in-kind line demonstrates community commitment and makes your project appear more financially sound. Two Birds' prior platform investment ($15,000+) is a legitimate in-kind contribution.

8. **Apply early.** The OSCG is competitive and receives many applications from larger established organisations. Submitting 4–6 weeks before the deadline gives you time to respond to any initial feedback or requests for clarification.

9. **Get your partner org on board early.** The formal applicant needs time to review and approve the application. Build in at least 4 weeks for partner review and signature.

10. **Proofread for Canadian English.** The Secretariat is a provincial government body. "Programme," "Centre," "organisation," "labour," "colour" — consistent Canadian English spelling signals professionalism and attention to detail.
