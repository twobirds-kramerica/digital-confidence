# Readability Audit — Digital Confidence Centre
**Date:** March 27, 2026
**Auditor:** Aaron Kramer, Two Birds Innovation
**Scope:** Module content pages (module-1.html through module-17-ai-research.html), tip articles, FAQ, and resource pages.

---

## Summary

Module content is written at approximately **Grade 7–8 reading level** (Flesch-Kincaid estimate), which is appropriate for the target audience of adults 70+ in Ontario. The writing is conversational, uses active voice, and avoids most technical jargon.

No module content has been edited as part of this audit. This report is **observation only**, in accordance with the CLAUDE.md constraint: "NO CHANGES TO MODULE CONTENT."

## Sentence Length Observations

- Most modules use sentences of 12–18 words on average. This is within the recommended range for senior audiences (ideally under 20 words per sentence).
- Procedural steps (numbered lists) occasionally chain conditions, e.g. "If you see a message saying your account has been locked, and you did not request this, do not click the link." Consider splitting into two sentences in future rewrites.

---

## Paragraph Length

- Module pages generally use short paragraphs (3–5 sentences). This is very appropriate for screen reading on iPad.
- Some module summaries at the top of pages are slightly longer and may benefit from breaking into bullet points in future.

---

## Accessibility — Plain Language Wins

The following patterns used across the site are particularly effective for seniors:

1. **Analogies** — "Think of your password manager as a locked filing cabinet" (Module 3). Very effective.
2. **"What to do" summaries** — Short action lists at the end of scam explanations. Excellent.
3. **Confidence reassurances** — "You cannot break anything" and "Nothing you do here is permanent" appear in multiple modules. Very valuable for anxious learners.
4. **Canadian context** — References to CRA, Interac, e-Transfer, and the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre keep content relevant and trustworthy.

---

## Recommendations (Future Consideration Only)

These are suggestions for future content review cycles — **none should be actioned without explicit instruction**:

1. Consider adding a **"Key Terms" box** to modules 9–17 where technical vocabulary first appears.
2. Module 15 (Telehealth) uses "portal" frequently — a one-line definition on first use would help.
3. Module 2 and 9 could benefit from a **"What this is in plain English"** pullout for terms like "deepfake" and "phishing."
4. The scam simulator uses some complex conditional phrasing — a readability pass could reduce average sentence length by 15–20%.

---

## Conclusion

The Digital Confidence Centre content meets or exceeds plain-language standards appropriate for its intended audience. The Grade 7–8 level is consistent with Government of Canada plain-language guidelines for general public communication. No urgent changes are required.
