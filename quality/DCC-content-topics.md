## TOPIC: "Feels special, but isn't" — gimmicks & pressure tactics (NOT scams)
*Added 2026-07-03 from Aaron. Example he shared: a Temu/TikTok "spin the wheel — Get All / Coupon / Credit / Free Gift, CA$1,000+ value" prize wheel with a Stop button and fine print ("App users only, with qualifying orders, for illustrative purpose").*

**The gap this fills:** DCC (and most seniors' programs) teach **scams** = illegal fraud (someone steals your money or data). This is different: **legal but manipulative marketing** — dark patterns engineered to make you *feel* you've won or that time is running out, to hook you into an app, a purchase, a subscription, or an upsell. Aaron's mother (and many seniors) are drawn to exactly these. **Few programs teach this — so it's both a real protection gap AND a DCC differentiator.**

**The skill to teach (calm, empowering, not fear):** *pause before the hook.* Ask: "Is this real value, or a feeling of winning / urgency built to get me to spend?" Then: close it, don't tap, and if unsure, ask a trusted person.

**Tells to teach (concrete, current, not dated):**
- **Fake prizes / "you've won":** spin wheels, scratch cards, "you're our 1,000th visitor," a big dollar "value" that is actually coupons/credit needing "qualifying orders."
- **Manufactured urgency:** countdown timers, "only 2 left," "offer ends in 5:00," flashing banners.
- **The fine print tell:** "app users only," "with qualifying orders," "for illustrative purpose," "value may contain." Real deals do not hide behind that.
- **The trap after the hook:** free-then-charged trials, hard-to-cancel subscriptions, endless upsells, "just pay shipping."

**Why it belongs in DCC:** it extends the "protect" mission from fraud to *everyday manipulation*, it is timeless enough not to date (the tactics persist even as brands change), and it pairs naturally with the scam-practice / Helper-mode format — a "spot the gimmick" practice alongside "spot the scam."

**Framing / words to use:** "gimmicks and pressure tactics," "hooks," "feels special but isn't," "real value vs. a feeling." AVOID implying the senior is foolish for being drawn in — the whole point is these are *professionally engineered* to work on everyone.

**Build note:** a strong candidate for a second practice lesson (after "Spot the Scam"): "Spot the Gimmick" — same safe-practice model, a pretend "you've won" screen, choose what you'd do, calm feedback. PARK for after the core rebuild; captured here so it is not lost.
