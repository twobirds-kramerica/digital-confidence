## EMAIL 2 — Day 4: The Case for Digital Literacy Training

**Subject:** The cost of digital exclusion for seniors — and what your organisation can do about it

**Preview text:** The gap is growing. The solution is closer than you think.

---

Hello [First Name],

A quick follow-up, and a few numbers worth considering.

According to Statistics Canada, seniors are the fastest-growing demographic of internet users in Canada — yet they are also the most likely targets of online fraud. In 2023, Canadians over 60 reported more than $200 million in fraud losses to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre. Many more cases go unreported.

Beyond fraud, digital exclusion creates real quality-of-life costs. Seniors who cannot confidently use digital tools are more likely to:

- Become socially isolated as communication moves online
- Miss health information, appointment bookings, and government services
- Depend on family members for basic tasks, eroding independence

Organisations like yours are on the front line of this. Staff and volunteers regularly field questions about technology. Family members come to you seeking guidance. The need is there — but dedicated resources are hard to find.

The Digital Confidence Centre was built to fill exactly this gap. It doesn't require staff training, classroom setup, or any technology beyond a tablet. It can be shared with residents or clients as a self-serve resource, or used as a facilitated learning tool alongside staff or volunteers.

## EMAIL 3 — Day 8: What Results Look Like

**Subject:** What actually changes when seniors have digital confidence

**Preview text:** Real outcomes. Not vanity metrics.

---

Hello [First Name],

What does success look like when an older adult completes a digital literacy programme?

From our beta cohort and early users, here's what we've observed:

**Reduced anxiety around technology.** Users consistently report feeling less worried about "breaking something" after completing even one module. That confidence shift is significant — it's often what keeps seniors from exploring digital tools at all.

**Better scam awareness.** Beta testers who completed Module 2 reported they felt better equipped to identify phone and email scams. Several mentioned applying what they'd learned to real situations within days of the module.

**Increased independence.** Users describe being able to do things on their own that they previously needed to ask family for — checking email, using the App Store safely, reviewing their bank balance. The reduction in dependency is meaningful for both seniors and the families supporting them.

**For your organisation specifically**, these outcomes translate to:

- Fewer "can you help me with my iPad?" requests on staff time
- Clients who feel more confident and self-sufficient
- A demonstrable contribution to digital inclusion — increasingly a priority for funders

We're actively collecting outcome data to support grant applications. If your organisation partners with us, we can share aggregate data that may support your own reporting.
