📱 Device basics

The Escape Hatch

⏱ About 10 minutes, at your own pace

✅ You are in a safe place. Nothing on this page can harm your device.

The single most important skill in using any device: how to get back to safety, no matter what happens on your screen.

Margaret's story

Margaret, 74, was browsing recipes on her iPad one Tuesday morning. She tapped a link that promised the "Best Butter Tart Recipe in Ontario," and suddenly her screen filled with flashing colours and a loud voice saying her iPad had a virus. Her heart raced. She could not find a way to close it, and she thought her iPad was broken, or worse, that someone was stealing her banking information right that second.

Here is the good news: Margaret's iPad was perfectly fine the entire time. That scary message was a fake, a common trick designed to frighten people. If Margaret had known the one skill we are about to learn, she could have closed that fake warning in about two seconds and gone right back to her butter tart recipe.

💬
From your coach

If you have ever frozen when a strange message appeared, you are not doing anything wrong. That reaction is exactly what those messages are built to cause. By the end of this page you will have a calm, reliable way out of any of them, and you will never have to hide your device in a drawer again.

The golden rule: nothing is permanent

The most comforting truth about your device

You cannot break your iPad or phone by tapping the wrong thing. You cannot accidentally delete the internet. You cannot ruin your device by opening the wrong app. Almost everything you do can be undone, closed, or fixed.

Think of your device like a library. When you open an app, it is like picking a book off the shelf. When you close it, you are putting the book back. The book is still there. You have not changed anything permanent.

Escape hatch number 1: the Home button (or Home gesture)

Your device has one primary escape route. On an iPhone or iPad it is the Home button or the swipe-up gesture:

If your iPad has a round button below the screen

1

Look at the very bottom edge, below the screen. You will see a round, slightly indented circle. That is your Home button.
2

Press it once with your thumb or finger. Not hard, a gentle, firm press.
3

Whatever was on your screen disappears. You are back at your Home screen, the one with all your app pictures on it.

If your iPad has no round button (newer models)

1

Look at the very bottom edge of the screen. You will see a thin, dark horizontal line.
2

Place your finger on that line and swipe upward, sliding from the bottom toward the middle.
3

You are back at your Home screen. Safe and sound.
Confidence check

You are doing wonderfully. The Home button is your best friend on this device. You cannot break anything by pressing it, so press it as often as you like.

Those scary pop-up messages

Let us talk about the situation that frightened Margaret. You are using your device, and suddenly a message fills your screen:

Example of a fake warning (this is NOT real)

WARNING! Your iPad has been infected with 3 viruses! Your personal data is at risk! Call Apple Support immediately at 1-888-555-0199. Do NOT close this window!

This message is fake, every single time. Real warnings from your device appear as small, calm notifications, never as flashing, full-screen alarms. Any message that tells you to call a phone number is a scam. Any message that says "Do NOT close this window" is trying to stop you from using your escape hatch. Close it.

The 3-second rule

  1. ✋Stop Take your fingers off the screen.
  2. 🫁Breathe One slow, deep breath.
  3. 🔍Verify Is this real, or is someone trying to scare me?
The move is

When a scary message appears, press the Home button (or swipe up from the bottom). The message is gone and your device is fine. Never call a number shown in a pop-up, and never enter a password to "fix" it. Real problems are never fixed by calling a number on your screen.
