🗂️ Digital life

Your Digital Life: Keeping It Safe and Organised

⏱ About 35 minutes, at your own pace

✅ You are in a safe place. Nothing on this page can harm your device.

How to take calm, organised control of your digital life: know what accounts you have, protect your photos, set up a trusted contact, and leave the memories that matter for the people you love.

💬
From your coach

Thinking about what happens to your accounts and photos after you are gone is heavy, and choosing to face it is an act of generosity toward the people you love. There is no deadline and no test here. Even one small step, like writing down where things are, is a gift your family will one day be grateful for.

Your digital footprint

Your digital footprint is simply everything about you that exists online. Your email account. Your social media profiles. Your online banking. The photos you have shared. The subscriptions you have signed up for.

Most people are surprised by how large their footprint is, and by how manageable it becomes once you look at it clearly.

Your digital inventory: start here

Take a few minutes to write down every online account you have. Include:

  • Email accounts (Gmail, Outlook, Rogers, and so on)
  • Social media (Facebook, Instagram, and others)
  • Online shopping (Amazon and similar stores)
  • Banking and financial accounts
  • Streaming services (Netflix and similar)
  • Any apps that required you to create an account
Keep the list somewhere safe

Keep this list somewhere safe: not on your phone, but perhaps in a notebook at home.

Confidence check

You do not need to do this all at once. One account at a time is perfectly fine.

Managing your accounts

Not every account you have ever created is worth keeping. Simplifying your digital life means deciding which accounts serve you, and safely closing the ones that do not.

Keep these accounts

  • Your primary email address: everything important is connected here
  • Online banking: essential and secure
  • Any account you use regularly (at least monthly)
  • Apple ID: needed for your iPhone and iPad

Consider closing these

  • Old email addresses you no longer check
  • Shopping accounts at stores you have not visited in years
  • Social media accounts you never use
  • Subscriptions you forgot you signed up for

To close an account: look for Settings or Account on the website, then look for Delete account or Close account. If you cannot find it, search online for "[service name] how to close account".

Confidence check

Is there one account you have been meaning to close? This is the week to do it.

Your digital photos: protecting your memories

Photos are often the most irreplaceable thing on a device. A backed-up photo can never truly be lost. An unbacked photo can disappear if your phone is lost, damaged, or stolen.

iCloud Photos: the simplest backup

iCloud Photos automatically backs up every photo you take to the cloud. When turned on, your photos are safe even if your phone is lost or broken.

Turning on iCloud Photos

1

Open Settings on your iPhone or iPad.
2

Tap [Your Name] at the top of the screen.
3

Tap iCloud, then tap Photos.
4

Toggle on iCloud Photos.
About iCloud storage

Free iCloud storage is 5GB, and it fills up quickly. Consider upgrading to iCloud+ (50GB for $1.29/month) if you take many photos.

Organising your photos into albums

1

Open Photos and tap Albums at the bottom.
2

Tap the + button at the top left.
3

Choose New Album and give it a name (for example, "Christmas 2025").
4

Select photos to add to the album.
Confidence check

Are your photos backed up right now? Check: Settings, then [Your Name], then iCloud, then Photos.

Passwords and trusted access

A practical question many seniors do not think about until it is urgent: if something happened to you, could someone you trust access your important accounts?

Your digital key list

Consider creating a physical, handwritten list of your most important accounts and how to access them. Store it in the same place as your will or other important documents, not on your phone. Include: your email address, your Apple ID (your iCloud login), your online banking login, and any other critical accounts.

Do not write out full passwords

Do NOT include your full passwords on a list that could be found by others. Instead, use a hint that only family would understand.

Apple Legacy Contact

Apple has a feature called Legacy Contact that lets you designate someone who can access your Apple account after you pass away. This person can download your photos, messages, and files.

Setting up an Apple Legacy Contact

1

Open Settings and tap [Your Name].
2

Tap Password & Security.
3

Tap Legacy Contact, then tap Add Legacy Contact and follow the steps.
Confidence check

Does at least one trusted person know how to access your most important accounts if they needed to?

Digital estate planning: the basics

This section is calm, practical, and empowering, not morbid. It is simply about making thoughtful decisions so your digital life is taken care of.

What happens to your accounts

  • Apple: your Legacy Contact can request access. Without one, accounts become inaccessible.
  • Facebook: family can request a "Memorialised" account (preserved as a tribute) or deletion.
  • Google/Gmail: Google has an Inactive Account Manager. You can decide what happens to your account after a period of inactivity.
  • Email: your family can request access from providers with a death certificate. It helps if they know which email provider you use.
Tell someone

The simplest digital estate plan is also the most effective: tell a trusted family member where your digital key list is kept, and what your Apple ID email address is. You do not need a lawyer for this. You just need one conversation.

Confidence check

Has someone in your family had this conversation with you? If not, consider starting it.

Cleaning up your digital life

Just like a tidy home feels better to live in, a tidy digital life is less stressful and more secure. Here are three simple housekeeping tasks.

Unsubscribing from email lists

If you get emails from stores, charities, or websites that you no longer want, scroll to the very bottom of any email and look for an Unsubscribe link. Tap it and follow the instructions. This is completely safe to do, and you will stop receiving emails from that sender.

Deleting old emails

Most people have hundreds or thousands of emails they no longer need. You can safely delete old emails; they are not important records unless they contain legal or financial information you need. In Gmail or Apple Mail, select multiple emails at once by checking the boxes next to them, then tap Delete.

Deleting apps you do not use

Apps you downloaded and never used are taking up space on your device. Press and hold any app icon until it wiggles, then tap the X or minus sign to delete it. If you accidentally delete an app you need, you can always re-download it from the App Store for free.

Confidence check

Can you identify 3 apps on your device right now that you have never used? Consider deleting them today.

Your digital legacy: leaving something behind

This is the warmest part of this lesson. Your digital life contains things that matter: photos, messages, maybe videos of your voice. These are gifts you can leave for family.

A photo album for your family

Consider creating a dedicated iCloud Shared Album called "Family Memories" and inviting your children or grandchildren. Add photos regularly. This becomes a living family archive that everyone can access.

A video message

One of the most meaningful things you can do with your phone is record a short video message to your family. Not for any urgent reason, but simply to say what you love about them, what you hope for them, and what you want them to remember. Open the Camera app. Switch to Video. Press the red button. Speak from the heart. Save it in your photo library and tell a trusted family member it is there.

A note to leave

The Notes app on your iPhone lets you write a note that can be shared with family. Some people write a brief note that tells family where important information is kept: where the will is, who to call, what their wishes are. Simple, practical, and incredibly kind.

Try it this week

Is there one thing, a photo, a message, a note, that you could create this week for someone you love? Start with that one thing.

Quick answers

What happens to my email account when I die?

Most email providers eventually delete inactive accounts after 12 to 24 months. Gmail allows you to set up an Inactive Account Manager to pass your data to a trusted contact. Apple's Legacy Contact feature lets you designate someone who can access your iCloud account after your death. It is worth setting this up now.

Do I need a lawyer to plan my digital estate?

No. The most important first step is simply writing down your accounts and having a conversation with a trusted family member. A lawyer can help include digital assets in a formal will, but the conversation and a written list are the most valuable actions and cost nothing.

What should I do with old photos on my phone?

The single most important action is to turn on iCloud Photos backup so all your photos are automatically saved to the cloud. You can then share your favourite albums with family using Shared Albums in the Photos app. Printing a small selection as a physical photo album is a wonderful gift that does not depend on technology.
