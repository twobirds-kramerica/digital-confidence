✉️ Email and messages

Tame Your Inbox

⏱ About 10 minutes, at your own pace

✅ You are in a safe place. Nothing on this page can harm your device.

A simple 5-minute daily routine that keeps the important emails visible and lets the spam flow by.

Robert's story

Robert, 81, has 14,327 unread emails. He has not deleted an email in four years because "what if I need it?" His inbox is so full that when his bank sent a genuine security alert, he did not see it for three days.

If this sounds familiar, you are not alone. Most people have thousands of unread emails, and no one has ever shown them a simple system for staying on top of it. This lesson will change that.

From your coach

Your inbox number does not measure anything about you. A big number just means messages have been arriving for years and no one showed you what to do with them. You do not need to catch up on old email. You only need a small daily habit, and this page will give you one.

Your inbox is a river, not a filing cabinet

Messages flow through your inbox. Some are important, like messages from your family, your bank, or your doctor. Most are not, like newsletters you signed up for years ago and automatic notifications. The goal is not to read every message. The goal is to find the important ones and let the rest flow by.

You do not need to read them all

If you have thousands of unread emails, do not try to go through them. That would take weeks and accomplish nothing. Focus only on emails from the last 7 days. Everything older is almost certainly no longer urgent, and you can always search for an old email if you need it later.

Archive versus delete

There are two ways to clear an email from your inbox, and understanding the difference changes how the whole thing feels.

Archive, the just-in-case button

When you archive an email, it leaves your inbox but is not deleted. It moves to a folder called "All Mail" or "Archive", where you can search for it any time. Use archive for receipts, confirmations, and messages you have already read. When in doubt, archive.

Delete, the gone-for-good button

When you delete an email, it goes to the Trash folder, waits about 30 days, and is then removed for good. Use delete for obvious spam, promotions you will never need, and anything from a sender you do not recognise.

How to unsubscribe safely

Robert was told that clicking "unsubscribe" is how scammers get you. That is partly true and partly a myth. The whole answer comes down to one question.

The simple rule

Did you ever give this company your email address? If yes, the unsubscribe link at the bottom of their email is safe and legal to use (Canadian law requires it to work). If no, do not click anything in the message.

Watch out

Do not click unsubscribe on emails you never signed up for, like lottery wins or package notices for things you never ordered. Clicking anything can confirm to the spammer that your address is active. Just delete these and mark them as spam.

Mark spam to train your filter

Every time you mark an email as spam, you teach your email app to recognise similar messages. The more you report, the smarter your filter gets. After about two weeks, most people see a large drop in the junk that reaches their inbox.

Report an email as spam

1

Open the spam email.
2

In Apple Mail, tap the flag or arrow icon, then Move to Junk.
3

In Gmail, tap the three dots in the top corner, then Report spam.
4

The message moves to your junk folder and your filter learns from it.
The two-week challenge

For the next two weeks, mark every spam email as junk rather than just deleting it. This is what trains the filter. After two weeks, your email will be noticeably better at catching spam before it ever reaches you.

Spam in texts and messaging apps

Unwanted messages now arrive through WhatsApp, Facebook Messenger, and regular texts too. Blocking and reporting them is quick once you know where to tap.

  • WhatsApp or Messenger: open the conversation, tap the person's name at the top, scroll down, and tap Block. They are not told, and they can no longer reach you.
  • Spam texts in Canada: forward the message to 7726 (it spells SPAM). This works with Bell, Rogers, Telus, and others. Your carrier will investigate and may block the sender.

Your 5-minute daily routine

The solution is not a big cleanup, it is a small daily habit. Five minutes each morning, with your coffee or tea, is all you need.

Every morning, four quick steps

1

Delete obvious spam (30 seconds). Do not open it, just delete or mark it as junk.
2

Read messages from family and your bank (2 minutes). Look for names you recognise, and reply to those.
3

Archive everything else (1 minute). It leaves your inbox but stays searchable if you ever need it.
4

Done. Do not try to read everything or reach an empty inbox. Keep the important messages visible and let the rest go.
The move is

Stop trying to catch up on old email. Start fresh tomorrow morning: delete the spam, read the important messages, archive the rest. Five minutes. That is your new system.
