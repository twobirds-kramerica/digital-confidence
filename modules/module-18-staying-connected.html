📞 Communication

Staying Connected When It Matters Most

⏱ About 25 minutes, at your own pace

✅ You are in a safe place. Nothing on this page can harm your device.

How to video call the people you love, share photos and memories, and keep your support network close, whatever the distance.

Meet Brenda

Brenda is 74 and lives in St. Thomas, Ontario. Her daughter moved to Vancouver three years ago. Her walking group stopped meeting during the pandemic and never quite restarted. She loves her home, her cat, and her garden, but she admits that some days go by without a real conversation.

Sound familiar? This lesson is for Brenda, and for everyone like her.

💬
From your coach

If distance from the people you love is the reason you are learning this technology, you are doing something brave: learning a new skill for the sake of a relationship. Fumbled calls and missed messages are part of everyone's learning curve. The people on the other end care that you showed up, not that you did it perfectly.

Why staying connected matters

Loneliness is not a character flaw. It is a health issue, as serious as smoking or high blood pressure. Research consistently shows that social isolation increases the risk of cognitive decline, depression, and physical illness in older adults.

But here is the encouraging truth: technology, used well, genuinely helps. Video calling, sharing photos, online groups, and even simple messaging can reduce isolation and bring real joy. This lesson will show you how.

Confidence check

Think of one person you have not spoken to in too long. By the end of this lesson, you will know exactly how to reach them.

Video calling your family

Video calling lets you see the face of someone you love, no matter how far away they are. Here are the three most common apps.

FaceTime (Apple devices only)

1

Find the green FaceTime app on your iPad or iPhone. If you and the person you are calling both have Apple devices, this is the easiest choice.
2

Tap the + button (top right corner).
3

Type the name of the person you want to call, or choose from your contacts.
4

Tap the video camera icon to start the call.
If the call will not connect

If the call does not connect within a few seconds, your Wi-Fi may be slow. Move closer to your router and try again.

Zoom (works on any device)

1

Download the Zoom app from the App Store. Zoom works on iPhones, iPads, Android phones, and computers, and is great for family group calls with 3 or more people.
2

When family sends you a Zoom link (it looks like zoom.us/j/12345...), tap it.
3

Tap Open in Zoom or Join Meeting.
4

Allow access to your camera and microphone when asked.

WhatsApp video (free, works everywhere)

1

Open WhatsApp and find the contact you want to call. WhatsApp is free and works between iPhones and Android phones, useful when family members have different devices.
2

Tap the video camera icon at the top right.
3

The call connects and you can see each other immediately.

Sharing photos and memories

One of the most meaningful things technology lets you do is share moments with people you love: a sunset from your backyard, your grandchild's drawing, a meal you are proud of.

Creating an iCloud Shared Album

1

Open the Photos app. A Shared Album is like a private photo album that you and your family can all add to.
2

Tap the Albums tab at the bottom.
3

Scroll down to Shared Albums and tap the + button to create a new one.
4

Give it a name (for example, "Family Photos 2026") and invite family by email or phone number.
5

Family members will get a notification and can add their own photos to the album too.

Sending a photo in a message

1

Open Messages and start a conversation.
2

Tap the photo icon (it looks like a mountain) to the left of the text box.
3

Choose a photo from your library and tap Send.

Messaging made easy

You do not have to type a full message every time. There are many ways to communicate that are quick, personal, and easy.

Voice messages: your new best friend. Instead of typing, you can hold a button and speak your message. The other person hears your voice, which is often much more meaningful than words on a screen. In iMessage: hold the microphone icon next to the text box, speak, release, and tap Send. In WhatsApp: hold the microphone icon on the right, speak, and release to send.

Reacting to messages. You do not always need to reply with words. In iMessage, press and hold on any message and choose a reaction (heart, thumbs up, thumbs down, haha, !!, ?). A quick heart reaction says "I saw this, I love you" without typing a single letter.

Confidence check

Have you tried sending a voice message? It takes less than 10 seconds and sounds much more personal than a text.

Online communities for seniors

There are online communities built specifically for people at your stage of life: people who share your interests, your experiences, and your questions about technology.

  • Facebook groups exist for almost every interest: gardening, knitting, local history, birdwatching, genealogy, your hometown, your faith community. These are private groups where you share with people who share your passions. To find one, tap the Groups icon in Facebook, then tap Discover and search for your interest.
  • Zoom meetups and classes. Many libraries, community centres, and seniors organisations now offer regular Zoom meetups: book clubs, exercise classes, language groups, current events discussions. These are free or very low cost. Check with your local library or seniors centre for current offerings.
Confidence check

Is there a hobby or interest you would enjoy sharing with others? Search for a Facebook group this week.

Keeping in touch across long distances

When family is far away, in another province, another country, or just a different time zone, staying connected takes a little more intention. Here are the habits that work.

Schedule your calls

The single most effective thing you can do is schedule a regular video call. Sunday afternoons. Wednesday mornings. Whatever works. Put it in your calendar and protect it like any other appointment. Even 15 minutes of video with a grandchild once a week creates a relationship that phone calls alone cannot.

Time zones: a quick cheat sheet. Vancouver (BC) is 3 hours behind Eastern time. The UK is 5 hours ahead (in summer). Australia is about 14 to 16 hours ahead. To check the current time anywhere, just ask Siri: "Hey Siri, what time is it in Vancouver right now?"

Confidence check

Is there one regular call you could schedule this week? Even 20 minutes?

When technology helps in difficult times

Grief and loss are part of a long life. When someone we love dies, or when friends and family move away, technology can be one way, not the only way, of staying connected to what matters.

  • Staying connected to family after loss. When families are scattered by grief, video calls become more important, not less. They let you grieve together even when you cannot be together physically. Sharing photos of a loved one in a shared album can also be a gentle way to celebrate a life together; everyone adds their favourite memory.
  • Online grief support groups. There are compassionate, moderated online communities for people navigating grief. The Canadian Mental Health Association and many hospice organisations offer free online support groups via Zoom. Your family doctor or local hospice can refer you to the right group.
A gentle note

This section is gentle for a reason. There is no action required here, just awareness that technology can be one more resource when you need it.

Your support network online

A support network is simply the list of people who are there for you: family, friends, neighbours, faith communities. Technology can help you keep that network strong and accessible.

Your trusted contact list. On your iPhone or iPad, keep a short list of trusted contacts easy to find: at least two family members, a close friend or neighbour, your family doctor's office, and your pharmacy. Add these to your Favourites in the Phone app: open Phone, tap Favourites, then tap + to add a contact.

Setting up emergency contacts on iPhone

1

Your iPhone has a Medical ID feature that lets first responders see your emergency contacts even if your phone is locked.
2

Open the Health app and tap your photo (top right).
3

Tap Medical ID, then Edit.
4

Add your Emergency Contacts and save.
Confidence check

Do the most important people in your life know how to reach you in an emergency? Does your phone have them saved as Favourites?

The move is

Pick the app your family already uses, schedule one regular call, and protect it like any other appointment. Connection is a habit, and habits are built one small call at a time.
