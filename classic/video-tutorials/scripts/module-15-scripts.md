### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: Types of Telehealth in Ontario**
[VOICEOVER]: "In Ontario, telehealth comes in several forms. Your own family doctor or specialist may offer video appointments through their clinic's patient portal — ask your doctor's office if they offer this. Ontario Telemedicine Network, or OTN, connects patients across Ontario with specialists virtually — your family doctor usually arranges referrals through OTN. On-demand services like Maple and Tia Health let you see a doctor within minutes, any time of day or night, for non-emergency issues. OHIP generally covers virtual visits with Ontario physicians — check with your provider."
[VISUAL CUE]: Ontario Telemedicine Network logo. Maple app icon. Your family doctor's clinic graphic.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Your doctor's portal · OTN specialist referrals · Maple for on-demand care · Most covered by OHIP

**SEGMENT 2: What You Need for a Video Appointment**
[VOICEOVER]: "For a video appointment, you need your iPad, a reliable Wi-Fi connection, the camera and microphone working, and a quiet, private space. Before your appointment: charge your iPad to at least 50 percent. Test your camera and microphone by opening the FaceTime app or the Camera app. Find a well-lit spot facing a window or lamp. Have a piece of paper and pen nearby to write down anything your doctor says — or open the Notes app on your iPad. If your appointment is through a patient portal, log in to the portal 5 to 10 minutes early to make sure everything is working."
[VISUAL CUE]: Pre-appointment checklist: charged iPad, Wi-Fi, camera test, quiet room, notes ready, log in early.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Pre-appointment: charged · Wi-Fi · camera test · quiet room · Notes app · log in early

**SEGMENT 3: Joining a Video Appointment**
[VOICEOVER]: "Your doctor's office will send you an appointment confirmation by email or through their patient portal app — often MyChart, OceanMD, or another regional system. The email or portal will have a link to join the video call, usually labelled 'Join Virtual Visit' or similar. Tap the link at your appointment time. The system may ask to access your camera and microphone — tap Allow for both. You'll usually enter a waiting room until the doctor is ready. On your iPad, the doctor's video will take up most of the screen and a small window will show your own camera view."
[VISUAL CUE]: Appointment confirmation email → Join Virtual Visit link tapped → permissions → waiting room → doctor appears.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Tap "Join Virtual Visit" in your confirmation email → Allow camera and microphone → wait in waiting room

**SEGMENT 4: Making the Most of a Virtual Appointment**
[VOICEOVER]: "A few things that help virtual appointments go well. Write down your main concerns before the appointment — it's easy to forget things when in the moment. If the doctor needs to see a physical symptom — a rash, a wound — hold your iPad close to the affected area and make sure the lighting is good. Speak clearly and slightly more slowly than usual — audio can occasionally lag slightly. At the end, repeat back any instructions or prescriptions to confirm you understood correctly, and ask whether follow-up is needed. Most provincial pharmacies can receive prescriptions electronically from telehealth visits."
[VISUAL CUE]: Notes with concerns written in advance. Holding iPad close to show skin concern. Repeating back prescription information.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Prepare: write concerns · Show symptoms clearly · Repeat back instructions · Electronic prescriptions available

**SEGMENT 5: On-Demand Telehealth — Maple and Tia Health**
[VOICEOVER]: "For times when your family doctor isn't available and you need medical advice quickly — a child's fever at 2am, a sudden rash, a question about a medication — on-demand telehealth services are remarkable. Maple offers access to a physician within minutes, 24 hours a day. Download the Maple app from the App Store, create an account, and start a visit. You'll answer a few questions and then connect with a doctor via video or text. Many issues can be assessed and a prescription sent to your pharmacy — all without leaving home. Some plans and employee benefits cover Maple; out-of-pocket a visit typically costs $49 to $79."
[VISUAL CUE]: Maple app → new visit started → doctor connected → prescription issued.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Maple: doctor in minutes, 24/7 · $49–$79 per visit · prescriptions sent electronically to your pharmacy

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Two telehealth frustrations. First: technical difficulties at appointment time. Always log in 5 to 10 minutes early and test your audio and video. If something isn't working, the patient portal usually has a technical support chat or phone number — use it before your appointment time, not during. Second: using telehealth for emergencies. Virtual appointments are not appropriate for chest pain, difficulty breathing, stroke symptoms, or any situation where you need immediate physical assessment. Call 911 for emergencies."
[VISUAL CUE]: Early login and tech test graphic. Emergency services — 911 — clearly displayed for urgent situations.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Technical issue? Log in early and test · Call 911 for emergencies — not telehealth

Four: Maple connects you with a doctor in minutes, around the clock."

## Video 2 of 3: Health Apps on Your iPad
**Target Duration:** 7 minutes
**Difficulty:** Beginner

### HOOK (First 15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Your iPad can be a remarkably capable health companion — tracking your medications so you never miss a dose, monitoring your heart rate, logging your blood pressure, and even integrating with your wearable device. Today we'll look at the apps that genuinely help, and how to use them effectively."

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "In this video, we'll cover the Health app that's already built into your iPad, apps for managing medications, blood pressure monitoring, and how to share health data with your doctor or family when you want to."
[VISUAL CUE]: Apple Health app icon. Medication reminder graphic. Blood pressure monitor device. Title card.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 15 · Video 2 of 3: Health Apps on Your iPad

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: The Apple Health App — Your Health Dashboard**
[VOICEOVER]: "The Health app comes pre-installed on every iPad and iPhone — it's the white icon with a red heart. It's a central dashboard that collects health data from your device and from compatible apps and devices. Tap 'Browse' at the bottom to see categories: Activity, Body Measurements, Heart, Medications, Mindfulness, Sleep, and Vitals. Many of these update automatically from an Apple Watch if you have one. Even without a wearable, you can manually add readings and set up a Medical ID — a screen that emergency responders can access without your passcode."
[VISUAL CUE]: Health app icon tapped. Browse tab shown with categories. Medical ID section highlighted.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Health app: white icon with red heart · Browse → Activity, Heart, Medications, Vitals

**SEGMENT 2: Setting Up Your Medical ID**
[VOICEOVER]: "Your Medical ID is one of the most valuable features of the Health app. It stores information that could be critical in an emergency — your blood type, allergies, medications, emergency contacts, and medical conditions. This screen can be accessed by emergency responders even when your iPad or iPhone is locked. To set it up, open the Health app, tap your profile photo in the top-right corner, then Medical ID, then Edit. Fill in what you're comfortable sharing. In an emergency, someone can access this by swiping to Emergency on your lock screen."
[VISUAL CUE]: Health app → profile icon → Medical ID → Edit → fields being filled: blood type, allergies, medications, emergency contact.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Medical ID: Health app → profile → Medical ID → Edit · Accessible from lock screen in emergencies

**SEGMENT 3: Medication Reminders**
[VOICEOVER]: "The Health app has a built-in Medications feature that lets you log all your prescriptions and set reminder notifications. Open Health, tap Browse, then Medications, then Add a Medication. Type the medication name — the app will suggest matching drugs — select the correct one. Choose your dosage and when you take it. Set a reminder time. You'll receive a notification at that time every day. When you take the medication, tap the notification to mark it as taken. The app tracks your history so you and your doctor can see your adherence over time."
[VISUAL CUE]: Health app → Browse → Medications → Add a Medication → drug name → dosage → reminder time set → notification shown.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Health → Medications → Add → set dose + reminder → tap notification to mark as taken

**SEGMENT 4: Blood Pressure Monitoring Apps**
[VOICEOVER]: "If your doctor has asked you to monitor your blood pressure at home, several apps make this easier. The HeartWatch and Blood Pressure Monitor - Smart BP apps allow you to log readings from a home blood pressure cuff and track trends over time with charts. Enter each reading after measuring. Some Bluetooth blood pressure cuffs — like those from Omron — sync automatically with an app on your iPad, eliminating manual entry. These apps can generate a report you can share with your doctor at your next appointment."
[VISUAL CUE]: Blood pressure app with reading entered. Trend chart shown over weeks. Omron Bluetooth cuff connecting to app.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Log BP readings in an app → trend charts → generate a report for your doctor

**SEGMENT 5: Sharing Health Data Safely**
[VOICEOVER]: "You can share your Health app data with family members or healthcare providers in two ways. First, through the Health app itself: tap your profile icon, then Health Sharing, to invite a trusted person to see selected data. They must have an Apple device. Second, many Ontario patient portals — MyChart, for example — allow you to connect your Apple Health app so your step count, heart rate, and other readings flow directly into your health record. Ask your healthcare provider whether their portal supports this integration."
[VISUAL CUE]: Health app → profile → Health Sharing → invite contact. MyChart app connecting to Health data.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Share health data: Health → profile → Health Sharing · Ask your doctor about MyChart integration

---

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Two health app cautions. First: using health apps as a replacement for medical advice rather than a supplement to it. Apps can identify trends worth discussing with a doctor, but a high blood pressure reading or an irregular heart notification is a prompt to call your doctor, not to self-diagnose or self-treat. Second: not keeping health app data private. Health data is among the most sensitive personal information you have. Only share Health app data with people or apps you fully trust. In Settings > Privacy > Health, you can see which apps have access to your health data and revoke access at any time."
[VISUAL CUE]: Health notification prompting a call to doctor — not self-treatment. Settings → Privacy → Health → app permissions list.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Health apps = trends to discuss with your doctor · Review Settings → Privacy → Health for app access

## Video 3 of 3: Managing Your Health Information Online
**Target Duration:** 7 minutes
**Difficulty:** Intermediate

### HOOK (First 15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Your health record used to live entirely in your doctor's filing cabinet. Today, much of it is accessible to you directly — lab results, immunisation records, referral status, upcoming appointments. Knowing how to access and use this information makes you a more informed and effective participant in your own healthcare."

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "In this final video of Module 15 — and of the Digital Confidence Centre series — we'll cover patient portals, accessing your health records online, and how to use your digital health tools to prepare for appointments and advocate for yourself effectively."
[VISUAL CUE]: MyChart app icon. Health record graphic. Title card.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 15 · Video 3 of 3: Managing Health Information Online

---

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: What Is a Patient Portal?**
[VOICEOVER]: "A patient portal is a secure website or app that gives you direct access to parts of your health record — lab results, prescription history, upcoming appointments, and in some cases, the ability to message your care team and request referrals or test results. In Ontario, several portal systems are in use: MyChart is used by many hospitals and clinics, Ocean by CognisantMD is used for pre-visit questionnaires, and some family doctors use Accuro or OSCAR with patient-facing components. Ask your family doctor's office which portal they use and request access."
[VISUAL CUE]: MyChart app shown. Patient portal login screen. Lab results accessible on screen.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Patient portal: secure access to your lab results, appointments, prescriptions, and messaging

**SEGMENT 2: Setting Up and Logging Into MyChart**
[VOICEOVER]: "MyChart is the most widely-used patient portal in Ontario hospitals, including many affiliated with Unity Health, University Health Network, and Sinai Health in Toronto, as well as many regional hospitals. Ask your hospital or specialist's office for a MyChart activation code — they'll either give you a printed code or send one by email. Go to mychart.ca or download the MyChart app. Enter your activation code, create a username and password, and your record is connected. Once set up, you can access it anytime from your iPad."
[VISUAL CUE]: MyChart activation code on paper. App download and activation setup flow. Lab results screen shown after login.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Ask your hospital for a MyChart activation code → mychart.ca → create account → access your records

**SEGMENT 3: Reading Your Lab Results**
[VOICEOVER]: "Once in your patient portal, lab results appear shortly after they're processed — sometimes before your doctor has had a chance to review and call you. Here's an important note: a flagged result — one marked 'High' or 'Low' or highlighted — is not necessarily cause for alarm. These flags indicate that a value is outside the standard reference range, which your doctor will interpret in the context of your full health picture. If you see a flagged result, contact your doctor's office to discuss it — don't try to diagnose it yourself based on internet searches."
[VISUAL CUE]: Lab results screen with a flagged 'High' value. Clear annotation: "Discuss with your doctor — do not self-diagnose."
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Flagged lab result? Call your doctor's office to discuss — context is everything

**SEGMENT 4: Accessing Your Immunisation and Health Records**
[VOICEOVER]: "Your Ontario immunisation records can be accessed through the Ontario Health app — the green shield icon. Download it from the App Store and log in with your provincial health credentials. You can view your vaccination history and download your COVID-19 vaccination record. For your full provincial health information, COVaxON and the Ontario COVID-19 Science Advisory Table provide public health data. For personal records, MyChart and your family doctor's portal are most relevant."
[VISUAL CUE]: Ontario Health app → immunisation records shown. Vaccination record downloaded as PDF.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Ontario Health app: view and download your immunisation records including COVID-19 vaccination

**SEGMENT 5: Preparing for Appointments Using Your Portal**
[VOICEOVER]: "Patient portals transform how you can prepare for appointments. Before a visit: review your recent lab results and write down any values you want to discuss. Check your medication list in the portal and make sure it's up to date — flag anything that's changed. If your portal has a pre-visit questionnaire, complete it in advance. After a visit: check your After Visit Summary if the portal provides one — this is a written record of what was discussed, your diagnoses, and any follow-up instructions. Having this in writing helps you remember and act on your doctor's recommendations."
[VISUAL CUE]: Lab results reviewed before appointment. Medication list in portal. After Visit Summary shown post-appointment.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Before appointment: review labs + medications · After: check After Visit Summary in portal

---

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "One final caution that applies to all health technology: your digital health tools are to support and enhance the relationship with your care team, not replace it. The most empowered patients are those who come to appointments informed and prepared — with questions written down, their medication list up to date, and an understanding of their own trends. This is not the same as making clinical decisions based on an app. Technology makes you a better participant in your care — your healthcare team remains the expert."
[VISUAL CUE]: Informed patient at doctor's appointment — notes prepared, questions ready. A warm, collaborative healthcare scene.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Digital health tools make you a better-informed participant — your care team remains the expert
