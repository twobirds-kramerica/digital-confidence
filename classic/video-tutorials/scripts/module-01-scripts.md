### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: What Is an iPad?**
[VOICEOVER]: "An iPad is a tablet computer made by Apple — the same company that makes iPhones. Think of it like a very flat, very lightweight computer that you hold in your hands. You use your finger to tap things on the screen, just like touching a button. There's no mouse, no keyboard you have to plug in, and nothing to trip over. It's designed to be simple and portable."
[VISUAL CUE]: Side-by-side comparison — a traditional desktop computer vs. an iPad on a table. Simple animation shows a finger tapping the screen.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Your iPad: A touchscreen computer you hold in your hands

**SEGMENT 2: Getting to Know the Outside**
[VOICEOVER]: "Let me show you the buttons on the outside of your iPad. On the top edge, there's the power button — we sometimes call it the Sleep/Wake button. When your iPad is off, pressing this button once will wake it up. On the right side, you'll find the volume buttons — two of them, stacked on top of each other. Press the top one to make sound louder, and the bottom one to make it quieter. On some iPads, there's also a small round button on the front, at the bottom — that's the Home button, and it's one of the most important things we'll talk about today."
[VISUAL CUE]: Close-up of an iPad showing each button highlighted in turn with a yellow circle as it is mentioned.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Power button (top) · Volume buttons (right side) · Home button (front, bottom)

**SEGMENT 3: Turning Your iPad On**
[VOICEOVER]: "To turn your iPad on, press and hold the power button on the top edge for about two seconds. You'll see the Apple logo — a silver apple — appear in the middle of the screen. That means it's starting up. Give it a moment. If your screen just lights up without the Apple logo, your iPad was already on but in sleep mode. That's perfectly normal — it saves battery by going to sleep when you're not using it."
[VISUAL CUE]: Screen recording of an iPad being powered on — power button held, Apple logo appearing, lock screen loading.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Hold the top button for 2 seconds to turn on

**SEGMENT 4: The Lock Screen and Unlocking Your iPad**
[VOICEOVER]: "When your iPad wakes up, the first thing you'll see is the lock screen. This is like the front door of your iPad — it keeps your information private. To get inside, you'll either press the Home button and enter your passcode — that's a 4 or 6 digit number you set up — or you might be able to use your fingerprint or face to unlock it. We'll cover those options in a later module. For now, try pressing the Home button once, and if it asks for a number, type in your passcode."
[VISUAL CUE]: Lock screen shown with an arrow pointing to the Home button. Then a passcode entry screen appears with the keypad highlighted.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Press Home button → Enter your passcode to unlock

**SEGMENT 5: The Home Screen**
[VOICEOVER]: "Once you're past the lock screen, you'll land on the Home screen. This is the main menu of your iPad — it's where all your apps live. Apps are the little pictures you tap to do things, like check email, look something up online, or take a photo. We'll explore apps in detail in future modules. For now, just know that this is your home base — and no matter where you go on your iPad, you can always press the Home button to come back here."
[VISUAL CUE]: Home screen shown with a gentle zoom. Icons highlighted briefly. Home button pressed and screen returns to Home after navigating away.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: The Home Screen: your starting point for everything

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Here are three things that trip up almost everyone in the beginning — and none of them are anything to worry about.

First: pressing too hard on the screen. Your iPad's touchscreen is very sensitive — it only needs a gentle, brief tap. You don't need to press firmly the way you might push a physical button. If your taps aren't registering, try a slightly firmer tap with the pad of your fingertip.

Second: accidentally waking up the wrong thing. When you first pick up your iPad, you might see unexpected things pop up — that's often just your hand brushing the screen. Pressing the Home button always brings you back to a safe, familiar place.

Third: worrying that you've broken something. You can't break your iPad by tapping the wrong thing. Everything can be undone or closed. The worst thing that can usually happen is that you end up somewhere unfamiliar — and the Home button will always bring you home."
[VISUAL CUE]: Simple animated illustrations for each mistake — a gentle tap vs. a hard press, a confused-looking person, and a reassuring green checkmark.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: The Home button always brings you back to a safe place

You've made a great start today."

## Video 2 of 3: Your Escape Hatch — Mastering the Home Button
**Target Duration:** 6 minutes
**Difficulty:** Beginner

### HOOK (First 15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Have you ever tapped something on your iPad and then had no idea how to get back to where you started? That sinking feeling of being lost on your device — that's exactly what we're solving today. And the solution fits under your thumb."

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Welcome back to the Digital Confidence Centre. Today we're going to master what I call the Escape Hatch — the Home button on your iPad. This is the single most important thing to know about your device. Once you truly understand how it works, you will never feel stuck on your iPad again. Not once."
[VISUAL CUE]: iPad Home button highlighted with a golden glow.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 1 · Video 2 of 3: Your Escape Hatch

---

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: What the Home Button Does**
[VOICEOVER]: "The Home button is the round button at the bottom of your iPad screen. On newer iPad models without a physical button, you swipe up from the very bottom of the screen to do the same thing. The Home button has one core job: it brings you home. No matter where you are on your iPad — no matter what app you've opened, no matter how confused you feel — pressing the Home button once will take you straight back to your Home screen. It's your reset button. Your panic button. Your escape hatch."
[VISUAL CUE]: Demo of being deep inside an app (e.g., a complex settings menu), pressing Home button, and immediately landing on the Home screen. Big, clear, slow.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Lost on your iPad? Press the Home button. You're home.

**SEGMENT 2: Pressing Once vs. Twice vs. Holding**
[VOICEOVER]: "The Home button does different things depending on how you press it. One press: takes you to the Home screen. Two quick presses: shows you all the apps that are currently open in the background — this is called the App Switcher. You can swipe them away to close them, but most of the time you don't need to worry about this. Press and hold for two seconds: activates Siri, Apple's voice assistant. If Siri pops up unexpectedly, don't panic — just press the Home button once to dismiss it."
[VISUAL CUE]: Three demonstrations in sequence — single press, double press (App Switcher shown), held press (Siri). Each clearly labelled on screen.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: 1 press = Home Screen · 2 presses = App Switcher · Hold = Siri

**SEGMENT 3: Newer iPads — The Swipe Gesture**
[VOICEOVER]: "If your iPad doesn't have a round button on the front, you have a newer model that uses a swipe gesture instead. To go home on these iPads, place your finger at the very bottom edge of the screen and slowly swipe up toward the centre. The screen will shrink and then jump to the Home screen. It takes a bit of practice, but after a few tries it becomes second nature. The same rule applies: whenever you're lost, swipe up from the bottom."
[VISUAL CUE]: Close-up of a hand performing the swipe-up gesture from the bottom of an iPad screen without a Home button. Slow-motion replay.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: No Home button? Swipe up slowly from the bottom of the screen

**SEGMENT 4: Practice — Getting Intentionally Lost and Found**
[VOICEOVER]: "Here's a great exercise to build your confidence. Open any app — it doesn't matter which one. Now press the Home button. You're back. Open the app again, navigate somewhere inside it. Press Home. You're back. Do this a few times. What you're doing is training your muscle memory — and more importantly, you're proving to yourself that no matter where you end up on your iPad, you can always find your way home. There is no trap you can get stuck in. The Home button is always there."
[VISUAL CUE]: Viewer-perspective recording of opening Settings, going several menus deep, pressing Home, returning, repeating. Upbeat, calm pace.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Practice: get lost on purpose — then find your way home

**SEGMENT 5: The Side Button on Face ID iPads**
[VOICEOVER]: "On newer iPads, the button on the top edge — we've been calling it the power button — is also called the Side button. On these models, pressing and holding the Side button and the volume-down button together will call emergency services — so be careful not to press those together by accident. If you do trigger an Emergency SOS screen, just press Cancel. No harm done."
[VISUAL CUE]: Demonstration of accidental Emergency SOS trigger and how to cancel it. Calm, matter-of-fact tone.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Accidentally pressed Emergency SOS? Just tap Cancel — you're safe.

---

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Two things to watch for. First: pressing the Home button in the middle of an action, like typing an email. The Home button will interrupt what you're doing and take you away from it — the email might not be saved yet. This is an easy fix: get into the habit of saving or sending before pressing Home. Second: confusing going home with closing an app. When you press Home, apps usually stay running in the background — you haven't deleted them or broken anything. Think of it like stepping away from your TV — the show is still on, you just walked into another room."
[VISUAL CUE]: Email being abandoned mid-type by pressing Home. Then visual of the app still appearing in the App Switcher — showing nothing was lost.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Home doesn't close apps — it just takes you to the main menu

---

### RECAP (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Three things to remember from today. One: the Home button is your escape hatch — one press and you're always back at the Home screen. Two: on newer iPads without a button, swipe up from the bottom of the screen. Three: you can never get permanently stuck. Practice getting lost and finding your way back — it truly builds confidence."
[VISUAL CUE]: Three-point list on screen.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: 1. Home button = always safe · 2. New iPads: swipe up · 3. You can never get stuck

## Video 3 of 3: When Things Go Wrong — Restarting and Recovering
**Target Duration:** 7 minutes
**Difficulty:** Beginner

### HOOK (First 15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Your iPad has frozen. The screen won't respond. You've tried tapping and nothing happens. Before you worry — this is completely normal, and it's very easy to fix. In the next few minutes, I'll show you exactly what to do."

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Welcome back. Today we're covering what to do when your iPad isn't behaving — whether it's frozen, running slowly, or an app has crashed. Every device has moments like this, and knowing how to handle them calmly is a genuine superpower. By the end of this video, you'll have a clear checklist for troubleshooting any problem on your iPad."
[VISUAL CUE]: iPad showing a frozen screen, then being successfully restarted and returning to normal.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 1 · Video 3 of 3: When Things Go Wrong

---

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: The First Thing to Try — Close and Reopen the App**
[VOICEOVER]: "If one specific app is frozen or acting strangely, the first thing to try is simply closing it and reopening it. Double-press the Home button — or swipe up and hold on newer iPads — to see the App Switcher. Find the problem app, then swipe it upward off the top of the screen to close it. Then press Home and tap the app icon to reopen it. This fixes the problem nine times out of ten."
[VISUAL CUE]: Step-by-step screen recording: App Switcher opened, app card swiped away, Home screen, app reopened.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Frozen app? Close it in the App Switcher, then reopen it

**SEGMENT 2: When the Whole iPad Seems Frozen — Soft Reset**
[VOICEOVER]: "If the whole iPad has frozen and even the Home button isn't responding, it's time for what's called a soft reset — which just means restarting it, the same way you'd restart a computer. This does not delete anything. All your photos, contacts, apps — everything stays exactly as it was. To restart, press and hold the power button until a red slider appears that says 'Slide to power off.' Drag that slider across, wait about 30 seconds, then press the power button again to turn back on."
[VISUAL CUE]: Power button held until power-off slider appears. Slider being dragged. Screen going black. Apple logo reappearing.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Restart your iPad: Hold power button → Slide to turn off → Wait → Turn back on

**SEGMENT 3: Force Restart — For Serious Freezes**
[VOICEOVER]: "Very occasionally, even the power slider won't appear because the iPad is completely unresponsive. This is called a hard freeze. Don't panic. There's a move called a Force Restart. On most iPads with a Home button: press and hold both the Home button and the power button at the same time, for about 10 seconds, until you see the Apple logo appear. On newer iPads without a Home button: quickly press and release the volume-up button, then quickly press and release the volume-down button, then press and hold the top button until the Apple logo appears. The iPad will restart itself. Again — no data is lost."
[VISUAL CUE]: Two sets of instructions shown side by side — Home button iPad and newer iPad — with button locations clearly labelled.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Force restart: Hold Home + Power for 10 seconds (older models)

**SEGMENT 4: Battery — What Low Battery Looks Like**
[VOICEOVER]: "Sometimes what looks like a frozen iPad is actually just a dead battery. If your iPad screen is completely black and won't respond to the power button at all, try plugging it in to charge for at least 5 minutes, then try the power button again. When the battery is critically low, the iPad needs a few minutes of charge before it will even show the startup screen. You might see a picture of a battery with a red or empty bar — that's normal, it just means it's charging."
[VISUAL CUE]: iPad connected to charger, low battery screen appearing, then Apple logo appearing a few minutes later.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Black screen and won't start? Plug it in for 5 minutes first

**SEGMENT 5: When to Ask for Help**
[VOICEOVER]: "Most problems on an iPad are solved by the steps we've just covered. But occasionally, something needs professional attention. It's time to call Apple Support or visit an Apple Store if: your iPad keeps freezing every day even after restarting; the screen is cracked or the touchscreen stops responding to your touches; your battery drains to zero within an hour of charging; or you see an error message you don't recognise and can't get past. Apple Support Canada's number is 1-800-MY-APPLE, and they are genuinely helpful and patient. You can also visit the Digital Confidence Centre website for local support options in Ontario."
[VISUAL CUE]: List of "when to get help" scenarios on screen, then Apple Support number displayed clearly.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Apple Support Canada: 1-800-MY-APPLE | Apple Store Genius Bar

---

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Here are two mistakes people make when their iPad acts up. First: repeatedly tapping a frozen screen. If the screen isn't responding, more tapping usually won't help — and can make things worse if the device is trying to process something. Give it 30 seconds of patience first. If nothing happens, then restart. Second: assuming the iPad is broken and giving up. A frozen app or slow response is not a sign that something is seriously wrong. It's as normal as a car engine that needs a moment to warm up on a cold morning. The steps in this video will solve almost everything."
[VISUAL CUE]: Someone frantically tapping a frozen screen — then a calmer approach with patience, restart, resolution.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Frozen screen? Wait 30 seconds first, then restart — don't panic

Three: a completely unresponsive iPad — force restart by holding Home and power together. And four: a black screen that won't start — plug it in and charge for at least five minutes. You've got this."
