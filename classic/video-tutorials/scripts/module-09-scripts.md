## Video 1 of 3: What Is AI and How Does It Work?

**Difficulty:** Beginner

[VOICEOVER]: "Welcome to Module 9 of the Digital Confidence Centre. Artificial intelligence — AI for short — is one of the most significant technologies of our time. ### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: AI in Plain Language**
[VOICEOVER]: "Artificial intelligence is software that can learn from information and do tasks that previously required a human — like recognising a face, understanding speech, translating a language, or answering a question. Think of it like a very well-read assistant who has read millions of books and websites and can now summarise them, answer questions about them, or create new things inspired by them. It doesn't think or feel the way humans do — but it can process and use information in ways that are incredibly useful."
[VISUAL CUE]: Simple graphic: books and articles going into a system → useful outputs coming out. Friendly, non-threatening visual.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: AI = software that learns from vast amounts of information and uses it to help you

**SEGMENT 2: AI Is Already on Your iPad**
[VOICEOVER]: "Here's something that may surprise you: AI is already running quietly in the background of your iPad doing useful things. The autocorrect that fixes your spelling as you type — that's AI. The face recognition in the Photos app that groups photos of the same person together — AI. The keyboard predictions that suggest the next word when you're typing — AI. Siri answering your voice questions — AI. You've been using artificial intelligence for years without knowing it."
[VISUAL CUE]: Four examples shown in sequence: autocorrect in action, Photos face grouping, keyboard word predictions, Siri answering a question. Each labelled "AI at work."
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Autocorrect · Face recognition in Photos · Word prediction · Siri — all AI

**SEGMENT 3: How AI Language Models Work**
[VOICEOVER]: "The newest type of AI — the kind behind tools like ChatGPT and Apple Intelligence — is called a large language model. Think of it as having read an almost inconceivably large amount of text: websites, books, news articles, scientific papers. It learned the patterns of language and information so well that it can now generate new text, answer questions, write drafts, and have conversations. It doesn't 'know' things the way a person knows them — it predicts what a helpful response would look like, based on everything it's been trained on."
[VISUAL CUE]: Simple animation of text going into a model → model learning patterns → generating a helpful response.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: AI language model: learned from vast text → predicts helpful responses

**SEGMENT 4: What AI Is Good At**
[VOICEOVER]: "AI tools are genuinely helpful for several types of tasks. Explaining things in plain language — 'Explain this medical term my doctor used.' Writing first drafts — 'Help me write a letter to my landlord about a repair.' Summarising — 'What are the main points of this news article?' Brainstorming — 'What would be a good gift for someone who loves gardening?' Translation — 'Translate this French text for me.' These are tasks where having a knowledgeable, patient, and always-available assistant is genuinely valuable."
[VISUAL CUE]: Five use cases shown as simple icons: question mark (explain), letter (write draft), list (summarise), lightbulb (brainstorm), globe (translate).
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: AI helps with: explaining · writing drafts · summarising · brainstorming · translating

**SEGMENT 5: What AI Gets Wrong — and Why That Matters**
[VOICEOVER]: "This is equally important: AI is not perfect and can be confidently wrong. It can produce information that sounds completely authoritative but is factually incorrect — this is sometimes called 'hallucinating.' It can reflect biases from the texts it was trained on. And it genuinely does not know things that happened very recently. For medical advice, legal decisions, financial choices, and anything with serious consequences — use AI as a starting point for a conversation with a qualified professional, not as the final answer."
[VISUAL CUE]: AI response shown with a warning: "Always verify important information with a qualified professional." Doctor, lawyer, financial advisor icons.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: AI can be confidently wrong — always verify medical, legal, and financial advice

---

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Two things to watch for. First: treating AI as completely reliable for facts. Always verify anything important through a second source — a trusted website, a professional, or a phone call to the relevant organisation. Second: sharing personal information with AI tools. Most AI chatbots store conversations to improve their systems. Avoid entering your full name, address, Social Insurance Number, banking details, or medical history into public AI tools. Treat them like a library reference desk — useful for general information, but not the place for your personal data."
[VISUAL CUE]: AI chat interface with a red 'stop' overlay on personal information being typed. "General questions = OK, personal data = No."
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Never share personal, financial, or medical information with AI chatbots

Two: your iPad already uses AI — autocorrect, face recognition, word prediction, Siri. Three: AI is great for explaining, drafting, and summarising. Four: verify anything important — AI can be confidently wrong."
[VISUAL CUE]: Four-point recap list.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: AI already on your iPad · Great for: explaining, drafting, summarising · Can be wrong — always verify

## Video 3 of 3: Using AI Safely and Thinking Critically
**Target Duration:** 7 minutes
**Difficulty:** Intermediate

### HOOK (First 15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "AI is a powerful tool — and like any powerful tool, the more you understand its limitations, the more safely and effectively you can use it. Today we're covering the important skill of AI literacy: knowing when to trust what AI tells you, and when to double-check."

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "In this final video of Module 9, we'll cover how to use AI tools like ChatGPT and Siri thoughtfully — how to spot when an AI might be wrong, how to identify AI-generated misinformation online, and how to protect your privacy when using AI services."
[VISUAL CUE]: Scale icon — balancing "useful AI" on one side and "critical thinking" on the other. Title card.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 9 · Video 3 of 3: Using AI Safely and Critically

---

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: Using ChatGPT on Your iPad**
[VOICEOVER]: "ChatGPT is an AI assistant made by a company called OpenAI. It's available as a free app on the App Store. Unlike Siri, ChatGPT specialises in conversation and text tasks — writing, explaining, translating, and brainstorming. To use it: download the ChatGPT app, create a free account, and type your question. Some examples: 'Explain what a living will is in plain language.' 'Help me write a thank-you note to my doctor.' 'What questions should I ask my pharmacist about this medication?' These are all things ChatGPT handles very well."
[VISUAL CUE]: App Store → ChatGPT downloaded → chat interface shown → question typed → helpful answer shown.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: ChatGPT: great for explaining, writing, and brainstorming in plain language

**SEGMENT 2: Verifying AI Answers — The Two-Source Rule**
[VOICEOVER]: "For any AI answer that matters — anything related to your health, legal situation, finances, or safety — use what I call the Two-Source Rule. Before acting on something AI told you, find at least one other reliable source that confirms it. Good reliable sources include: the Government of Canada website (canada.ca), your provincial health authority, the Mayo Clinic or Health Canada for health questions, and the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada for financial questions. If AI and one of these sources agree, you can feel confident."
[VISUAL CUE]: AI answer shown. Two-Source Rule graphic: AI → check canada.ca → agree? = confident. Disagree? = ask a professional.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Two-Source Rule: AI answer + one reliable source that confirms it = more trustworthy

**SEGMENT 3: Recognising AI-Generated Misinformation**
[VOICEOVER]: "AI can be used to create convincing but false news articles, fake videos of real people, and realistic-sounding scam messages. A few warning signs to watch for. AI-written text often sounds very formal and smooth — unusually polished without any of the small imperfections of natural human writing. AI-generated images sometimes have strange details on close inspection — unusual hands, blurry text within the image, lighting that doesn't quite match. Reverse image search — available through Google — lets you find out where an image actually came from."
[VISUAL CUE]: AI text sample shown with 'too polished' annotation. AI-generated image with subtle hand anomaly highlighted. Google reverse image search demonstrated.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: AI text: unusually smooth · AI images: check hands and text · Use reverse image search

**SEGMENT 4: Privacy When Using AI Tools**
[VOICEOVER]: "When you use AI tools like ChatGPT, your conversations may be stored and used to improve the system. Here's a practical privacy guide. Safe to ask: general questions, writing help, explanations of concepts. Not safe to enter: your full name and address, your Social Insurance Number, health details, financial account information, passwords, or information about other people without their consent. Treat an AI chatbot like a well-staffed public library — incredibly useful for general knowledge, but not the place for personal records."
[VISUAL CUE]: Two-column table: "Safe to ask" with green checkmarks vs. "Don't enter" with red X marks and examples.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Safe: general questions · Not safe: personal info, SIN, banking, health details

**SEGMENT 5: Turning Off AI Learning in ChatGPT**
[VOICEOVER]: "If you'd prefer that your conversations with ChatGPT not be used to improve their AI, you can turn this off. In the ChatGPT app, tap your profile icon, go to Settings, then Data Controls, and turn off 'Improve the model for everyone.' With this off, your conversations are not used for training. You can also delete your conversation history at any time from the same settings menu. Taking these steps doesn't reduce the quality of responses you receive — it just keeps your conversations more private."
[VISUAL CUE]: ChatGPT app → profile → Settings → Data Controls → toggle off.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: ChatGPT → profile → Settings → Data Controls → turn off "Improve the model"

---

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Two AI literacy mistakes. First: assuming that because an AI answer sounds confident and complete, it must be correct. AI systems do not experience uncertainty the same way humans do — they will deliver wrong answers in the same calm, assured tone as correct ones. Second: dismissing AI entirely because of its flaws. Despite its limitations, AI is genuinely useful for the right tasks. The skill is knowing when to use it and when to verify — not avoiding it completely."
[VISUAL CUE]: AI confidently stating a wrong fact. Balance scale — AI usefulness on one side, critical thinking on the other.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: AI sounds confident even when wrong — verify anything that matters

Four: never share personal information with AI tools."
[VISUAL CUE]: Four-point recap list.
