## Video 1 of 3: Filling In Online Forms
**Target Duration:** 7 minutes
**Difficulty:** Beginner

### HOOK (First 15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Whether it's booking a doctor's appointment, renewing a library card, or ordering something online, almost every website asks you to fill in a form at some point. Today we're going to make forms feel completely manageable — because once you know the pattern, they're all pretty much the same."

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Welcome to Module 2.5 of the Digital Confidence Centre — Everyday Digital Tasks. This module is all about the practical things you do online regularly: filling in forms, shopping safely, and using email. In this first video, we'll walk through how online forms work and how to fill them in accurately and confidently."
[VISUAL CUE]: A simple web form on an iPad screen. Cursor moving to the first field.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 2.5 · Video 1 of 3: Filling In Online Forms

---

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: What Is a Web Form and Why Does It Exist?**
[VOICEOVER]: "An online form is just a digital version of the paper forms you've filled in your whole life — at the doctor's office, at the bank, at Service Ontario. Instead of writing with a pen, you tap a field on the screen and type using the on-screen keyboard. Forms collect information — your name, address, what you're ordering — so the other side knows what to do for you."
[VISUAL CUE]: Side-by-side comparison: a paper form and a digital form side by side. Same fields, different format.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Online forms work just like paper forms — tap a field, then type

**SEGMENT 2: The Keyboard — Appearing and Disappearing**
[VOICEOVER]: "When you tap on a text field in a form, the keyboard will automatically pop up from the bottom of the screen. Type your information, then tap the next field. The keyboard will stay visible until you tap somewhere outside the form fields, or until you tap Done at the top-right of the keyboard. If the keyboard is covering the form and you can't see a field, scroll up slightly with your finger — the page will move but the keyboard will stay. This is one of those things that feels awkward at first and then becomes automatic."
[VISUAL CUE]: iPad screen showing a form — keyboard appearing when a field is tapped, scrolling to reveal hidden fields, Done button tapped.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Tap a field → keyboard appears · Tap Done when finished typing

**SEGMENT 3: Required vs Optional Fields**
[VOICEOVER]: "Most forms mark some fields as required — meaning you have to fill them in before you can submit. These are usually marked with a little red asterisk, which looks like a star or a snowflake. Optional fields don't have this symbol, and you can skip them if you want. If you try to submit a form and it highlights a field in red, that's the form telling you that a required field is missing or has an error — it hasn't been submitted yet and nothing has gone wrong."
[VISUAL CUE]: A form with asterisks highlighted and labelled. A submit error shown — field highlighted in red — with explanation of what it means.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Red asterisk (*) = required field · Red highlight on submit = missing info

**SEGMENT 4: Dropdowns, Checkboxes, and Radio Buttons**
[VOICEOVER]: "Not all form fields are text boxes. Dropdown menus — they look like a box with a small arrow on the right — let you pick from a list of options. Just tap the box and a list of choices will appear. Checkboxes are small squares you tap to put a checkmark inside. Radio buttons are small circles where you can only pick one option at a time. These look different from text fields but they all work the same way: tap to select, tap again to deselect."
[VISUAL CUE]: Each type demonstrated in sequence — dropdown opening with list, checkbox ticked, radio button selected.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Dropdown = tap to see choices · Checkbox = tick to select · Radio = one choice only

**SEGMENT 5: Submitting the Form and What Happens Next**
[VOICEOVER]: "Once you've filled in all the required fields, look for the Submit or Send or Continue button — it's usually at the bottom of the form. Tap it once. Wait a moment. You should see a confirmation message — something like 'Thank you, your form has been submitted' or 'Your appointment has been booked.' That's it — you're done. If the page just refreshes and the form is still there with some fields highlighted in red, that means something needs to be corrected before it will submit."
[VISUAL CUE]: Completed form, Submit button tapped, confirmation screen appearing.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Wait for the confirmation message — that means it worked

---

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Three things that cause confusion with forms. First: tapping Submit more than once because nothing seemed to happen. This can submit the form twice — just tap once and be patient for up to 10 seconds. Second: closing the page after filling in the form but before hitting Submit. All your work is lost and you'd have to start again. Third: entering a phone number or postal code with spaces or dashes when the form doesn't want them. If a field says 'phone number' and turns red after you type, try removing dashes and spaces and just entering the digits."
[VISUAL CUE]: Each mistake illustrated — double-tap on Submit, closed browser, phone number format issue.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Tap Submit once, then wait — and always submit before closing the page

---

### RECAP (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Today's key points. One: tap a field to bring up the keyboard — tap Done when you're finished typing. Two: red asterisks mark required fields. Three: dropdowns, checkboxes, and radio buttons all just need a tap to select. Four: submit once and wait for the confirmation message. You've now got forms sorted."
[VISUAL CUE]: Four-point recap list.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Tap · Type · Submit once · Wait for confirmation

## Video 2 of 3: Shopping Online Safely
**Target Duration:** 8 minutes
**Difficulty:** Beginner

### HOOK (First 15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Did you know that nearly 80 percent of Canadians now shop online at least occasionally? The convenience is genuinely wonderful — especially for those times when getting to a store isn't easy. Today we'll make sure you can shop online with full confidence that your information is safe."

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "In this video, we'll cover the complete process of buying something online — from finding a trustworthy website, adding something to your cart, paying safely, and tracking your order. We'll also cover the signs that a website is legitimate and the red flags that suggest you should shop elsewhere. By the end, you'll have everything you need to shop online confidently."
[VISUAL CUE]: iPad showing a familiar, trustworthy shopping website homepage.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 2.5 · Video 2 of 3: Shopping Online Safely

---

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: Choosing a Trustworthy Website**
[VOICEOVER]: "The first step in safe online shopping is making sure you're on a legitimate website. The most reliable approach is to stick to stores you already know in real life — Shoppers Drug Mart, Canadian Tire, Chapters Indigo, Amazon Canada. For these, type the web address directly into your browser rather than searching — this avoids accidentally landing on a fake lookalike site. Whatever website you're on, check the address bar at the very top of the screen. It should start with 'https://' — the 's' stands for secure. You'll also see a small padlock icon to the left of the address. If the padlock is missing or shows a warning, do not enter your payment information."
[VISUAL CUE]: Browser address bar highlighted showing "https://" and padlock icon. Then a site without https shown with a warning label.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Look for https:// and the padlock before entering any payment details

**SEGMENT 2: Searching for a Product and Adding to Cart**
[VOICEOVER]: "Once you're on a trusted shopping site, look for a search bar — usually near the top of the page — and type what you're looking for. The results will show you products with photos, names, and prices. Tap on one to see more details. When you find what you want, look for a button that says 'Add to Cart' or 'Add to Bag' — tap it once. You'll usually see a small notification that your item has been added. To see your cart and move toward paying, look for a shopping cart icon near the top-right of the screen."
[VISUAL CUE]: Screen recording of searching for a product, viewing the product page, adding to cart, and cart icon updating with a count badge.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Search → Tap product → Add to Cart → Tap cart icon to pay

**SEGMENT 3: Creating an Account vs. Guest Checkout**
[VOICEOVER]: "Most shopping sites will offer you the option to either create an account or check out as a guest. Checking out as a guest means you don't need to create a username and password — you just enter your details for this one purchase. This is perfectly fine and actually simpler for occasional shopping. Creating an account makes sense only if you plan to shop at that store regularly and want to save your address for next time. Either way, you'll need to enter your name, delivery address, and payment details."
[VISUAL CUE]: Checkout screen showing two options — "Create Account" and "Guest Checkout" — with Guest Checkout highlighted as the simpler option.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Guest checkout is safe and simpler for occasional shopping

**SEGMENT 4: Paying — What Information to Enter**
[VOICEOVER]: "The payment page will ask for your credit or debit card information. This includes: the long card number (usually 16 digits on the front of the card), the expiry date (month and year, on the front), and the CVV or security code — that's the 3-digit number on the back of the card, in the signature strip. Enter these carefully and double-check before tapping Submit or Place Order. Important: never shop on a public Wi-Fi network — like at Tim Hortons or the library — unless you're only browsing, not paying. Save your payments for when you're on your home internet."
[VISUAL CUE]: Credit card front showing card number and expiry, then back showing CVV location. Each highlighted in turn.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Card front: number + expiry · Card back: 3-digit security code

**SEGMENT 5: Order Confirmation and Tracking**
[VOICEOVER]: "After placing your order, you should receive a confirmation email within a few minutes. This email will have your order number and an estimated delivery date. Most stores will also send a second email when your order ships, with a tracking number. You can click the tracking number in that email to follow your package. Keep your order confirmation email until your order arrives in case there is ever a question about it."
[VISUAL CUE]: Order confirmation email shown on iPad. Tracking number highlighted. Link clicked to show Canada Post tracking page.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Save your order confirmation email until your package arrives

---

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Three common mistakes. First: tapping 'Place Order' without checking the cart total one last time. Prices can change after you've been browsing for a while. Always review the order summary page before paying. Second: using a debit card instead of a credit card. Credit cards offer much stronger fraud protection — if something goes wrong, your bank can reverse the charge. Debit card payments are harder to recover. Third: forgetting to check the return policy. A trustworthy site will have a clear, readable return policy. If you can't find it, shop elsewhere."
[VISUAL CUE]: Order review page shown before payment — total highlighted. Credit vs debit card comparison graphic.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Use a credit card for online purchases — stronger fraud protection

---

### RECAP (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Today's key points for safe online shopping. One: use websites you know and trust, and check for https and the padlock. Two: guest checkout is fine for occasional purchases. Three: pay with a credit card and never on public Wi-Fi. Four: save your order confirmation email until the package arrives."
[VISUAL CUE]: Four-point recap list.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: https + padlock · Guest checkout · Credit card · Save the confirmation email

## Video 3 of 3: Sending and Receiving Email
**Target Duration:** 7 minutes
**Difficulty:** Beginner

### HOOK (First 15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Email is one of the most useful things you can do on your iPad — staying in touch with family, receiving receipts, communicating with your doctor's office. Once you get comfortable with it, it opens up a whole new level of connection. Let's walk through it step by step."

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "In this final video of Module 2.5, we're covering email on your iPad — how to read messages that arrive, how to write and send a reply, and how to compose a brand new message to someone. We'll also look at a few common email frustrations and how to solve them."
[VISUAL CUE]: iPad showing the Mail app inbox. A few messages visible.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 2.5 · Video 3 of 3: Sending and Receiving Email

---

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: Opening the Mail App and Your Inbox**
[VOICEOVER]: "The Mail app on your iPad is the blue square with a white envelope icon. Tap it to open. When it opens, you'll see your inbox — a list of messages you've received. Unread messages have a blue dot on the left side. Tap any message to open and read it. The most recent messages are at the top. If you have more messages than fit on the screen, scroll down to see older ones."
[VISUAL CUE]: Mail app icon tapped. Inbox shown with unread blue dots highlighted. Message tapped to open.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Blue dot = unread message · Tap to open and read

**SEGMENT 2: Replying to an Email**
[VOICEOVER]: "To reply to an email, open the message first. Then look for the Reply button — it's an arrow curving to the left, usually at the bottom or top of the message. Tap it, and a new message will open with the recipient's address already filled in and the original message quoted below. Type your reply at the very top, above the quoted text. When you're ready to send, tap the blue Send button — it often looks like a paper airplane in the top-right corner of the message."
[VISUAL CUE]: Open email, reply button highlighted and tapped, compose window appears with address pre-filled, message typed, Send button tapped.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Reply arrow → type your message at the top → tap the blue Send button

**SEGMENT 3: Writing a New Email From Scratch**
[VOICEOVER]: "To start a brand new email to someone, look for a compose button — on the iPad Mail app, it's usually a pencil-and-paper icon in the top-right corner of the inbox. Tap it. A new blank message will open. Tap the 'To:' line and type the email address of the person you're writing to. Tap 'Subject:' and type a brief description of what the email is about — this helps the recipient understand what it is before they open it. Then tap in the large blank space below and type your message. When you're done, tap Send."
[VISUAL CUE]: Inbox → compose icon tapped → new blank email → To field filled → Subject filled → body typed → Send tapped.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Compose icon → To: address → Subject: brief description → your message → Send

**SEGMENT 4: Attachments — Sending a Photo**
[VOICEOVER]: "Many emails include attachments — files or photos added to the message. To add a photo to an email you're writing, look for a small camera or paperclip icon in the toolbar above the keyboard. Tap it, choose Photo Library, and select the photo you want to send. It will appear in the body of the email. Keep in mind that some photos are very large files — if you're sending more than two or three, the email might be slow to send or might not arrive for the recipient. For sharing lots of photos, we cover better options in Module 7."
[VISUAL CUE]: Compose window with attachment icon tapped. Photo picker shown. Selected photo appearing in email body.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Camera/paperclip icon → Photo Library → choose photo to attach

**SEGMENT 5: Organising Your Inbox**
[VOICEOVER]: "Over time, your inbox can fill up with messages you've already dealt with. To delete an email you don't need anymore, swipe it to the left in the inbox list and tap the red Trash button. You can also archive messages — this moves them out of your inbox without deleting them permanently, so you can find them later if needed. Tap and hold a message to see more options. Most people find it easiest to delete things they've dealt with and don't need, and to keep only current, relevant messages in their inbox."
[VISUAL CUE]: Inbox message being swiped left to reveal Trash button. Message deleted. Clean inbox shown.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Swipe left on a message → Trash to delete it

---

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Two email mistakes to avoid. First: accidentally tapping Reply All when you meant to just Reply. Reply All sends your response to everyone who received the original message, not just the person who sent it. Always check whether you're replying to one person or everyone — look for a Reply or a Reply All button. When in doubt, use Reply. Second: confusing Sent and Drafts. Drafts are emails you've started but not sent yet — they are not sent until you press Send. If you're not sure whether something went through, check the Sent folder."
[VISUAL CUE]: Reply vs Reply All buttons highlighted. Draft folder and Sent folder shown in sidebar.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Reply = one person · Reply All = everyone · Drafts = not yet sent

---

### RECAP (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Today's key points. One: blue dot means unread — tap to open and read. Two: use the Reply arrow to respond and the compose icon to start a new message. Three: the paperclip or camera icon adds a photo or file. Four: swipe left to delete messages you don't need. You're now well set up with email on your iPad."
[VISUAL CUE]: Four-point recap list.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Read · Reply · Compose · Attach · Delete — email made simple
