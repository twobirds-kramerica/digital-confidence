## Video 1 of 3: Online Banking Basics
**Target Duration:** 8 minutes
**Difficulty:** Beginner

### HOOK (First 15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Check your balance, pay a bill, transfer money between your accounts — all from the comfort of your kitchen table, in your pyjamas if you like. Online banking is one of the most genuinely useful things you can do on your iPad, and today we're going to make sure you can do it safely and confidently."

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Welcome to Module 6 of the Digital Confidence Centre. This module covers banking and financial transactions on your iPad. In this first video, we'll look at how online banking works, how to download and set up your bank's app, and how to check your balance and recent transactions. We'll use examples from some of Canada's major banks, but the steps are similar for every institution."
[VISUAL CUE]: Bank app icons — TD, RBC, Scotiabank, BMO shown briefly. Title card.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 6 · Video 1 of 3: Online Banking Basics

---

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: Is Online Banking Safe?**
[VOICEOVER]: "This is the question most people ask first — and it deserves a direct answer. Yes, online banking through your bank's official app is very safe. Banks use the same level of encryption that governments use to protect classified communications. Every transaction is monitored for unusual patterns. And if someone does fraudulently access your account, Canadian banks are required to reimburse you — as long as you haven't shared your password and you report it promptly. The app is safer than calling your bank on the phone, where someone could potentially overhear you."
[VISUAL CUE]: Shield and padlock graphic. Text: "Bank-grade encryption." Brief CDIC (Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation) reference.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Bank apps use the same encryption as government security systems

**SEGMENT 2: Downloading Your Bank's Official App**
[VOICEOVER]: "Open the App Store and search for your bank's name. TD customers search for 'TD Canada Trust.' RBC customers search for 'Royal Bank of Canada' or 'RBC Mobile.' BMO, Scotiabank, CIBC — each has its own app. Look for the app with your bank's real logo, published by your bank's own name as the developer. Check that it has a very large number of reviews — tens of thousands. Download it using your Apple ID, and do not pay for it — every major Canadian bank app is free."
[VISUAL CUE]: App Store search for 'TD Canada Trust.' Official app shown with developer name and review count highlighted.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Search your bank name in the App Store · Official app = bank's own developer name

**SEGMENT 3: Logging In for the First Time**
[VOICEOVER]: "Open your bank's app after downloading it. You'll need your online banking username and password — these are the same ones you use on your bank's website if you already bank online, or you can create them through the app. Tap 'New User' or 'Enrol' if it's your first time. You'll need to verify your identity using your card number, account number, or by calling your bank. Once enrolled, the app will offer to set up Face ID or Touch ID for future logins — say yes to this. It's more secure than typing a password."
[VISUAL CUE]: Bank app first launch → login screen → Enrol button → identity verification step → Face ID setup offer.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: First time? Tap Enrol → verify identity → set up Face ID for future logins

**SEGMENT 4: Checking Your Balance and Transactions**
[VOICEOVER]: "Once logged in, your account balances usually appear on the home dashboard right away. Tap any account — chequing, savings — to see your recent transactions. They're listed with the most recent at the top, showing the date, description, and amount. Green numbers usually mean money going in — a deposit or transfer. Red or negative numbers mean money going out — a purchase or withdrawal. Look through your transactions regularly — at least weekly. This helps you catch any purchases you don't recognise early."
[VISUAL CUE]: Bank app dashboard → account balance shown → account tapped → transaction list with deposits in green and withdrawals labelled.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Green = money in · Red/negative = money out · Check weekly for anything unfamiliar

**SEGMENT 5: Paying a Bill Through the App**
[VOICEOVER]: "Most bank apps let you pay bills directly — hydro, phone, internet, credit card — without writing a cheque. Look for 'Pay Bills' or 'Payments' in the app's navigation. You'll add a payee — this is just the company you want to pay. Search for their name (for example, 'Hydro One' or 'Bell Canada') and enter your account number with that company. Save the payee. The next time you want to pay that bill, just select it, enter the amount, and confirm. The payment typically processes overnight or within two business days."
[VISUAL CUE]: Bank app → Pay Bills → Add Payee → Hydro One searched and selected → account number entered → payee saved → payment made.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Pay Bills → Add Payee → search company name → add your account number → done

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Two important cautions. First: logging in to your bank on a public Wi-Fi network, like at a coffee shop. Wait until you're home on your private Wi-Fi or use your phone's mobile data instead. Public Wi-Fi can be intercepted. Second: entering your banking password on a website — as opposed to the app. If you ever receive an email with a link that takes you to a page asking for your online banking password, close it immediately. Access your banking only through the official app or by typing your bank's web address directly."
[VISUAL CUE]: Public Wi-Fi warning graphic (Tim Hortons Wi-Fi → bank app = red X). Direct app access = green checkmark.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Bank on home Wi-Fi or mobile data only — never on public Wi-Fi

---

Three: check your transactions weekly. Four: pay bills directly through the app under Pay Bills. Next video: Interac e-Transfer."
[VISUAL CUE]: Four-point recap list.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Official app · Face ID · Check weekly · Pay bills in-app

## Video 2 of 3: Safe Interac e-Transfers
**Target Duration:** 8 minutes
**Difficulty:** Intermediate

### HOOK (First 15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Interac e-Transfer is how millions of Canadians send and receive money digitally — splitting a restaurant bill, paying a contractor, sending birthday money to a grandchild across the country. It's fast, it's free at most banks, and when done correctly, it's completely safe. Let's walk through it."

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "In this video, we'll cover how to send an Interac e-Transfer from your bank's app, how to receive and deposit one, and — very importantly — how to set up Autodeposit so you never have to answer a security question to receive money. This last part alone is worth watching the whole video for."
[VISUAL CUE]: Interac logo (generic digital transfer graphic). Title card.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 6 · Video 2 of 3: Safe Interac e-Transfers

---

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: What Is an Interac e-Transfer?**
[VOICEOVER]: "Interac e-Transfer is a service that lets you send money directly from your bank account to someone else's bank account, using only their email address or mobile phone number. You don't need their account number. The money moves through the Interac network, which is the same network behind every debit card transaction in Canada. It's available at virtually every Canadian bank and credit union. Most banks offer a limited number of free transfers per month."
[VISUAL CUE]: Graphic showing money moving from one bank account to another via email address, with Interac network in the middle.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Interac e-Transfer: send money using just an email or phone number

**SEGMENT 2: How to Send an e-Transfer**
[VOICEOVER]: "Open your bank app and log in. Look for 'Transfers' or 'Send Money' in the navigation. Select 'Interac e-Transfer.' Choose the account you want to send from, then select or add a contact — you'll need their email address or phone number. Enter the amount. Create a security question and answer that only the recipient will know — for example, 'What city did we meet in?' with the answer 'London' — and communicate the answer to them separately, not in the same message as the transfer. Review everything carefully, then confirm. The recipient will receive an email or text notification."
[VISUAL CUE]: Bank app → Transfers → Interac e-Transfer → Send → contact selected → amount entered → security question created → review screen → confirm.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Send: account → contact → amount → security question → confirm

**SEGMENT 3: How to Receive and Deposit an e-Transfer**
[VOICEOVER]: "When someone sends you a transfer, you'll receive an email or text notification. Open the email and tap the link inside. This will take you to a web page asking which bank you use — select yours and log in. You'll be asked the security question — type the answer carefully. Then choose which account to deposit into and confirm. The money arrives within a few minutes. Keep in mind: if you haven't set up Autodeposit, you must go through this process for every transfer you receive."
[VISUAL CUE]: E-Transfer notification email → tap link → Interac page → bank selected → login → security question → deposit account selected → confirmed.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Receive notification → tap link → answer security question → choose account → deposit

**SEGMENT 4: Setting Up Autodeposit — Strongly Recommended**
[VOICEOVER]: "Autodeposit is the best thing you can do for receiving e-Transfers. Once it's set up, any Interac e-Transfer sent to your email address is automatically deposited into your designated account without you needing to do anything at all — no link to click, no security question to answer. This is more convenient and actually more secure, because there's no security question step that a scammer could potentially intercept. To set it up, go to your bank app, find Interac e-Transfer settings, and look for Autodeposit. Register your email address. Done."
[VISUAL CUE]: Bank app → Interac settings → Autodeposit → email registered → confirmation. Then: transfer received → automatically deposited → notification only.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Set up Autodeposit once — money deposits automatically, no action needed

**SEGMENT 5: e-Transfer Scams — What to Watch For**
[VOICEOVER]: "e-Transfer scams do exist, so here's what to watch for. The 'accidental transfer' scam: someone sends you money and then claims it was a mistake, asking you to send it back. They use a fraudulent payment so the original never actually clears — and you've sent them real money. Never send money back to someone you don't know. Second: fake e-Transfer notification emails with links to phishing sites. Always check the sender's email address — legitimate Interac notifications come from interac.ca domain addresses, not variations like interac-notifications.net or similar."
[VISUAL CUE]: Fake e-transfer email shown with suspicious sender domain highlighted. Warning text overlay.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Check: real Interac emails come from @interac.ca — not look-alike domains

---

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Two e-Transfer mistakes to avoid. First: using an obvious security question whose answer the recipient doesn't know privately — like 'What's your last name?' or 'What city do you live in?' if those are things many people know. Pick a question whose answer only the two of you would know. Second: sending a transfer to the wrong email address. Once sent, an e-Transfer can only be cancelled if the recipient hasn't yet deposited it. If you realise you've sent to the wrong address, go to your bank app immediately, find the transfer in your activity, and cancel it before the other person deposits."
[VISUAL CUE]: Security question shown with a strong answer vs a weak answer. Cancel transfer option shown in bank app.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Sent to the wrong address? Cancel it immediately in your bank app before deposit

---

### RECAP (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Today's key points. One: Interac e-Transfer moves money using email or phone number — it's Canada's standard. Two: create a security question only the recipient can answer. Three: set up Autodeposit in your bank app settings — it's the safest and most convenient option. Four: real Interac emails come from @interac.ca."
[VISUAL CUE]: Four-point recap list.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Security question only recipient knows · Set up Autodeposit · @interac.ca emails only

## Video 3 of 3: Reading Your Bank Statement Online
**Target Duration:** 7 minutes
**Difficulty:** Beginner

### HOOK (First 15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Understanding your bank statement — whether it's the paper version you've received for years or the digital one in your app — is one of the most empowering financial habits you can have. Today we'll decode what every section means and show you how to access statements directly from your iPad."

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "In this final video for Module 6, we'll walk through how to access your online bank statement, how to read each section, and how to set up paperless statements if you'd like to reduce mail. We'll also cover what to do if you notice a transaction that doesn't look right."
[VISUAL CUE]: Bank app statement screen. Title card.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 6 · Video 3 of 3: Reading Your Statements Online

---

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: Where to Find Your Statements**
[VOICEOVER]: "Log in to your bank app. Look for a section called 'Statements,' 'Documents,' or 'e-Statements' — it's usually in the account settings or in the menu for each individual account. Tap your chequing or savings account, then look for a statements link. You'll see a list of statements organised by month and year. Tap any month to view that statement. Most bank apps show the last 12 to 24 months of statements. Statements can also be downloaded as PDF files if you need to keep a copy."
[VISUAL CUE]: Bank app → account selected → Statements option → monthly list → statement opened.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Bank app → account → Statements → select a month → view or download

**SEGMENT 2: Understanding the Statement Header**
[VOICEOVER]: "The top of your statement — the header — contains your account information. This includes your name and address, your account number (usually partially hidden for security — you might see just the last 4 digits), the statement period showing the start and end dates, and your opening and closing balances. The closing balance is your account balance at the end of the statement period. If there's a difference between opening and closing that surprises you, the transactions section will explain it."
[VISUAL CUE]: Statement header section highlighted. Each element labelled: name, account number (partially masked), statement period, opening balance, closing balance.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Header: account info · opening balance (start) · closing balance (end of period)

**SEGMENT 3: Reading the Transaction List**
[VOICEOVER]: "Below the header is the transaction list — every deposit and withdrawal during the period, in date order. Each line shows the date, a description of the transaction, and the amount. Deposits — money coming in — are shown in the 'Credit' or 'Deposits' column. Withdrawals — money going out — are in the 'Debit' or 'Withdrawals' column. Some banks just show positive or negative amounts in one column. The 'Balance' column on the far right shows your running total after each transaction."
[VISUAL CUE]: Transaction list annotated: date column, description, credit/debit amounts, running balance on right.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Credit = money in · Debit = money out · Balance = running total after each transaction

**SEGMENT 4: Identifying a Transaction You Don't Recognise**
[VOICEOVER]: "If you see a transaction you don't recognise, don't panic — there are a few common explanations. Many purchases show the parent company name, not the store. A Tim Hortons purchase might appear as 'Restaurant Brands International.' A Shoppers Drug Mart transaction might show 'Loblaw Companies.' If you still can't identify it after thinking about it, tap the transaction in the app — some banks give you more details. If it still doesn't make sense, call your bank's fraud line immediately. It's always better to ask."
[VISUAL CUE]: Transaction showing "Restaurant Brands International" with a note: "This is Tim Hortons." Fraud line contact graphic.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Don't recognise a charge? Tap it for details — then call fraud if still unclear

**SEGMENT 5: Going Paperless — Digital Statements**
[VOICEOVER]: "If you'd like to reduce your paper mail, most banks let you switch to electronic statements — these appear in the same Statements section of the app instead of arriving by post. To switch, look for 'Paperless Statements' or 'e-Statements' in your account settings, and toggle it on. You'll typically receive an email notification when a new statement is ready. If you ever need a paper copy — for taxes or a loan application — you can download and print the PDF version from the app at any time."
[VISUAL CUE]: Account settings → Paperless Statements toggle → confirmation. Then PDF download demonstrated.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Switch to e-Statements to reduce mail — download PDF any time you need a paper copy

---

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Two statement-reading mistakes. First: only looking at the closing balance rather than reviewing transactions. Your balance could be correct overall even if there's a fraudulent charge that was balanced out by a deposit. Review the actual transactions regularly, not just the total. Second: filing or discarding statements without checking them first. Even with automatic alerts for suspicious activity, it's a habit worth keeping — 10 minutes once a month, reviewing last month's transactions."
[VISUAL CUE]: Balance-only glance vs full transaction review. Monthly calendar reminder graphic.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Don't just check your balance — review individual transactions monthly

---

### RECAP (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Today's key points. One: find statements in your bank app under your account or the main menu. Two: credits are money in, debits are money out, the Balance column shows your running total. Three: unrecognised transactions — tap for details, call fraud if unclear. Four: switch to e-Statements to get notifications when new statements are ready."
[VISUAL CUE]: Four-point recap list.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Statements in-app · Credit in / Debit out · Tap unrecognised transactions · Go paperless
