## Video 1 of 3: How the App Store Works
**Target Duration:** 6 minutes
**Difficulty:** Beginner

### HOOK (First 15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Your iPad comes with a few built-in apps — but the real magic happens when you add more. The App Store has over two million apps — from games to health tools to grocery delivery — and every one of them has been reviewed by Apple before you can download it. Today we'll show you how to navigate it safely and confidently."

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Welcome to Module 4 of the Digital Confidence Centre. This module is about the App Store — Apple's marketplace where you find and install apps on your iPad. We'll cover what the App Store is, how Apple keeps it safe, and how to navigate the Store to find exactly what you're looking for."
[VISUAL CUE]: App Store blue icon. Title card.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 4 · Video 1 of 3: How the App Store Works

---

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: What Is the App Store?**
[VOICEOVER]: "The App Store is the only place you should install apps on your iPad. Think of it like a shopping mall where every store has been vetted and approved before they're allowed inside. Apple reviews every app before it appears in the Store — checking for malicious code, privacy violations, and misleading behaviour. This doesn't mean every app is perfect, but it means the worst actors are filtered out before they can reach you."
[VISUAL CUE]: App Store icon. Simple graphic showing Apple's review gate before apps reach users.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: App Store = Apple-reviewed apps only — your first layer of protection

**SEGMENT 2: Finding the App Store and Navigating It**
[VOICEOVER]: "The App Store app is the blue icon with a white letter 'A' made of three lines. Tap it to open. At the bottom of the screen, you'll see five tabs: Today, Games, Apps, Arcade, and Search. The Search tab at the far right is where you'll spend most of your time — type the name of an app or a description of what you need. The Today tab shows featured apps chosen by Apple's editors. Apps shows category browsing."
[VISUAL CUE]: App Store opened. Bottom tabs highlighted in sequence — Today, Games, Apps, Arcade, Search.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: App Store bottom tabs: Today · Games · Apps · Arcade · Search

**SEGMENT 3: Understanding App Listings**
[VOICEOVER]: "When you find an app in the search results, tap it to see its full listing. Here's what each part means. The star rating shows you what other users thought — 4 stars and above is generally a good sign. The number of ratings matters too — an app with 4.8 stars from 8 people tells you much less than one with 4.6 stars from 50,000 people. Scroll down to read the description and look at the screenshots. Check 'App Privacy' at the bottom of the listing — this tells you exactly what data the app collects."
[VISUAL CUE]: App Store listing shown with each element annotated: star rating, number of ratings, screenshots, App Privacy section.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Check: stars + number of reviews + App Privacy section

**SEGMENT 4: Free vs Paid Apps and In-App Purchases**
[VOICEOVER]: "Some apps are free to download, some cost a few dollars, and some are free with optional paid features inside — these are called in-app purchases. An app that says 'Free' might still try to sell you things once you've opened it. Look for 'In-App Purchases' or 'Subscriptions' text under the Get or price button. This is not necessarily bad — but it's good to know before you download. We'll cover how to control in-app purchases in Video 3 so you can never be accidentally charged."
[VISUAL CUE]: App listing showing "Get" for free, "$4.99" for paid, and "In-App Purchases" label.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: "In-App Purchases" label means you may be offered paid features inside the app

**SEGMENT 5: Your Apple ID and the App Store**
[VOICEOVER]: "To download any app — free or paid — you need an Apple ID. This is the account you use for everything Apple. When you tap Get or the price button, your iPad will ask you to confirm with Face ID, Touch ID, or your Apple ID password. This confirmation step is a security check — and it means that nobody can download or purchase apps on your iPad without your fingerprint, face, or password."
[VISUAL CUE]: Get button tapped → Face ID confirmation → Downloading animation.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Apple ID confirmation keeps others from downloading apps without permission

---

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Two things to be cautious about. First: apps that ask for far more permissions than they need. A flashlight app should not need access to your contacts. A recipe app should not need your location. When an app first opens and asks for permissions, read the request carefully and tap 'Don't Allow' if it doesn't make sense. Second: downloading apps from websites instead of the App Store. If you ever see a message online saying 'Download this app here' with a link — do not tap it. Always go to the App Store directly to find the app."
[VISUAL CUE]: Permission request popup — "Allow access to Contacts" from a flashlight app — with "Don't Allow" highlighted.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Only download apps from the official App Store — never from a link in a website

---

### RECAP (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Three key points. One: the App Store is your only safe source for iPad apps — Apple reviews everything before it appears there. Two: check star ratings and the App Privacy section before downloading. Three: your Apple ID confirmation prevents unauthorised purchases. Next video: how to search for and install specific apps."
[VISUAL CUE]: Three-point recap list.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: App Store only · Check ratings + privacy · Apple ID confirmation = your protection

## Video 2 of 3: Finding and Installing Useful Apps
**Target Duration:** 7 minutes
**Difficulty:** Beginner

### HOOK (First 15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Once you know how to find good apps, your iPad becomes dramatically more useful. Today we're going to walk through searching for specific apps, checking that they're the right ones, and installing them step by step — including a few apps that are especially useful for everyday life in Ontario."

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "In this video, we'll do a complete walkthrough of finding, checking, and installing an app — from the search box to the Home screen. We'll use some real examples from apps many Canadians use every day. By the end of this video, you'll be able to find any app with confidence."
[VISUAL CUE]: App Store search bar highlighted. Title card.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 4 · Video 2 of 3: Finding and Installing Useful Apps

---

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: Searching for an App by Name**
[VOICEOVER]: "Tap the Search tab at the bottom of the App Store. The search bar at the top becomes active — tap it and type the name of the app you're looking for. Let's try searching for 'Canada Post.' The results will show a list of apps. The official Canada Post app will usually be at the very top, with a recognisable logo. Look for the app from the official developer — in this case, 'Canada Post' — listed under the app name. Tap the app to see its full listing."
[VISUAL CUE]: Search tab tapped, 'Canada Post' typed, results shown, official app at top with developer name visible.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Search the app name → look for the official developer name below the title

**SEGMENT 2: Verifying the Right App**
[VOICEOVER]: "Before you install, spend 30 seconds checking the app listing. Look at the developer name — it should match the company you expect. For a banking app, it should say the bank's own name as the developer. Check the number of reviews — a well-known app like TD Bank or Shoppers Drug Mart should have tens of thousands of reviews, not just a few dozen. Read the first couple of user reviews. Look at the screenshots to make sure it looks like the real app."
[VISUAL CUE]: App listing with developer name annotated, review count highlighted, screenshots section shown.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Developer name + thousands of reviews = likely the real app

**SEGMENT 3: Installing a Free App**
[VOICEOVER]: "To install a free app, tap the Get button. Your iPad will ask you to confirm with Face ID, Touch ID, or your password. Confirm, and the app will start downloading — you'll see a progress circle on the icon on your Home screen. When it finishes, the icon will appear and you can tap it to open. Most apps take just a few seconds to download over Wi-Fi."
[VISUAL CUE]: Get button tapped → Face ID confirmation → downloading circle on Home screen icon → icon appears → app opens.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Get → confirm with Face ID or fingerprint → wait for the progress circle → done

**SEGMENT 4: Ontario-Relevant Apps Worth Downloading**
[VOICEOVER]: "Here are a few apps that many Ontarians find genuinely useful. The Ontario Health app lets you view your OHIP card, access your COVID-19 vaccination record, and connect with health services. The Canada Post app lets you track packages and manage your mail. The Tim Hortons app — useful for ordering ahead and collecting rewards. Service Ontario online lets you renew your health card and driver's licence. The MyChart app connects to many Ontario hospital systems for lab results and appointment booking. These are all free, well-reviewed, and from official developers."
[VISUAL CUE]: Each app icon shown as it's named, with star rating and developer name briefly visible.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Ontario Health · Canada Post · Tim Hortons · Service Ontario · MyChart — all free

**SEGMENT 5: Organising New Apps on Your Home Screen**
[VOICEOVER]: "After you install an app, it appears at the end of your Home screen. To move it somewhere easier to find, press and hold the app icon for about two seconds until all the icons start to wiggle. Now you can drag the app to a different spot. Drag it on top of another app to create a folder. Tap Done in the top-right corner when you're finished. This lets you keep related apps together — for example, putting all your health apps in one folder."
[VISUAL CUE]: App icon held, wiggling, dragged to new position. Then dragged onto another app to create a folder.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Hold app → wiggle → drag to move or group into folders

---

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Two things that cause confusion during installs. First: accidentally installing a lookalike app that has a similar name to the one you want but is from a different developer. Always check the developer name and review count. Second: thinking a downloaded app has disappeared. New apps go to the last page of your Home screen — or to the App Library, which is the screen to the right of your last Home screen page. Swipe all the way right past your Home screens to find the App Library, which shows every installed app in alphabetical order."
[VISUAL CUE]: App Library screen shown — accessed by swiping right past all Home screens. Alphabetical list visible.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Can't find your new app? Swipe right to the App Library — it's alphabetical

Four: find installed apps on your last Home screen page or in the App Library. Five: hold app icons to move them."
[VISUAL CUE]: Five-point recap list.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Search · Verify developer · Get · Find in App Library · Organise with hold-drag

## Video 3 of 3: Managing and Removing Apps
**Target Duration:** 6 minutes
**Difficulty:** Beginner

### HOOK (First 15 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Downloading an app is just the beginning. Keeping them updated, knowing which ones to remove, and making sure you're never accidentally charged — those are the skills that keep your iPad running well and your account safe. Let's cover all three today."

### INTRODUCTION (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "In this final video for Module 4, we'll cover three things: how to update your apps so they have the latest security fixes, how to delete apps you no longer use, and how to set up controls around purchases so you can never be surprised by an unexpected charge."
[VISUAL CUE]: Title card. Settings and App Store icons shown.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Module 4 · Video 3 of 3: Managing and Removing Apps

---

### MAIN CONTENT

**SEGMENT 1: Why Updating Apps Matters**
[VOICEOVER]: "App updates are not just about new features — they often contain important security fixes. An outdated app can have known vulnerabilities that bad actors know how to exploit. Updating takes about 30 seconds per app over a good Wi-Fi connection. The simplest approach is to let your iPad update apps automatically in the background. Go to Settings, scroll down to App Store, and make sure 'App Updates' is turned on under Automatic Downloads."
[VISUAL CUE]: Settings → App Store → Automatic Downloads → App Updates toggle shown turned on.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Settings → App Store → turn on App Updates (automatic)

**SEGMENT 2: Manually Updating an App**
[VOICEOVER]: "If you want to update apps yourself, open the App Store and tap your profile picture or initial in the top-right corner. Scroll down to see a list of apps with available updates. Tap Update next to any app to update it individually, or tap Update All to update everything at once. After updating, the app will restart with its new version."
[VISUAL CUE]: App Store → profile icon tapped → Updates list shown → Update All button.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: App Store → profile icon → scroll down → Update All

**SEGMENT 3: Deleting Apps You Don't Use**
[VOICEOVER]: "Unused apps take up storage space and can occasionally run in the background using battery life. To delete an app, press and hold its icon on the Home screen for about two seconds until the icons wiggle and an X or minus icon appears. Tap the minus or X, then tap Delete App. Your iPad will ask you to confirm — tap Delete. Don't worry: if you change your mind, you can always re-download a deleted app from the App Store for free — as long as it was a free app or one you paid for using your Apple ID."
[VISUAL CUE]: App held, wiggle mode, X tapped, confirmation prompt, Delete tapped. App disappears from Home screen.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Hold app → X or minus → Delete App — you can always re-download

**SEGMENT 4: Controlling In-App Purchases and Subscriptions**
[VOICEOVER]: "In-app purchases and subscriptions can sometimes catch people by surprise. To see and manage all your active subscriptions, go to Settings, tap your name at the top, then Subscriptions. Here you'll see every active subscription and what it's charging you. Tap any subscription to cancel it or see when it renews. To prevent accidental purchases, go to Settings, then Screen Time, then Content & Privacy Restrictions, and turn on a Purchase restriction requiring your password for in-app purchases."
[VISUAL CUE]: Settings → name → Subscriptions list shown. Then Settings → Screen Time → purchase restriction path.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Settings → your name → Subscriptions → see and cancel any active charges

**SEGMENT 5: Offloading Apps to Save Space**
[VOICEOVER]: "There's a useful middle ground between keeping an app and deleting it permanently — it's called Offloading. When you offload an app, your iPad removes the app itself to free up space, but keeps all your data and settings for it. If you re-install it later, everything is right where you left it. To offload apps automatically, go to Settings, then App Store, and turn on 'Offload Unused Apps.' Your iPad will do it quietly in the background when storage is running low."
[VISUAL CUE]: Settings → App Store → Offload Unused Apps toggle. Then Settings → General → iPad Storage → individual app offload option.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Offload = removes app but keeps your data — perfect for rarely-used apps

---

### COMMON MISTAKES (1 minute)
[VOICEOVER]: "Two things to watch for. First: signing up for a free trial and forgetting to cancel before it converts to a paid subscription. Apps are required to remind you before a trial ends, but it's easy to miss. Whenever you start a free trial, set a reminder in your calendar for two days before it expires. Second: thinking deleted apps are gone forever. Any app you've previously downloaded can be reinstalled from the App Store for free — just search and tap Get, and it will remember that you already purchased it."
[VISUAL CUE]: Calendar reminder being set. Then App Store showing a previously purchased app with a cloud download icon instead of Get.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Start a free trial? Set a calendar reminder before it converts to paid

---

### RECAP (30 seconds)
[VOICEOVER]: "Today's key points. One: turn on automatic app updates in Settings > App Store for security. Two: delete unused apps by holding the icon and tapping Delete. Three: check your subscriptions in Settings > your name > Subscriptions. Four: offloading saves space while keeping your data. You've now got full control of your apps."
[VISUAL CUE]: Four-point recap list.
[ON-SCREEN TEXT]: Update automatically · Delete unused · Check subscriptions · Offload to save space
