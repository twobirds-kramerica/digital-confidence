Medication and Drug Discount Scams

⚠️ Medications purchased from unlicensed online pharmacies may be counterfeit, contain dangerous substances, or have no active ingredient at all. This is a health risk, not just a financial one.

Why medication scams target seniors

Prescription medication costs are a significant concern for many older Canadians, particularly for those not fully covered by the Ontario Drug Benefit (ODB) program or private insurance. Scammers exploit this by advertising medications at dramatically lower prices — sometimes 70–90% below regular pharmacy rates. The catch: these medications are often counterfeit, improperly stored, or simply never shipped.

Types of medication scams

1. Fake online pharmacy websites

These websites look professional and legitimate but are not licensed to operate in Canada. They may use names that sound like real pharmacies and have fake customer reviews. Signs of a fake online pharmacy include: no prescription required, prices that seem impossibly low, no verifiable Canadian address, no licensed pharmacist to speak with, contact only by email or online form.

2. Unsolicited calls about drug discount programmes

A caller claims you qualify for a new government or insurance-sponsored drug discount programme. To enrol, they ask for your OHIP number, date of birth, and sometimes credit card information to "process your application." There is no such programme — your information is being collected for identity theft.

3. The Medicare/OHIP card renewal scam

Callers claim your provincial health card has expired and that you must pay a fee or update your information online to continue receiving health services. In Ontario, OHIP cards are renewed by mail by the government — ServiceOntario will never ask you to update your card details by phone or online payment. If you receive this call, hang up immediately.

The 3-Second Rule for medication offers

Before you click or call: Does my real pharmacy offer this? If not — why would a stranger online sell it cheaper without a prescription? The answer is they would not. Stop and call your real pharmacist first.

How to safely reduce medication costs in Ontario

  • Ontario Drug Benefit (ODB): If you are 65 or older, you may qualify for subsidised prescription costs. Ask your doctor or pharmacist.
  • Trillium Drug Program: For those with high drug costs relative to household income. Apply at ontario.ca.
  • Ask your pharmacist about generic alternatives: Generic drugs contain the same active ingredients as brand-name medications at a fraction of the cost.
  • Manufacturer discount programmes: Many pharmaceutical companies offer patient assistance programmes for those who cannot afford their medications. Ask your doctor.

If you think you may have received counterfeit medication

Stop taking the medication immediately. Contact your doctor or a pharmacist. Do not throw the medication away — it may be needed as evidence. Report to Health Canada at 1-866-225-0709 or hc-sc.gc.ca. Report the scam to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre at 1-888-495-8501.
