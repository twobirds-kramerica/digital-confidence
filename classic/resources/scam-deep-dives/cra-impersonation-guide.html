CRA Impersonation: The Complete Canadian Guide

⚠️ The CRA will NEVER demand payment by gift card, Bitcoin, or wire transfer — and will NEVER threaten immediate arrest. These are always scam tactics.

Why CRA impersonation is Canada's most common phone scam

The Canada Revenue Agency (CRA) is one of the most feared names in Canada — and scammers know it. Every year, thousands of Canadians lose money to criminals who call pretending to be CRA agents. According to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre (CAFC), CRA impersonation scams cause millions of dollars in losses annually, with seniors over 60 being the most targeted group.

The scam works because it triggers fear. Most people want to comply with tax authorities. Scammers exploit this by creating artificial urgency — claiming you have unpaid taxes, that a warrant has been issued for your arrest, or that your SIN number has been compromised. None of these scenarios require immediate action, but the scammer will not let you think clearly enough to realise that.

What a CRA impersonation call sounds like

The caller typically says something like:

"This is Officer [Name] calling from the Canada Revenue Agency. We have been trying to reach you about an outstanding tax liability. There is a warrant for your arrest. If you do not call us back immediately at [number], the police will be dispatched to your home."

The script varies but the core elements are always the same: urgency, fear of authority, and a demand for immediate payment or action.

What the real CRA actually does (and does not do)

Real CRA CRA Scammer
Sends a letter before calling Calls without prior notice
Gives you time to call back Demands immediate action
Accepts cheque or online payment Demands gift cards, Bitcoin, wire
Provides your full name and account info May only have partial information
Will not threaten arrest over taxes Threatens police, arrest, deportation
Encourages you to verify the call Tells you not to tell family or bank

The 3-Second Rule for CRA calls

Before you do anything the caller asks, take 3 seconds and ask: Would the real CRA arrest me without sending a letter first? The answer is no. You now have all the information you need. Hang up.

What to do right now if you receive this call

  1. Hang up. Do not press 1, do not say "yes," do not ask questions.
  2. If you are concerned you may have a real tax issue, call CRA directly at 1-800-959-8281 — this is the number on their website, not the one the caller gave you.
  3. Report the call to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre at 1-888-495-8501 or at antifraudcentre.ca.
  4. If you already sent money: call your bank immediately and explain you were scammed. Ask about a chargeback or wire recall.
  5. Tell a family member or trusted person what happened — there is no shame in having been targeted. Scammers are skilled professionals.

A real story from Ontario

Margaret, 72, from St. Thomas, received a call from someone claiming to be from CRA. The caller had a professional tone and told her she owed $4,200 in back taxes and that police were on their way unless she paid immediately with iTunes gift cards. Feeling panicked, she drove to Walmart to buy the cards. A store employee noticed she was buying several gift cards and asked if she was being asked to pay a bill with gift cards. Margaret said yes — and the employee told her it was a scam. She did not lose a cent.

The lesson: Any payment method that feels unusual for paying taxes IS unusual. Real tax payments go through your bank, My CRA Account online, or a cheque mailed to CRA. Not gift cards. Ever.
