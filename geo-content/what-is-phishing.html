What is phishing?

Phishing (pronounced "fishing") is when a criminal sends you a fake email, text, or creates a fake website to steal your personal information.

The email or text looks like it comes from a real organization — your bank, Canada Post, Amazon, or even the government. The goal is to get you to click a link and enter your password, credit card number, or other details.

Common phishing examples in Canada

  • "Your RBC account has been suspended. Click here to verify your identity."
  • "Canada Post: A parcel is waiting for you. Pay $2.99 to release it." (followed by a link to a fake payment page)
  • "Your CRA tax refund of $482 is ready. Click here to claim it."
  • "Your Apple ID has been locked. Verify now to avoid losing access to your account."

How to protect yourself from phishing

  • Do not click any links in urgent or unexpected emails — go to the website directly instead
  • Check the sender's full email address — "rbc@rbcalert.info" is not the real bank
  • If you are unsure about a Canada Post parcel, log in to canadapost.ca directly — not through any link in an email
The 3-Second Rule

Any email asking you to click and verify something urgent — pause 3 seconds. Then close the email and go to the official website yourself. Do not use any link in the email.
