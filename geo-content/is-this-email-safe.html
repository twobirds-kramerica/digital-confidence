Is this email safe to open?

Opening an email is usually safe — the danger comes from clicking links or downloading attachments. Use these four steps before you click anything:

  • Check the sender's email address. Tap or hover the sender's name to see the full address. "Royal Bank" is fine; "royalbank@gmail.com" or "rbcservice@rbcc.info" is a fake.
  • Look for spelling mistakes. Scam emails often contain unusual spelling or grammar, or say things your bank would never say.
  • Do not click links. Instead, open a new tab and type the website address yourself. Go directly to your bank or the company — not through any link in the email.
  • When in doubt, delete it. If you are not sure whether an email is real, it is always safe to delete it and contact the organization another way.
The 3-Second Rule

Before clicking any link in an email — pause 3 seconds. Ask: "Did I expect this email? Do I recognise the exact email address?" If not, close the email.

Emails your real bank or government will never send

  • "Your account will be closed unless you verify your information right now"
  • "We need your password or PIN to process your claim"
  • "You have won a prize — click here to claim it"
