Module 2.5: Everyday Digital Tasks

What you will learn: How to open and send email attachments, find files you have downloaded, print from any device, organise your files so you can find them again, and understand the most common file types. These are the tasks you will use every single day.

⏱ About 25–35 minutes — go at your own pace

✅ You are in a safe place.
Nothing on this page can harm your device. You cannot break anything by reading. If anything feels overwhelming, take a break and come back — your progress is saved.

🔊 Listen to This Page

iPhone / iPad: Touch and hold any text, then tap Speak.

Windows: Press Windows + Ctrl + Enter to start Narrator.

Mac: System Settings → Accessibility → Spoken Content → turn on Speak Selection.

Margaret's Story

Margaret, 74, lives in St. Thomas. Her granddaughter sent her a PDF copy of a family recipe by email — a wonderful treasure. Margaret opened the email, tapped the attachment, and the file appeared on her screen. Then she closed the email. A week later, she wanted to find that recipe again. She searched her email, couldn't remember what the subject line was, and spent 45 minutes feeling frustrated.

The recipe was actually sitting safely in her Downloads folder the whole time. She just didn't know where to look.

This module is about solving exactly that problem. Once you know where things go and how to find them, your device stops feeling like a mystery and starts feeling like a trusted assistant.

Part 1: Opening Email Attachments

An attachment is a file someone has added to an email — like a photo, a document, or a PDF. You can tell an email has an attachment by looking for the 📎 paperclip icon next to the subject line.

What is a PDF?

A PDF is like a digital photocopy — you can read it but you cannot easily change it. It looks the same on every device. Bills, government letters, and recipes are often sent as PDFs.

How to Open an Attachment

iPad / iPhone — Opening an Attachment

  1. Open the Mail app and tap the email with the attachment.
  2. Scroll down until you see the attached file (it will look like a white box with an icon).
  3. Tap the attachment once. It opens on your screen.
  4. To save it: tap the Share icon (square with arrow pointing up) → tap Save to Files → choose a folder → tap Save.

Windows — Opening an Attachment (Gmail or Outlook in browser)

  1. Click the email with the attachment.
  2. You will see the attachment listed at the bottom of the email (paperclip icon).
  3. Click Download (or the attachment name). The file saves to your Downloads folder.
  4. A pop-up may appear at the bottom of your screen — click Open to view it immediately, or Open folder to see where it saved.

Mac — Opening an Attachment

  1. Click the email in Mail (or in your browser).
  2. Find the attachment at the bottom of the email.
  3. Double-click the attachment to open it, or right-click → Save As to choose where it saves.
  4. By default, attachments save to your Downloads folder in Finder.
Safety Check Before Opening

Before you open any attachment, ask yourself: Was I expecting this file? and Do I know this person? If the answer to either is "no," use the 3-Second Rule from Module 2 — Stop, Breathe, Verify. Scammers sometimes hide malicious files in attachments.

Part 2: Sending a File or Photo by Email

Sending an attachment is like putting a letter inside an envelope — the file travels along with your email. Here is how to do it on each type of device.

iPad / iPhone — Attaching a Photo or File

  1. Open the Mail app and tap Compose (pencil icon, bottom right).
  2. Type your recipient's email address and subject line.
  3. Tap inside the email body area where you write your message.
  4. Tap and hold your finger in the body area for 2 seconds until a menu appears.
  5. Tap Add Document to attach a file, or Insert Photo or Video to attach a photo.
  6. Browse to your file or photo, tap it to select it, then tap Use or Add.
  7. Tap Send (the blue upward arrow).

Gmail (in your browser) — Attaching a File

  1. Click Compose (bottom left, with a pencil icon).
  2. Fill in the recipient and subject.
  3. At the bottom of the compose window, click the paperclip icon (📎).
  4. A file browser opens. Navigate to your file and click it to select it.
  5. Click Open. The file attaches to your email.
  6. Click Send.

Outlook — Attaching a File

  1. Click New Mail or New Email.
  2. Fill in the recipient and subject.
  3. Click Attach File (or the paperclip icon in the toolbar).
  4. Choose Browse This PC (Windows) or browse to your file.
  5. Select your file and click Insert.
  6. Click Send.
File Too Large?

Most email services have a limit of about 25 MB per attachment. If your file is too large, you will get an error message. A simple solution: ask a family member to help you share the file using Google Drive or iCloud instead — these services handle large files easily.

Part 3: Finding Your Downloads Folder

Every time you download something from the internet or save an attachment, it usually goes to one special place: the Downloads folder. Think of it as the "inbox tray" on a real desk — things land there first, and it is up to you to sort them out.

Finding Downloads on Windows

  1. Click the yellow folder icon in your taskbar (the bar at the bottom of your screen). This opens File Explorer.
  2. In the left panel, look for Downloads — it is usually listed under "Quick access" near the top.
  3. Click Downloads. You will see all the files you have downloaded.
  4. To sort by date (newest first), click the Date modified column heading at the top of the list.

Finding Downloads on Mac

  1. Click the Finder icon (the smiley-face icon in your Dock at the bottom).
  2. In the left panel of Finder, click Downloads.
  3. Your downloaded files appear on the right side.
  4. You can also click the stack of papers icon in your Dock — this is a shortcut directly to your Downloads folder.

Finding Downloads on iPad / iPhone

  1. Open the Files app (it looks like a blue folder).
  2. Tap Browse at the bottom of the screen.
  3. Tap On My iPhone or On My iPad.
  4. Look for a folder called Downloads. Tap it to see your downloaded files.
💡

Remember: If you can't find a file, it is almost certainly in your Downloads folder. That is always the first place to look.

Part 4: Organising Your Files

Your Downloads folder fills up quickly — that is perfectly normal. The solution is to move files into organised folders, just like sorting papers into labelled binders on a shelf.

Creating a New Folder (Windows)

Step-by-Step: Create a Folder in Windows

  1. Open File Explorer and go to Documents (in the left panel).
  2. Right-click in an empty space (not on a file) in the right panel.
  3. A menu appears — click New, then click Folder.
  4. A new folder appears with the name highlighted in blue. Type your folder name (e.g. "Tax Documents 2026") and press Enter.
  5. To move files into your new folder, click a file once to select it, then drag it into the folder — or right-click → Cut, then open the folder and right-click → Paste.

Creating a New Folder (Mac)

Step-by-Step: Create a Folder on Mac

  1. Open Finder and go to Documents.
  2. Press Command + Shift + N — or click File → New Folder from the menu bar at the top.
  3. A new folder appears, ready for you to type a name. Type your name and press Enter.
  4. Drag files into the new folder, or right-click a file → Move To → select your folder.

Creating a New Folder (iPad)

Step-by-Step: Create a Folder in Files (iPad)

  1. Open the Files app and go to the location where you want the folder (e.g. On My iPad).
  2. Tap and hold on an empty area. A menu appears.
  3. Tap New Folder.
  4. Type your folder name and tap Done.
Ontario Senior Filing Tip

A simple folder system that works well:

  • Documents → Government Documents — CRA letters, Service Ontario, OHIP, OAS
  • Documents → Bills & Receipts — Hydro, Bell, property tax
  • Documents → Medical — prescriptions, test results, appointment letters
  • Pictures → Family Photos — organised by year or event
  • Downloads — Clean out monthly; move keepers to proper folders

Part 5: Printing a Document or Photo

Printing is one of those things that seems simple but has a few steps to learn. Once you have done it a few times, it becomes second nature.

Printing on Windows

  1. Open the file you want to print (double-click it to open).
  2. Press Ctrl + P on your keyboard — or click File → Print from the menu.
  3. A print window opens. Choose your printer from the dropdown at the top.
  4. Choose how many copies you need.
  5. Click the blue Print button.

Printing on Mac

  1. Open the file you want to print.
  2. Press Command + P — or click File → Print from the menu bar.
  3. Choose your printer from the dropdown.
  4. Adjust settings as needed (copies, colour vs black & white).
  5. Click Print.

Printing on iPad / iPhone (AirPrint)

  1. Open the document or photo you want to print.
  2. Tap the Share icon (square with an arrow pointing up).
  3. Scroll through the options and tap Print.
  4. Tap Select Printer and choose your printer from the list. (Your printer must be on the same Wi-Fi network as your iPad.)
  5. Choose the number of copies, then tap Print.

Printing Troubleshooting

  • "Printer not found" — Make sure your printer is turned on, and that it is connected to the same Wi-Fi network as your device. Try turning the printer off and back on.
  • "Print comes out too small" — Look for a "Scale" or "Fit to page" setting in the print window. Set it to 100% or "Actual Size."
  • "Everything prints in black and white" — Check the print settings for a "Colour" option. Make sure your colour ink cartridges are not empty.
  • "Print job is stuck" — On Windows, search for "Printers & Scanners" in the Start menu, find your printer, and click "Open queue" to cancel stuck jobs.

Part 6: Saving a File Where You Can Find It Again

Many people just click "Save" without thinking about where the file goes. A few seconds of planning now saves many minutes of searching later.

The Difference Between "Save" and "Save As"

  • Save (Ctrl + S on Windows, Command + S on Mac): Saves the file in the same place it already is. Good for updating a file you are already working on.
  • Save As (Ctrl + Shift + S on Windows, Command + Shift + S on Mac): Lets you choose a new name AND a new location. Use this when you are saving something for the first time.

How to Use "Save As" to Choose Your Location

  1. In whatever programme you are using, click File in the top menu bar.
  2. Click Save As.
  3. A window opens showing your folders. Browse to where you want to save the file (e.g. Documents → Bills & Receipts).
  4. In the File name field, type a clear name — for example: Hydro Bill March 2026.
  5. Click Save.
File Naming Tips

Good file names tell you what it is, when it is from, and (if needed) who it is for. Examples:

  • ✅ Dentist Invoice - April 2026.pdf
  • ✅ Christmas 2025 - Family Photo.jpg
  • ✅ CRA Notice of Assessment 2025.pdf
  • ❌ document1.pdf — too vague, impossible to find later
  • ❌ scan0047.jpg — meaningless name, easy to lose

Part 7: Finding a File You Cannot Remember Where You Saved

Do not worry — every computer has a powerful search tool that can find your file in seconds, even if you forgot where you saved it.

Searching for Files on Windows

  1. Click the Start button (Windows logo, bottom left) — or press the Windows key on your keyboard.
  2. Start typing the name of the file (or part of it — even just a word from the name).
  3. Results appear instantly as you type. Look for your file under "Documents" or "Best match."
  4. Click the file to open it.

Searching for Files on Mac (Spotlight)

  1. Press Command + Space. A search bar appears in the centre of your screen.
  2. Start typing the file name. Results appear as you type.
  3. Find your file in the results and press Enter to open it — or click it with your mouse.

Searching for Files on iPad

  1. Open the Files app.
  2. Tap the Search icon (magnifying glass) at the top.
  3. Type the file name or a keyword from the name.
  4. Tap the file in the results to open it.

Part 8: Understanding Common File Types

Every file has a "type" — you can see it in the three or four letters at the end of the file name (after the dot). Here are the ones you will encounter most often:

📄

PDF

Bills, government letters, forms. Looks the same on every device. You cannot easily edit it — but you can read and print it.

📝

DOCX

A Microsoft Word document. You can open and edit these. Good for letters and notes.

🖼️

JPG / PNG

Photo files. JPG is the most common for photos. PNG is used for images with sharp lines (like logos).

📦

ZIP

A compressed folder containing multiple files bundled together. You need to "unzip" it first before you can see the files inside.

📊

XLSX

A Microsoft Excel spreadsheet. Used for budgets and lists with rows and columns.

🎵

MP3 / MP4

Audio (MP3) and video (MP4) files. Your device can play these without any extra apps.

How to Open a ZIP File

Opening a ZIP on Windows

  1. Find the ZIP file in your Downloads folder.
  2. Right-click the ZIP file.
  3. Click Extract All.
  4. Choose a destination folder (or leave it as the default), then click Extract.
  5. A new folder appears containing all the files. Open that folder to see them.

Opening a ZIP on Mac

  1. Find the ZIP file in your Downloads folder.
  2. Double-click the ZIP file. Mac extracts it automatically.
  3. A new folder appears in the same location as the ZIP. Open it to see the files inside.

Part 9: Practice Scenarios

The best way to learn is by doing. Try these short exercises at your own pace.

📸 Scenario 1: Your Friend Sends You Photos

Your task: Your friend emails you three photos from a birthday party. Download the photos, move them to your Pictures folder, then print one photo.

  1. Open the email and download the photo attachment.
  2. Go to your Downloads folder and find the photo.
  3. Move it to your Pictures folder (drag and drop, or right-click → Move To).
  4. Open the photo and print it (File → Print, or Ctrl + P).

✅ Success: The photo prints clearly, and the other photos are saved in your Pictures folder.

🧾 Scenario 2: Download and Save a Bill

Your task: Your Hydro bill arrives by email as a PDF. Download it and save it to a "Bills 2026" folder so you can find it at tax time.

  1. Open the email and download the PDF attachment.
  2. Create a new folder in Documents called "Bills 2026" (if you have not already).
  3. Move the PDF from Downloads into your Bills 2026 folder.
  4. Rename the file clearly: "Hydro Bill March 2026.pdf"

✅ Success: The bill is in the correct folder with a clear name, easy to find later.

✉️ Scenario 3: Email a Photo to a Family Member

Your task: Take a photo of something meaningful — your garden, your pet, a meal — and email it to a family member.

  1. Find the photo in your Pictures folder (or take a new one on your phone or iPad).
  2. Open your email and compose a new message.
  3. Attach the photo using the paperclip icon or tap-and-hold method.
  4. Write a short note and press Send.

✅ Success: Your family member receives the email with your photo attached and can see it clearly.
