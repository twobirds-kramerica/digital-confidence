**Target:** Member services manager, community development officer, or branch manager
**Goal:** Partnership conversation — DCC as a member benefit for senior members
**Cadence:** 5 emails over 21 days

---

## Email 2 — Day 4: The Online Banking Safety Angle
**Subject:** 1 in 3 seniors has clicked a suspicious link — here's what protects them
**Preview text:** Your members are being targeted. This is the practical response.

---

Hi [First Name],

Following up on my note earlier this week.

According to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre, seniors are the most targeted age group for financial fraud in Canada — and digital fraud is now the primary delivery method. Phishing emails, fake banking pages, urgent text messages, and phone spoofing all exploit the same gap: seniors who haven't yet built confident digital habits.

**Module 6: Banking & Transactions** in the Digital Confidence Centre addresses this directly. It covers:
- How to recognise a fake banking website vs. the real one
- What to do before entering any financial information online
- How to set up two-factor authentication on banking apps
- The signs of account takeover fraud and how to respond

This module was written specifically for Canadian seniors using Canadian financial institutions. It's free, works on any device, and takes about 25 minutes to complete.

[DCC URL — direct link to Module 6]

If your staff have ever spent 20 minutes on the phone reassuring a member they've been scammed — this is the resource that gets them to that call less often.

Aaron

---
**Personalisation notes:** Check CAFC or OPP fraud alerts for current local scam statistics to personalise the opener.

## Email 3 — Day 8: The Member Trust Angle
**Subject:** What happens when your senior members feel safe online
**Preview text:** Confidence with technology keeps your most loyal members engaged.

---

Hi [First Name],

One more thought.

Senior members are often the most loyal members a credit union has. They've been with you for decades. They believe in the co-operative model. But there's a growing risk: as banking moves online, seniors who lack digital confidence start to disengage — from online banking, from mobile apps, from the digital services you've invested in building.

It's not reluctance. It's fear of doing something wrong.

The Digital Confidence Centre was built to address exactly that. Every module starts from zero, assumes no prior digital knowledge, and builds confidence through plain-language instruction and practical exercises. Module 6 on banking safety, Module 2 on scam protection, and Module 3 on passwords are the three most directly relevant to financial services members.

When your members trust themselves online, they use your digital services more. That's better for them, and better for [Credit Union Name].

[DCC URL]

Aaron
