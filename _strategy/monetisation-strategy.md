# DCC Monetisation Strategy
*Internal Strategy Document — Two Birds Innovation*
*Date: March 2026*

---

## Executive Summary

The Digital Confidence Centre has a clear path to sustainable revenue without venture capital, without pivoting its mission, and without compromising the trust it is building with Ontario's senior population. The model is a three-tier ramp: immediate B2C revenue from coaching sessions and speaking engagements funds operations in the first quarter; B2B institutional licensing and sponsored content provide a step change in revenue in months 3–6; and grant income, premium features, and curriculum licensing create durability and scale from month 6 onward. Conservative Year 1 total revenue is projected at approximately $116,000; base case is $155,000; optimistic scenario assuming a major B2B deal closes early is $220,000+. The business is buildable by a single operator in Year 1 with part-time contractor support, without requiring full-time hires until revenue justifies it.

## Tier 1 — Immediate Revenue (Months 0–3)

### B2C Bootcamp Sessions

**Pricing:**
- 1:1 coaching: $75/hour (base rate); $95/hour for specialised topics (fraud prevention, banking setup, telehealth)
- Group sessions: $35/person for groups of 4–8 participants (minimum 4 to proceed)
- Half-day workshop (3 hours, up to 8 participants): $250 flat (approx. $31/person)

**Target volume — realistic monthly projections:**
- Month 1: 3 sessions (mix of 1:1 and small groups) — revenue ~$350
- Month 2: 6 sessions — revenue ~$750
- Month 3: 9–10 sessions — revenue ~$1,100
- By Month 6: 15–18 sessions/month — revenue ~$2,000/month

The ramp is slow deliberately. The first 90 days are about proof of concept, testimonial capture, and word-of-mouth seeding — not volume. One genuinely excellent session with a senior who then tells four friends is worth more than ten mediocre sessions.

**Revenue projection:**
| Scenario | Monthly (Month 3) | Monthly (Month 6) | Year 1 Total |
|----------|--------------------|-------------------|--------------|
| Conservative | $1,100 | $1,800 | $14,000 |
| Base | $1,500 | $2,500 | $20,000 |
| Optimistic | $2,000 | $3,500 | $28,000 |

**Sales channels:**
- Library bulletin boards and program calendars (St. Thomas, Elgin County, London branches)
- Facebook groups for seniors in Elgin County and surrounding regions (Elgin Seniors, St. Thomas Community Buy and Sell, etc.)
- Physician office noticeboards (especially family health teams — ask reception to display a card)
- Church bulletins and parish newsletters — remarkably effective in small-city Ontario
- Word of mouth from the first 5 participants — incentivise referrals with a $10 credit on next session
- Adult children of seniors on LinkedIn and local parent Facebook groups

**Script for first call / first session:**

*First call (5–10 minutes):*

"Thank you for calling — I'm so glad you reached out. Can I ask, what's been the most frustrating thing about your [iPhone/iPad] lately? ... That's really common, and you're definitely not alone. What I do is work with people one-on-one, at your own pace, to build your confidence step by step — there's no rushing, no jargon, no judgment. We'd start with exactly the things that are bothering you most. A one-hour session is $75, and most people find that one or two sessions makes a big difference. Would you like to book a time?"

*First session opening (in-person or video):*

"Before we do anything, I just want you to know — there's no such thing as a silly question here. Everything we cover today, we'll go at your pace. If I use a word you don't recognise, just stop me. My job is to make this make sense for you, not to show you how fast I can do things. What would you most like to feel more confident about by the end of today?"

### Referral Partnerships

**Connected Canadians affiliate/referral model:**
Connected Canadians has a volunteer-pairing model that cannot deliver structured curriculum. Their participants who want to go deeper — to actually progress through a learning path — are natural DCC referrals. Propose the following terms to Connected Canadians:

- DCC refers participants who want peer support after completing modules to Connected Canadians at no cost
- Connected Canadians refers participants who want structured curriculum instruction to DCC bootcamp sessions
- No financial exchange initially — this is a mutual referral agreement with a 6-month review
- If volume justifies formalising: DCC pays Connected Canadians $15 per converted referral; Connected Canadians receives no fee from DCC for referrals to DCC (DCC provides the value)

**Cyber-Seniors content referral:**
Cyber-Seniors has a YouTube video library that DCC can reference and link to in its resources section. In exchange, propose that Cyber-Seniors link to DCC's Ontario program page from their website and acknowledge DCC as a recommended provider for Ontario seniors wanting structured instruction. This costs nothing and provides mutual SEO and referral value.

**Expected referral volume:**
- Months 1–3: 0–3 converted referrals/month (relationship building phase)
- Months 4–6: 5–8 converted referrals/month
- Months 7–12: 10–15 converted referrals/month
- Revenue impact: at $75/session, 12 referral-converted sessions/month = $900/month in incremental B2C revenue by Month 9

## Tier 2 — Near-Term Revenue (Months 3–6)

### B2B White-Label Licensing

**Pricing model:**

The institutional licence covers: access to DCC's full online platform for an agreed number of users, the right to present DCC curriculum under a co-branded banner, one annual trainer orientation session, printed quick-reference materials for participants, and quarterly program support check-ins with Aaron.

- **Starter (single branch / small organisation, up to 200 users):** $12,000/year
- **Standard (multi-branch / mid-size organisation, up to 500 users):** $20,000/year
- **Enterprise (healthcare system, large library network, financial institution, up to 2,000 users):** $30,000/year
- **Custom pricing** for government agencies and national organisations — quote on request

**What's included in the licence:**
- Full online DCC platform access (all 12 modules + resources + print centre)
- Co-branded landing page hosted on DCC infrastructure
- Two half-day instructor orientation sessions per year (virtual or in-person within 100km)
- Printed quick-reference card set (100 cards for Starter, 300 for Standard, 1,000 for Enterprise)
- Quarterly impact reporting (module completion rates, participant feedback summaries)
- Email support for program coordinator (48-hour response time)
- Priority access to new modules and curriculum updates

**Sales approach:**
Institutional B2B sales in the seniors sector are relationship-driven and move slowly. The winning approach is: establish credibility in the community first (speaking, library events), get one champion inside the target organisation, offer a no-risk pilot (3-month paid pilot at 50% of annual licence fee, credited against full licence if they renew), and deliver exceptional results so the renewal is automatic.

Do not cold-email institutions without a warm introduction. Use the library and community centre relationships built in Tier 1 to get warm introductions to purchasing decision-makers.

### Sponsored Content / Featured Partner Slots

**Concept:**
DCC's Recommended Tools and Resources page (and a dedicated "Trusted Partners" section to be created) features carefully selected products and services that DCC genuinely recommends for seniors. Organisations pay for featured placement. DCC maintains editorial control and does not accept sponsors whose products it cannot endorse in good conscience.

**Pricing:**
- Featured Partner listing (logo, 100-word description, link): $500/month or $5,000/year
- Sponsored article / explainer (e.g., "How to use [Bank]'s mobile app safely" — branded as partner content): $750 per article (one per month per partner maximum)
- Sponsored email mention to DCC newsletter subscribers: $300 per send (only applicable once newsletter list exceeds 1,000 subscribers — target Month 6)

**Categories to prioritise:**
1. Telecommunications (Rogers, Bell, Telus, Koodo, Freedom — senior plan promotions)
2. Canadian banking (TD, RBC, Scotiabank senior banking programs)
3. Insurance (Sun Life, Manulife digital tools for policy management)
4. Health technology (telehealth platforms, remote monitoring device makers)
5. Home security / emergency response (Lifeline, medical alert services — high relevance to target audience)

**Ad policy to protect user trust:**
DCC will not accept sponsorship from organisations that have been subject to CRTC enforcement for misleading senior-directed marketing in the past 3 years. DCC will not accept sponsorship that requires endorsing specific financial products (investments, insurance policies) — only tools and services. All sponsored content must be labelled "Partner Content" in plain language. DCC reserves the right to terminate any sponsorship agreement with 30 days notice if the partner's products or practices are found to be harmful to seniors. Trust is DCC's primary asset; it cannot be sold.

### Grant Revenue

**New Horizons for Seniors Program (NHSP)**

*Eligibility:* Projects that enable seniors to share their knowledge and experiences with others, support social participation and inclusion, and assist seniors who are at risk of abuse. Digital literacy programs for isolated or vulnerable seniors qualify strongly.

*Typical amounts:* Community-based projects: $25,000–$100,000. Pan-Canadian projects: up to $150,000. First application should target the community-based stream.

*Timeline:* Applications typically open once or twice per year (spring and fall calls). From open call to funding notification: approximately 4–6 months. From notification to first payment: 2–3 months. Plan for a 9–12 month timeline from application to cash in hand.

*Recommendation:* Submit a NHSP application in the first spring call after DCC has 3+ months of program delivery data. Real participant outcomes are the single strongest element of a NHSP application.

**Ontario Seniors Community Grant Program**

*Eligibility:* Ontario-based not-for-profit organisations delivering programs that support seniors' independence, social inclusion, and community participation. Note: DCC's current structure as a for-profit social enterprise may require the creation of a nonprofit delivery vehicle or a formal partnership with an eligible nonprofit (e.g., a library or community centre) to apply under their umbrella.

*Typical amounts:* $5,000–$50,000.

*Timeline:* Annual intake, typically fall. Allow 6–8 months from application to funding.

*Recommendation:* In Year 1, pursue this grant via a partnership application with St. Thomas Public Library or Elgin County Library as the lead applicant. DCC is named as the program provider. The library administers the grant; DCC delivers the program and receives program delivery fees.

**Digital Literacy Exchange Program (ISED / Innovation Canada)**

*Eligibility:* Canadian organisations delivering digital literacy training to underserved populations, including seniors, Indigenous peoples, and newcomers. For-profit organisations can apply. Ontario projects are strongly competitive.

*Typical amounts:* $100,000–$500,000 for larger projects; smaller project stream available at $25,000–$100,000.

*Timeline:* Competitive call, annual. Long application process with detailed budget justification required. Allow 12–18 months from initial preparation to funding.

*Recommendation:* This is a Year 2 target unless DCC has a partner with prior ISED application experience. Priority in Year 1 is NHSP and the Ontario Seniors Community Grant.

| Ontario Seniors Community Grant (via library partner) | Month 3 | Month 9 | $20,000 |
| NHSP Community Stream | Month 4 | Month 11 | $25,000 |

Note: These amounts are conservative and achievable. Well-documented program delivery, participant testimonials, and a clear theory of change are the differentiating factors in competitive grant applications — DCC should build these assets from day one.

## Tier 3 — Medium-Term Revenue (Months 6–12)

### Premium User Tier

**Justification:**
The free DCC platform builds the audience and the trust. A premium tier creates recurring revenue from the most engaged users and from adult children who want ongoing peace of mind for their parents.

**What features justify a paid tier:**

For seniors:
- Personalised learning path with progress tracking across all 12 modules
- "Ask a Question" access — submit a written question and receive a video response from Aaron within 48 hours (high perceived value, scalable with good tooling)
- Monthly live Q&A session (group Zoom call, 1 hour, Aaron answers top questions from premium members)
- Printable completion certificate for each module (valued by seniors for whom achievement recognition matters)
- Early access to new modules before public release

For adult children (the "Family Supporter" tier):
- Dashboard showing their parent's module progress
- "Teach My Parent" guides — step-by-step scripts for adult children to use at home
- Bi-annual "Is Mum OK Digitally?" check-in session (30 min video call with Aaron, reviewing the parent's digital safety setup)

**Suggested pricing:**
- Senior Premium: $4.99/month or $39/year (annually saves approx. $21 — leads to annual)
- Family Supporter: $7.99/month or $59/year

**Conversion rate assumptions:**
If DCC has 500 registered free users by Month 6 (conservative for a locally marketed program):
- 8–12% premium conversion = 40–60 premium subscribers
- At $39/year average: $1,560–$2,340/year
- By Month 12 with 1,500 free users and 10% conversion: 150 premium subscribers = ~$5,850/year

Premium tier revenue is not transformative in Year 1. It becomes meaningful in Year 2–3 as the free user base grows into the thousands. Build it in Month 6–8 so it is ready to scale.

### Data Insights Product

**What anonymised aggregate data DCC will have by Month 12:**

DCC will accumulate aggregated (non-personally identifiable) data on: which digital tasks Ontario seniors find most difficult (by module completion rates and question submissions), which scam types are most commonly encountered (from fraud prevention module engagement), geographic patterns in digital confidence levels by postal code region, and generational differences in learning pace and topic prioritisation.

This data has genuine value to several buyer categories:

- **Insurance companies:** Understanding digital behaviour patterns in the 65+ cohort is relevant to risk assessment for home, life, and health insurance products
- **Telecommunications companies:** Identifying which digital tasks drive the most anxiety informs customer support design and senior-targeted marketing
- **Government agencies:** Ontario and federal government digital service teams are designing citizen-facing digital tools; senior-specific usability data is directly actionable
- **Pharmaceutical companies:** As telehealth and digital health management grows, pharma has interest in adoption barriers for digital health tools in the senior cohort

**Privacy/ethics framework:**
DCC will not sell individual-level data under any circumstances. Insights products are exclusively aggregate, anonymised, and reported at the regional or cohort level (never individual). DCC will maintain a public "Data Ethics Commitment" statement on its website that explicitly describes what data is and is not sold. Any insights product offering will be disclosed in DCC's terms of use, and users may opt out of aggregate analytics at any time. This is not only ethically necessary — it is commercially necessary, because DCC's primary asset is senior trust, and any perception of data misuse would destroy the business.

**Pricing model:**
- Annual insights subscription (telecom or insurance sector): $15,000–$25,000/year for quarterly aggregate report
- Custom research report (government, academic): $5,000–$15,000 per report
- This revenue stream is Year 2+ once data volume is statistically meaningful

### Curriculum Licensing

**Educational institutions:**
College and university programs in gerontology, social work, nursing, and healthcare administration need current, quality digital literacy curriculum to train students who will work with seniors. DCC's curriculum can be licensed to these programs as a teaching resource. Pricing: $3,000–$8,000/year per institution for unlimited student access.

**Healthcare worker training:**
Personal support workers (PSWs), home care workers, and nursing home staff regularly help seniors with technology but receive no formal training in how to teach digital skills to anxious older adults. DCC's curriculum, with an added "how to teach this" layer, is a natural professional development product for the PSW sector. Ontario has approximately 100,000 PSWs. Even 1% engagement at $150/person per training completion would be significant. This is a Year 2–3 channel but worth beginning to design the product in Year 1.

**Francs-parler (French-language) version:**
Ontario has approximately 600,000 francophone residents, a significant portion of whom are seniors. No French-language senior digital literacy curriculum comparable to DCC's exists. A French translation of the DCC platform — even a partial one covering the 5 most critical modules — opens the ISED francophone funding stream, the Franco-Ontarian institutional buyer market, and a genuinely underserved community. This is a Year 2 investment with strong grant funding potential.

## Funding Reinvestment Priorities

When DCC reaches $50,000 in cumulative revenue (projected around Month 7–8 on the base case):

**Priority 1: Part-time marketing and admin support ($800–$1,200/month).**
The single greatest constraint on scaling from $5,000/month to $15,000/month is time. A part-time contractor handling social media, email follow-up, booking coordination, and grant report preparation frees Aaron to focus on delivery and business development.

**Priority 2: Platform improvements — accessibility audit and mobile optimisation ($3,000–$5,000 one-time).**
The DCC platform is functional. To command B2B licence fees of $20,000+, it needs to be demonstrably excellent. A professional accessibility audit and a round of mobile usability improvements is the single highest-ROI product investment at this stage.

**Priority 3: Professional photography and video content ($2,000–$3,000).**
Senior-facing programs live and die on trust, and trust in the Ontario market is built by seeing real people, real spaces, and real results. A half-day professional photography session capturing DCC sessions in action — with participant consent — provides marketing assets that will be used for years.

**Priority 4: Grant application writing support ($2,500–$4,000 for NHSP application).**
NHSP and ISED grant applications are competitive and time-consuming. Engaging an experienced grant writer for the first major application is a sensible investment — a successful $50,000 NHSP grant has a 12.5x return on a $4,000 writing fee.

**Priority 5: Legal — simple B2B licence agreement and terms of use ($1,500–$2,500).**
Before the first B2B licence closes, DCC needs a professionally drafted licence agreement. This protects both parties and signals that DCC is a credible commercial entity. Do not use a template from the internet for a $20,000 contract.

**Reserve:** Hold a minimum of $10,000 in operating reserve at all times from Month 4 onward. The senior services sector has payment cycles of 30–90 days for institutional invoices; cash flow management is critical.
