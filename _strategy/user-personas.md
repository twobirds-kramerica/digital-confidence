# User Personas — Digital Confidence Centre

Strategic audience analysis for product, content, and marketing decisions.

---

## Persona 1: The Fearful Avoider *(Primary — like Brenda)*

> **Key message needed:** "You can't break anything. You're safe here."

### Quick Reference

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Age | 72 |
| Location | Southwestern Ontario |
| Background | Retired teacher |
| Device | iPad (given by adult child) |
| Primary fear | Identity theft, banking scams |
| How they found DCC | Adult child set it up and walked her through Module 1 |
| Dropout risk | **HIGH** |
| Monetisation potential | Free user → premium candidate if trust is earned over 6+ months |

### Goals
- Feel safe enough to video call grandchildren
- Gain enough confidence to check email independently

### What Success Looks Like
- Completes 3 or more modules
- Feels confident checking email without calling a family member for help

### Notes
One bad experience ends engagement permanently. Every interaction — every word, every button — must reinforce safety and calm. Progress must feel achievable. Errors must be invisible or gently handled, never alarming.

## Persona 2: The Curious Adopter

> **Key message needed:** "There are no dumb questions. Everyone starts somewhere."

### Quick Reference

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Age | 68 |
| Location | Ontario |
| Background | Retired trades worker |
| Device | Smartphone — used for calls only |
| Motivation | Wants to share YouTube videos with grandchildren, try AI tools, video calling |
| How they found DCC | Flyer at the library or senior centre |
| Dropout risk | **MEDIUM** |
| Monetisation potential | Free user; HIGH word-of-mouth value |

### Goals
- Learn to use YouTube
- Understand what AI is
- Try video calling

### What Success Looks Like
- Confidently navigates two or three apps without assistance
- Shares the resource with a peer group or family member

### Notes
Embarrassment about "not knowing things" is the primary drop-off trigger. Tone must never condescend. Framing every new skill as *normal* and *learnable* keeps this persona engaged. High referral potential once they feel comfortable — they become informal ambassadors at senior centres and through word of mouth.

## Persona 3: The Adult Child *(Acquisition Channel)*

> **Key message:** "We'll take it from here."

### Quick Reference

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Age | 44 |
| Location | Anywhere in Canada |
| Background | Working professional, parent of young children |
| How they found DCC | Googled "how to teach my mom to use her iPad" |
| Primary goal | Set parent up with a trusted, self-sufficient resource so they stop calling for tech help |
| Dropout risk | **LOW** — motivated by a clear pain point |
| Monetisation potential | **Highest premium conversion probability**; advocate for institutional sales |

### Goals
- Find a resource they can hand off with confidence
- Reduce frequency of tech-support calls from their parent
- Know their parent is in safe, patient hands

### What Success Looks Like
- Parent uses the site independently after the initial setup
- Adult child shares the DCC link with friends, colleagues, and social media

### Notes
This persona shares the DCC link widely and is the most likely to pay for a premium tier (especially a Family tier if one becomes available). They are also a natural channel for introducing DCC to credit unions, libraries, and community organisations. The "Set Up for a Loved One" page directly serves this persona and should be treated as a high-priority landing page.

## Persona 4: The Institutional Buyer

> **Key message needed:** ROI evidence, grant funding assistance, implementation support.

### Quick Reference

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Age | 55 |
| Background | Programme manager at a regional library, credit union, or community health org |
| Evaluating DCC as | White-label solution for their member/client base |
| Decision process | Will request a demo; may require board approval |
| Monetisation potential | **$12,000–$25,000/year** contract (Tier 2 or Tier 3) |

### Decision Factors

| Factor | Priority |
|---|---|
| Credibility and evidence of outcomes | High |
| Bilingual support (English/French) | High |
| WCAG accessibility compliance | High |
| Customisable branding | High |
| Ontario-specific content | High |
| No data licensing risk | High |
| Implementation support | Medium |
| Grant funding assistance | Medium |

### What Success Looks Like
- A signed multi-year service agreement
- Positive member outcomes that the institution can report to funders or their board
- Re-signing or expanding scope in year two

### Notes
This persona does not use the product directly — they evaluate it on behalf of their institution. They need proof, not persuasion. Case studies, accessibility audits, and a clear pricing and implementation guide will move them through the funnel. Connection to Ontario provincial grants (e.g., Senior Community Grant, Digital Literacy programs) is a significant value-add in the sales conversation.

## Persona 5: The Independently Capable Senior

> **Key message needed:** "This is built for you — not dumbed down."

### Quick Reference

| Attribute | Detail |
|---|---|
| Age | 65 |
| Location | Anywhere in Ontario |
| Background | Recently retired professional; comfortable with email and basic social media |
| How they found DCC | LinkedIn post or referred by adult child |
| Dropout risk | **LOW** |
| Monetisation potential | Advocate and referrer; community group channel |

### Goals
- Use DCC as a reference for specific topics (scam awareness, banking security, AI)
- Recommend DCC to peers and community groups they belong to

### What Success Looks Like
- Browses selectively rather than completing every module in order
- Connects DCC to a seniors' organisation, book club, or community group
- Becomes an informal "tech mentor" within their social circle using DCC content

### Notes
This persona will leave if the content feels patronising or oversimplified. They value accuracy, Canadian context, and being treated as competent adults. They are not the primary audience, but they are a highly effective distribution channel — they have influence within seniors' networks and will refer the product upward (to Persona 4) or laterally (to Personas 1 and 2).

## Strategic Notes

- **Acquisition funnel is adult-child-led.** The majority of Persona 1 and 2 users will arrive because a family member (Persona 3) found the site and set it up for them. The "Set Up for a Loved One" page and any future shareable onboarding flow are therefore the highest-leverage acquisition assets on the site.

- **Trust is the only currency that matters for Personas 1 and 2.** Premium conversion for these users is a long-cycle process — 6 to 12 months of positive, low-pressure experience. Rushing monetisation messaging will destroy trust before it is built. A well-timed, non-pushy prompt at the point of module completion is the appropriate conversion moment.

- **Institutional sales (Persona 4) require a dedicated pitch track.** The current public site is not optimised for an institutional buyer. A dedicated landing page (or a private demo package) covering outcomes data, accessibility certification, white-label options, and Ontario grant eligibility would meaningfully increase Tier 2/3 conversion.

- **Persona 5 is underserved and underutilised.** A "Peer Facilitator Kit" — a downloadable package designed for Persona 5 to run informal sessions at community groups, libraries, or faith organisations — would activate this persona's referral potential at low cost and create a grassroots distribution network across Ontario.
